\begin{abstract}
After a load is restored or a line is reclosed, the network can keep
oscillating. A later intervention may then resemble the earlier event's
recovery. We study this ambiguity for successive load changes and line
openings observed by eight PMUs in an IEEE 39-bus simulation. A bank of
switched differential-algebraic predictors starts from estimated past
states and preserves physical and instrumental memory across restoration.
Each report supplies voltage, oriented terminal current, frequency and
ROCOF. The bank retains ten seconds of conditional state history, while
its frozen onset screen searches only the most recent 18 reports.
Conditional evidence integrates load severity and weights five fixed
network hypotheses. Separate active-event and originating-cause masses
allow a restored event to explain a continuing response.
In a sealed comparison, 18 six-event physical schedules are replayed under
three network conditions and two noise levels. The five-model procedure
makes \COneMixTimely{}/\COneMixTotal{} timely family/source decisions with
\COneMixExtras{} extra alarms; a frozen ExtraTrees reference makes
\COneETTimely{}/\COneETTotal{} with \COneETExtras{}. Under network changes
outside the model bank, the physical procedure produces 68 extra alarms.
The ExtraTrees reference is a causal successor to our earlier 30-s-window
hackathon submission, not that submission itself. The replays share physical
parents, and the methods have different alarm budgets. The measured gain is
timely load/source attribution on this simulator, accompanied by a larger
false-alarm cost; the experiment does not isolate recovery memory alone.
\end{abstract}
