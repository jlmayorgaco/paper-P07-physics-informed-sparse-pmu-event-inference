\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A restored electrical intervention can remain the cause of the observed
response. We formulated load/line attribution through physical histories
that start from estimated past states and retain that response through
restoration. Active and originating-cause weights separate the current
intervention from its continuing physical effect.

The independent simulated comparison gives
\COneMixTimely{}/\COneMixTotal{} timely family/source decisions and
\COneMixExtras{} extra alarms for the five-model bank, compared with
\COneETTimely{}/\COneETTotal{} and \COneETExtras{} for the fixed causal
ExtraTrees reference. The advantage is concentrated in loads; line
coverage is similar to the short-window learned comparator. The controlled
study identifies a strong state-carry effect, while marginal and profiled
severity scores tie. Together, these results support an executable approach that uses
electrical dynamics to attribute interventions from remote PMUs, preserves
the response across restoration, and separates timely coverage from alarm
burden. Physical-state continuity supplies the consistent reference needed
to evaluate subsequent interventions during recovery.
