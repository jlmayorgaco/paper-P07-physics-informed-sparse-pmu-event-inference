\label{sec:nominal-results}

\subsection{What the Sparse PMUs Recover}

Table~\ref{tab:nominal-results} gives the nominal TEST results. Conditioning the equilibrium estimate on the current PMU sample (B1) reduces hidden-voltage HVRE by more than one order of magnitude. The causal Kalman recursion (B2) improves on B1 in \BTwoWinRate{} of the same trajectories, but its paired mean advantage is only \BTwoMinusBOnePP{} percentage points. Most of the nominal gain therefore comes from the measurement-conditioned physical map; temporal propagation supplies a smaller correction.

\subsection{Functional Structure as a Predictor of Error}

At horizon 180, the finite-horizon Gramian has pseudo-rank 39 of 114 and the aggregate target residual is nonzero; neither full-state nor exact functional observability is declared. The preregistered bus residual nevertheless correlates with B2 HVRE at Spearman $\rho=\FunctionalSpearman$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:nominal-reconstruction}(b)], with a two-sided permutation $p$-value of \FunctionalPermutationP{}. Because the 31 buses share trajectories and network coupling, bus-resampling intervals are treated as exploratory rather than as independent-unit confidence intervals. The same qualitative relation appears for B1.

The relationship is informative but incomplete. Bus 36 has the largest structural residual, whereas the five largest B2 errors occur elsewhere. The projector captures one source of difficulty but not the nonlinear excitation distribution, estimator covariance, or every conditioning effect. Electrical distance to the nearest PMU has not been computed on the same audited network, so the analysis cannot yet show that $r_{F,i}$ adds information beyond that confounder. The residual remains a diagnostic, not a binary observability certificate.

\subsection{Retrospective Smoothing Test}

Across lags from one frame to 2~s, the implemented fixed-lag smoother never improves mean HVRE over lag zero; every positive lag is slightly worse in the nonlinear TEST trajectories. A correctly specified linear-Gaussian smoother cannot increase expected squared error, so this result diagnoses a violated modeling premise rather than a general failure of smoothing. The local model absorbs deterministic governor/load excitation into white process noise, while lag-one innovation autocorrelation remains \OriginalInnovationACF{} and the Ljung--Box test rejects whiteness. An unknown-input estimator \cite{gillijns2007input} and a re-estimated process model are required before a latency--accuracy conclusion is drawn.
