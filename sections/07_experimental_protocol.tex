\section{Nominal Reconstruction and Structural Evidence}
\label{sec:nominal-results}

\subsection{What the Sparse PMUs Recover}

Table~\ref{tab:nominal-results} reports the frozen nominal TEST results. Remaining at the equilibrium (B0) gives \BZeroTVE{} mean hidden-voltage HVRE. Applying the same PMU evidence through the snapshot LMMSE update (B1) reduces this to \BOneTVE{}, showing that the dominant nominal gain comes from the measurement-conditioned physical map rather than temporal recurrence. The causal Kalman recursion (B2) reaches \BTwoTVE{}. Because both estimators score the same 100 trajectories, the relevant comparison is the paired difference: B2 minus B1 is \BTwoMinusBOnePP{} percentage points (95\% paired-bootstrap CI, \BTwoMinusBOneCILowPP{} to \BTwoMinusBOneCIHighPP{}) and B2 wins on \BTwoWinRate{} of trajectories. The temporal contribution is therefore consistent but small in absolute magnitude, a \BTwoRelativeImprovement{} reduction in the mean.

\subsection{Does Functional Structure Predict Error?}

At horizon 180, the finite-horizon Gramian has pseudo-rank 39 of 114 and the aggregate target residual is nonzero. Accordingly, the experiment does not declare exact full-state or exact functional observability. The preregistered bus residual nevertheless correlates with B2 HVRE at $\rho=\FunctionalSpearman$ (Spearman), as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:nominal-reconstruction}(b). A bus-level permutation test gives the two-sided $p$-value \FunctionalPermutationP{}, and Kendall $\tau=\FunctionalKendall$. Because the 31 bus errors share trajectories and network coupling, the previously computed resampling interval [\FunctionalSpearmanCILow, \FunctionalSpearmanCIHigh] is retained only as exploratory sensitivity, not as an independent-unit confidence interval. The same qualitative relation appears for B1.

The relationship is informative but incomplete. Bus 36 has the largest structural residual, whereas the largest B2 errors occur at buses 33, 34, 20, 37, and 38. Thus the structural projector captures one source of difficulty but not the nonlinear excitation distribution, estimator covariance, or all numerical conditioning effects. Electrical distance to the nearest PMU has not yet been computed from the same audited PowerDynamics network, so the present analysis does not establish that $r_{F,i}$ adds information beyond that confounder. This counterexample and unresolved control are why the residual is reported as a diagnostic rather than converted into a binary observability certificate.

\subsection{Does Retrospective Smoothing Help?}

Across lags from one frame to 2~s, the exact fixed-lag smoother never improves mean HVRE over lag zero; every positive lag is slightly worse in the frozen results. Figure~\ref{fig:temporal-uncertainty}(a) shows the full curve. In this regime, current voltage and terminal-current phasors already dominate the hidden-voltage update, and additional future samples do not compensate for the local-model approximation. The negative result rules out latency as a useful trade for accuracy under the tested nominal perturbations.
