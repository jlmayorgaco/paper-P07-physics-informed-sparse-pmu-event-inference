\section{Nominal Reconstruction and Structural Evidence}
\label{sec:nominal-results}

\subsection{What the Sparse PMUs Recover}

Table~\ref{tab:nominal-results} reports the frozen nominal TEST results. Remaining at the equilibrium (B0) gives \BZeroTVE{} mean hidden-bus TVE. Applying the same PMU evidence through the snapshot electrical update (B1) reduces this to \BOneTVE{}, showing that the dominant nominal gain comes from the measurement-constrained physical map rather than temporal recurrence. The causal Kalman recursion (B2) reaches \BTwoTVE{}, a further \BTwoRelativeImprovement{} reduction relative to B1. Its 95\% trajectory-bootstrap interval overlaps that of B1, so the evidence supports a modest temporal improvement rather than a categorical advantage.

\subsection{Does Functional Structure Predict Error?}

At horizon 180, the finite-horizon matrix has numerical rank 39 of 114 and the aggregate target residual is nonzero. Accordingly, the experiment does not declare exact full-state or exact functional observability. The preregistered bus residual nevertheless correlates with B2 TVE at $\rho=\FunctionalSpearman$ (Spearman), as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:nominal-reconstruction}(b). The same qualitative relation appears for B1. This supports the narrower claim that the local structural diagnostic ranks empirical difficulty better than chance under the nominal benchmark.

The relationship is informative but incomplete. Bus 36 has the largest structural residual, whereas the largest B2 errors occur at buses 33, 34, 20, 37, and 38. Thus the structural projector captures one source of difficulty but not the nonlinear excitation distribution, estimator covariance, or all numerical conditioning effects. This counterexample is precisely why the residual is reported as a diagnostic rather than converted into a binary observability certificate.

\subsection{Does Retrospective Smoothing Help?}

Across lags from one frame to 2~s, the exact fixed-lag smoother never improves mean TVE over lag zero; every positive lag is slightly worse in the frozen results. Figure~\ref{fig:temporal-uncertainty}(a) shows the full curve. In this regime, current voltage and terminal-current phasors already dominate the hidden-voltage update, and additional future samples do not compensate for the local-model approximation. The negative result rules out latency as a useful trade for accuracy under the tested nominal perturbations.
