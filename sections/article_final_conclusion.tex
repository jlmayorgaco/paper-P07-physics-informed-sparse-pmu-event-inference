\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We formulated sequential load and line attribution from sparse PMUs as
a comparison of switched physical histories. The predictor retains
estimated state at restoration and separates an active intervention
from a preceding cause of recovery. On a held-out simulation under a
common calibration budget, the nominal bank attributed
\RecBankTimely{} events in time, versus \RecGlrTimely{} for a continuous
physical GLR and \RecTreesTimely{} for ExtraTrees. The respective
unmatched alarm counts were \RecBankExtras{}, \RecGlrExtras{} and
\RecTreesExtras{}. A fixed-threshold state-reset control shows the cost
of discarding the estimated state. The comparison leaves open the
specific benefit of retaining explicit recovery histories at matched
alarm budgets, transfer to another dynamic plant and robustness to
independent PMU errors.
