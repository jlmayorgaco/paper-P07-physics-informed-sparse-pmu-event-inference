\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The proposed procedure attributes load and line interventions by comparing
their predicted PMU responses with the observed stream. Each candidate
starts from an estimated past state. Restoration changes its input or
topology while its physical state continues to evolve. The method
integrates local load severity and reports event activity separately from
the cause of a continuing response.

The sealed continuous
evaluation gives \COneMixTimely{}/\COneMixTotal{} timely family/source
decisions with \COneMixExtras{} extra alarms for the mixture, and
\COneNomTimely{}/\COneNomTotal{} with \COneNomExtras{} for nominal.
The frozen ExtraTrees reference gives \COneETTimely{}/\COneETTotal{}
with \COneETExtras{} extras. Most mixture extras arise under out-of-bank
R/X, so the gain in timely attribution does not imply uniformly better
alarm control.

The component experiments preserve useful earlier findings: network
conditioning recovers hidden voltage information, graph-temporal features
improve a historical learned localizer, and mixed-source terms reduce
local response error. They do not establish an isolated causal gain from
Bayes or tensor storage in the final estimator.

The posthoc component study sharpens the recovery result. On the same 18
continuous trajectories, both score arms with physical-state carry attribute
108/108 events on time with one extra alarm; both reset arms attribute
75/108 with 166 extras. Marginal and bounded-profile scores tie on these
endpoints, while the profile arm retains conditional Bayesian state
estimation. The observed contrast supports physical-state carry at inferred
closure in this cohort. It does not establish the necessity or dispensability
of Bayesian state estimation, prior weighting, or other temporal information.

The earlier hackathon pipeline remains a broader nine-label,
feature-based system. Its 30-s-window validation is a separate result;
the causal ExtraTrees successor in this paper provides the direct
same-stream comparison. The physical bank's strongest supported gain is
timely load-source attribution, while learned features already diagnose
most lines in this catalog with fewer unmatched alarms.

The evidence concerns a specified load/line catalog in one simulator.
Unmodeled R/X increases the alarm burden. The controlled component study
addresses amplitude scoring and state carry at inferred closure; it does
not remove all Bayesian estimation or all temporal memory. All-family
diagnosis, calibrated event probabilities, field transfer and real-time
deployment remain outside the demonstrated scope.

A source-excluded comparison would test whether the procedure generalizes
beyond its supported catalog. A learned correction of physical residuals
could address model mismatch, provided it is evaluated independently of
source-label fitting. Joint state/context uncertainty and event-probability
calibration also require further validation.
