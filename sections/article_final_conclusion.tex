\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A restored load or line can leave a transient that resembles a new
intervention. The proposed bank predicts that transient from the carried
state and evaluates new source hypotheses against it. At a common
calibration target, the bank correctly attributed \RecBankTimely{}
interventions before restoration, compared with \RecGlrTimely{} for a
continuous GLR and \RecTreesTimely{} for ExtraTrees. During event records,
the three procedures emitted \RecBankExtras{}, \RecGlrExtras{} and
\RecTreesExtras{} unmatched alarms, respectively.

The controlled two-plant study distinguishes retaining the physical mean
from retaining alternative recovery histories. Mean preservation improves
on reset in both plants. History retention adds
\CThreeFOneHistoryCarryDifferencePP{} percentage points over mean merging
on F01, but its difference on the second plant has an interval containing
zero. These outcomes support state continuity across the tested plants and
bound the attribution advantage of explicit histories to the original
plant. The experiments cover nonoverlapping load and line interventions
from a fixed catalog. Independent per-PMU noise and model mismatch
remain important sources of attribution error.
