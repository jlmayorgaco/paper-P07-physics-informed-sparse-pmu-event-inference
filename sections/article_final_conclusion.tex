\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A restored electrical intervention can remain the cause of the observed
response. We formulated load/line attribution from sparse PMUs as change
detection and isolation with switched-DAE post-change models, in which
histories start from estimated past states, keep that state through
restoration and separate the active intervention from its originating
cause. Because the histories are linear-Gaussian given their mode paths,
the network operators also give each candidate's detection and isolation
information, its minimum detectable change, the cost of forgetting the
physical state and the information left when a PMU is missing or
corrupted.

In an independent simulated evaluation the bank attributes
\COneMixTimely{}/\COneMixTotal{} interventions before restoration and raises
no alarm in 11.8\,h of normal records; its remaining errors and most of its
\COneMixExtras{} extra alarms arise from network-model mismatch. The
information analysis explains this level of performance, separates the
information-limited part of the alarm delay from its algorithmic part,
and predicts the cost of resetting the state and the noise level at which
the smallest load becomes undetectable. \RevConclusionNoise{} On the
competition record, sequence-parity and network-coupling relations
diagnose every labeled missing and corrupted PMU episode at its first
report, with no training, and reveal corruptions that the labels miss.
A training-free attributor built on the same network then attributes all
12 labeled events of every type on that record. In a stored simulated
test, however, it locates most line pulses but misses most short load
pulses, which only the dynamic model attributes.
The single-model bank already runs faster than the PMU reporting rate.

Three directions follow. First, a missing PMU must switch the observation
operator of every history, as Proposition~\ref{prop:integrity} requires;
imputing held values defeats the bank even though ample information
remains. Second, the cross-PMU noise covariance must be estimated from the
startup prefix, so that the bank can start on field data, including the
competition record. Third, the detectability analysis can be extended
from a fixed catalog to placement and catalog design, and the bank to
faults and generation changes, which the network attributor already
covers; transfer to an independent dynamic model is the test toward field
use.
