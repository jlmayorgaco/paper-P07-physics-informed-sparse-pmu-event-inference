\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We formulated sequential load and line attribution from sparse PMUs as
a comparison of switched physical histories. The predictor retains
estimated state at restoration and separates an active intervention
from a preceding cause of recovery. On a held-out simulation under a
common calibration budget, the nominal bank made timely,
correct family-and-source attributions for \RecBankTimely{}
interventions, versus \RecGlrTimely{} for a continuous
physical GLR and \RecTreesTimely{} for ExtraTrees. The respective
unmatched alarm counts were \RecBankExtras{}, \RecGlrExtras{} and
\RecTreesExtras{}. A fixed-threshold state-reset control shows the cost
of discarding the estimated state. The complete-procedure comparison does
not isolate the benefit of retaining recovery histories from the loss of
uncertainty under compression. The findings apply to sequential interventions
from a known catalog in one simulator family. Transfer to another dynamic
plant, shorter gaps and unseen sources remain untested; independent per-PMU
noise at 0.05\% TVE severely degrades load attribution.
