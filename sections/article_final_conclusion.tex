\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

After a load returns or a line recloses, the pre-restoration estimate
supplies a physical prediction for what the PMUs should see next.
Comparing new interventions against that prediction gave the history
bank more correct source diagnoses before restoration than either
comparator. It also issued more unmatched alarms than ExtraTrees and
fewer than the continuous GLR. On both simulated plants, keeping the
estimated physical state at closure produced more timely diagnoses
than resetting it. Keeping separate histories met the prespecified criterion for a
short-gap coverage gain on the original plant. On the second plant its
measurable advantage was fewer unmatched alarms during event records.
At common alarm times, spectral classification confused several second
load changes with line events. Carrying the physical state gave the
family estimator more supported diagnoses than resetting it. Summing
the retained histories by family at a fixed lag matched the bank's
original family decisions on the new pairs. Improving the common
trigger remains important because a missed alarm prevents either
classifier from reporting the event. On the reduced four-family plant,
the main remaining loss was generator attribution. Six isolated short
pulses went undetected, and four pulses during recovery were assigned
to the wrong generator bus. A stricter voltage check lost joint
diagnoses and increased duplicate or unmatched alarms. These results
favor improving the early generator response model before tightening
the publication rule.
