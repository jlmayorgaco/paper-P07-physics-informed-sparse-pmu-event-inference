\section{Interventions and PMU Observations}
\label{sec:narrative-problem}

The monitor receives one report at $t_k=k/30$ seconds. It must detect a
new intervention, identify its family and asset, and recognize restoration.
Physical onset, alarm time and diagnosis time are distinct. The source and
switch times are available only for scoring. The monitor estimates the
initial operating point and noise scale from the first 300 reports, which
are assumed to precede any intervention. Monitoring begins at 12~s.

Figure~\ref{fig:reference-event-types} places the two tested families in
the wider range of events that a PMU can record. The evaluation considers
load changes and line openings. The supplement examines missing and
corrupted observations separately.

\subsection{Switched Network Model}

A load intervention scales its active and reactive setpoints by $1+a$,
with unknown fractional change $a$. Opening a line removes its branch
primitive from the network equations. The catalog in
Table~\ref{tab:source-catalog} excludes islanding openings.
An intervention history $h=(e,c,\tau,\kappa,\mathcal I)$ records the
family $e$, asset $c$, onset $\tau$ and restoration $\kappa$, with
$\kappa=\infty$ for a sustained event. A load history also specifies the
interval $\mathcal I$ containing $a$. A line history has no severity
parameter.

For network mode $q$, differential states $x$ include the machines and
their controls, algebraic variables $z$ include the bus voltages, $u$
contains the load inputs, and $\theta$ denotes the plant parameters. They
satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \dot x=f_q(x,z,u;\theta),\qquad 0=g_q(x,z,u;\theta).
 \label{eq:narrative-dae}
\end{equation}
At an admitted switch time $s\in\{\tau,\kappa\}$, the differential state
is continuous and the post-switch network determines $z(s^+)$,
\begin{equation}
 x(s^+)=x(s^-),\quad
 g_{q^+}\big(x(s^-),z(s^+),u(s^+);\theta\big)=0.
 \label{eq:narrative-switch}
\end{equation}
We fix the angle reference and work on local solutions with nonsingular
algebraic Jacobian. A report at $s$ uses the post-switch solution.
Reclosing a branch restores its admittance. The machine angles and speeds
take their values from the immediately preceding instant, so the same
admittance can produce a different terminal current after reclosure.

The eight PMUs measure voltage at the buses marked in
Fig.~\ref{fig:narrative-topology},
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P=\{2,5,6,10,19,22,29,39\}.
 \label{eq:narrative-pmus}

Current refers to a specific branch terminal. For example, the voltage
at bus~2 is paired with current at the bus~3 end of line~2--3
(Table~\ref{tab:main-terminal-map}). The bus~5 current retains the
provisional assignment to line~5--6. At bus~39 the current angle is
excluded because its phase convention is only partially verified.

Let $\zeta_k=H_q(x_k,z_k,u_k)$ collect the selected electrical phasors.
Phase history and instrument-filter memory $\eta_k$ enter each report
through
\begin{align}
 \eta_k&=F_M(\eta_{k-1},\zeta_k;\theta_M),\nonumber\\
 y_k&=G_M(\eta_k,\zeta_k;\theta_M)+b+\varepsilon_k ,
 \label{eq:narrative-measurement}
\end{align}
where $\theta_M$ specifies the measurement convention, $b$ is the
observation offset and $\varepsilon_k$ is instrument noise. Bus frequency
and its rate of change are derived from the voltage phase history.
The resulting vector has 47 coordinates per report,
\[
 y_k=\big(|V_A|_{\mathcal P},\angle V_A{}_{\mathcal P},
 |I_A|_{\mathcal P},\angle I_A{}_{\mathcal P\setminus\{39\}},
 f_{\mathcal P},\dot f_{\mathcal P}\big)_k .
\]
The simulator exports balanced phase-A phasors and constructs the other
two phases by fixed angle shifts. Phase A therefore supplies all the
independent phasor information in these records.
