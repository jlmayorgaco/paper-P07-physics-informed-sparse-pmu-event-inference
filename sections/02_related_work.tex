\section{Related Work and Research Gap}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{PMU Event Identification and Localization}

Synchrophasor methods have addressed disturbance detection, type identification, and localization through signal processing, model-based estimation, and learned representations. Low-dimensional subspace characterization compares streaming synchrophasor data with an offline event dictionary \cite{li2018subspace}; convolutional fault-line localization has examined the interaction between learned voltage features and PMU placement \cite{li2019cnn}; staged diagnosis can combine detection, classification, and topology-aware localization \cite{pandey2020realtime}; and graph learning can infer temporal interactions for event classification \cite{yuan2023gnn}. These are meaningful recognition and localization families, but their reported data, label, and evaluation contracts differ. The present problem asks a narrower question: can a physical hypothesis be evaluated when the corresponding asset has supplied no source-labeled training or threshold-selection example?

\subsection{Physics-Based Candidate Inference}

Physical inference supplies a complementary mechanism to source-label recognition. For loss-of-generation events, Azizi \emph{et al.} derive sparse-PMU relationships between a candidate source and voltage/current changes \cite{azizi2021log}. Their line-outage work derives a transfer relation and ranks candidates with a weighted residual without requiring a fully observable network \cite{azizi2023line}. These methods motivate a family-specific physical hypothesis bank and residual baseline. They do not supply a common likelihood for faults, line interventions, generation changes, load changes, and measurement faults, nor do they establish the behavior of a learned discrepancy on assets withheld from source-labeled fitting.

\subsection{Model-Based Dynamic Estimation}

Dynamic state estimation places measurements in a state-space or differential-algebraic model and has a broad role in monitoring and control. The IEEE task-force review by Zhao \emph{et al.}\ distinguishes dynamic, tracking, forecasting-aided, and static estimation while identifying model uncertainty, computation, and communication as central implementation concerns \cite{zhao2019dse}. This literature provides the modeling foundation for physical residuals and nonlinear refinement. It does not automatically yield a source-decision rule under sparse, changing measurements: state estimation, intervention inference, and measurement-integrity diagnosis pose related but different inverse problems.

\subsection{Sparse Observability and Placement}

PMU-placement research formalizes where instruments should be installed to achieve observability or improve a designated task. Jamei \emph{et al.} analyze fault-location ambiguity under incomplete observability through hypothesis distributions and a KL-based separation measure \cite{jamei2020limits}. Recent work has explicitly optimized sparse placement for event detection and identification, illustrating that placement changes the attainable accuracy of both model-based and data-driven methods \cite{yildiz2025sparse}. Placement, however, is normally planned before a deployment. This manuscript addresses the complementary online question: given the PMUs currently reporting, which physical hypotheses remain distinguishable and when should the system abstain from a unique source claim?

\subsection{Streaming, Imperfect Data, and Communication}

Streaming PMU applications must accommodate temporal dependence, missing or poor-quality samples, and communication constraints. Event-identification studies have used PMU status and data cleaning to manage imperfect measurements \cite{yuan2021learning,liu2023imperfect}; real bulk-system PMU records also make curated data-quality contexts available for later external study \cite{biswas2023library}. Communication reviews show that measurement and transfer standards, architecture, and reliability are part of the broader synchrophasor system \cite{appasani2018communication}. These treatments motivate explicit availability and quality variables. We retain them as measurement-side states instead of merging them with faults, outages, or dispatch changes in a single event label.

\subsection{Transfer and the Boundary of the Present Claim}

Transfer learning has been proposed for power-system event identification when source and target grids differ in measurement spaces, distributions, and location labels \cite{li2022transfer}. That work aligns target labels at the zone level; it is not an exact-asset source-holdout experiment. Its distinction between measurement, distribution, and label transfer informs the conditional knowledge-transfer campaign here. P07 therefore does not use ``transfer'' as shorthand for a new random seed, a new event replica at a known asset, or a different simulator without a frozen semantic mapping.

\subsection{Research Gap}

The literature contains strong components for event recognition, physical residual inference, dynamic estimation, placement, transfer, and data-quality handling. What remains to be tested in one coherent setting is a dynamic intervention framework that evaluates source hypotheses beyond source-label training, quantifies their separation after PMU membership changes, fuses evidence without assuming a fixed sensor vector, and abstains when the resulting posterior ambiguity is material. The paper does not claim that the underlying divergence, information, residual, or placement tools are new. Its contribution is the proposed integration and the experimental contracts required to evaluate it.
