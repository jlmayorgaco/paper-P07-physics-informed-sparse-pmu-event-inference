\section{Related Work and Research Gap}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{PMU-Based Dynamic State Estimation}

Power-system dynamic state estimation combines high-rate synchronized measurements with machine, control, load, and network models. The IEEE task-force review in \cite{zhao2019dse} distinguishes dynamic, tracking, forecasting-aided, and static formulations and identifies model uncertainty, numerical stability, and measurement availability as persistent concerns. Local Gaussian methods remain important because they provide a transparent baseline, recursive covariance, and direct connection to the classical Kalman filter \cite{kalman1960}. Robust unscented and cubature filters address non-Gaussian measurement noise and communication-aware updating \cite{zhao2018gmukf,li2023eventtriggered}. More recent nonlinear DAE work estimates differential and algebraic states jointly even when some component models are unavailable \cite{katanic2024recursive}. The present paper addresses a different contract: all modeled components are available nominally, but the PMU set is sparse and the physical plant may depart from the frozen estimator model.

Incomplete-observability estimators recover algebraic variables by combining observable PMU regions with linear or robust updates \cite{pourkeivani2024algebraic}; joint state-input filters instead treat unavailable bus-voltage dynamics as unknown inputs and can be distributed across a network \cite{nguyen2021unknown}. Data-driven estimators can also reconstruct unmeasured states or learn dynamical representations from PMU histories. Such methods can be useful baselines when the physical model is incomplete. However, a low nominal test error does not establish behavior under a physically rebuilt network or operating-point shift. This study first isolates what audited physics alone can achieve; a learned residual model is deferred until it can be compared against an adaptive physical baseline without target leakage.

\subsection{Functional Rather Than Full-State Observability}

Functional observers estimate a specified linear function of the state without requiring reconstruction of every state coordinate \cite{fernando2010functional}. Recent work provides unified conditions for functional and structural functional observability and connects them to sensor placement \cite{zhang2025functional}. This distinction is directly relevant to PMU monitoring: rotor, controller, and algebraic coordinates may contain directions that are weakly observable or irrelevant to the requested bus-voltage output. The present study uses a finite-horizon output-nullspace residual as a diagnostic, preregisters its bus-level ranking, and then tests whether that ranking predicts nonlinear reconstruction error. It does not claim that a nonzero numerical residual constitutes exact functional observability.

PMU-placement studies similarly show that sensor value depends on the downstream task. Empirical observability Gramians have been optimized for dynamic-state estimation \cite{qi2015placement}, and nonlinear observability has been connected directly to limited-synchrophasor estimation under instability and moderate parameter error \cite{sun2016observability}. For fault localization, limited PMU placement creates hypothesis ambiguity and information limits \cite{jamei2020limits}; sparse placement can also be optimized for event detection and identification \cite{yildiz2025sparse}. Those studies motivate target-specific sensing, but the current target is continuous hidden-voltage reconstruction rather than complete dynamic-state recovery or event source classification.

\subsection{Model Uncertainty, Calibration, and Adaptation}

Robust filters and incomplete-model estimators account for process, measurement, or component uncertainty \cite{zhao2018gmukf,li2023eventtriggered,katanic2024recursive}. A separate issue is whether a nominal posterior covariance remains calibrated when the physical plant changes. Temperature scaling can repair marginal dispersion on an independent nominal split without correcting temporal innovation correlation or generative model error. Likewise, a low-rank colored residual can be statistically real yet fail to improve hidden-state uncertainty when inserted at the wrong point in the state-space model.

This paper therefore separates three questions: mean reconstruction under nominal physics, probabilistic diagnostics under nominal data, and robustness when the nonlinear plant itself is rebuilt with changed parameters. Oracle recentering and relinearization are used only to identify the failure mechanism; they are not deployable estimators. A causal observed-PMU recentering proxy is then tested and allowed to fail. This hierarchy prevents oracle information, post-hoc calibration, and adaptive estimation from being conflated.

\subsection{Benchmark Validity and Research Gap}

PowerDynamics.jl provides an open implementation for nonlinear grid dynamics \cite{plietzsch2022powerdynamics}. Package availability alone, however, does not validate a translated admittance matrix, a terminal-current operator, a dynamic equilibrium, or a linearization. The benchmark used here passes these gates before inference. Experimental PMU studies have additionally shown that model mapping, auxiliary systems, and cross-validation matter when DSE is taken to an operating grid \cite{lorenzmeyer2023field}. The package-defined IEEE 39-bus dynamics used here remain pedagogical, so the paper claims reproducible internal validity rather than field realism or cross-simulator transfer.

The unresolved gap is the full evidence chain from sparse PMUs and target-specific structure to nominal reconstruction, uncertainty diagnostics, and the physical mechanism of failure under mismatch.
The novelty claim is not that Kalman filtering, functional observability, covariance calibration, or Mahalanobis innovations are individually new. The contribution is a reproducible evaluation showing when each level is sufficient, when it fails, and which additional physical adaptation is justified by the observed failure.
