\section{Uncertainty, Physical Mismatch, and Adaptation}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Marginal Calibration Does Not Whiten Innovations}

The nominal B2 covariance is highly conservative: U0 covers essentially all real and imaginary errors at the nominal 95\% level. Fitting two temperatures on 30 independent CAL trajectories improves TEST hidden NLL from $-13.477$ to \UtwoNLL{} and gives \UtwoCoverageRe{} and \UtwoCoverageIm{} marginal 95\% coverage for real and imaginary components, respectively. This is useful marginal calibration, but it leaves the innovation lag-one autocorrelation unchanged at \OriginalInnovationACF{} and the Ljung--Box test rejects whiteness. Figure~\ref{fig:temporal-uncertainty}(b)--(c) and Table~\ref{tab:uncertainty-results} therefore separate interval coverage from temporal model adequacy.

The calibration residual is strongly low rank: its first two principal components explain 95.7\% of variance and four explain 99.2\%. That empirical fact does not guarantee a useful generative correction. The rank-four measurement Gauss--Markov model reduces lag-one autocorrelation from \OriginalInnovationACF{} to \MeasurementGMInnovationACF{}, but worsens HVRE, produces hidden NLL 14.991, and undercovers both components. The rank-one process placement leaves both HVRE and autocorrelation effectively unchanged. These ablations reject the claim that low-rank colored PMU residuals, inserted without stronger physical structure, improve hidden-state uncertainty.

\subsection{Which Physical Mismatches Matter?}

The 980-case rebuilt-plant campaign reveals sharp family dependence (Fig.~\ref{fig:physical-mismatch} and Table~\ref{tab:mismatch-results}). At $m=1.5$, network mismatch reaches \NetworkMismatchRatio{}$\times$ the pooled nominal median and operating-point mismatch reaches \OperatingMismatchRatio{}$\times$. The coupled family is smaller at 1.602$\times$, while machine, governor, AVR, and load-model families remain near the nominal median for the tested perturbations. A blanket claim of robustness would therefore be misleading: the estimator is insensitive to several tested component shifts but fails materially when the algebraic network map or equilibrium center changes.

Nominal covariance repair does not transfer automatically. Averaged across all mismatch cases, U0 has \MismatchUzeroCoverage{} 95\% coverage and nominally fitted U2 has only \MismatchUtwoCoverage{}. \subsection{Adequacy Signals Mismatch and Physical Static MAP Recovers M6}

The observed-PMU innovation statistic detects many cases in which hidden HVRE is unacceptable. Macro AUROC/AUPRC are \AdequacyHalfAUROC{}/\AdequacyHalfAUPRC{} at $m=0.5$ and \AdequacyMaxAUROC{}/\AdequacyMaxAUPRC{} at $m=1.5$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:physical-mismatch}(c)]. Undefined family-level AUROCs, caused when every frame has the same frozen adequacy label, are omitted from the macro mean; AUPRC remains reported. Consequently, the statistic supports model-adequacy triage, not mismatch-family classification or guaranteed alarm calibration.

The earlier R2-A offset-only ablation remains scientifically useful because it isolates what does not solve the problem. Across nonnominal identifiable M1, M6, and M7 cases it closes only \OnlineClosurePercent{} of the oracle gap, despite passing its nominal-safety gate. That failure does not imply that adaptation from observed PMUs is impossible; it shows that grouped voltage offsets without AC balance and a rebuilt physical center are inadequate.

The corrected regularized-recentering experiment directly tests that distinction for M6. All \EZeroHTestCount{} TEST solves satisfy the frozen convergence gate. From $m=0.5$ to 1.5, the nominal-center median HVRE rises from \EZeroHFrozenHalf{} to \EZeroHFrozenMax{}, whereas the physical solve gives \EZeroHCorrectedHalf{} to \EZeroHCorrectedMax{} [Fig.~\ref{fig:adequacy-adaptation}(a)]. Paired oracle-gap closure is \EZeroHClosureHalf{}, \EZeroHClosureOne{}, and \EZeroHClosureMax{} across the three scales, with bootstrap intervals reported in Table~\ref{tab:e06h-results}. Median solve time is \EZeroHRuntimeMedian{}~ms and the 95th percentile is \EZeroHRuntimePNinetyFive{}~ms. These timings characterize the implemented static solve only; they do not establish end-to-end real-time operation.

The nuisance information matrix has median rank \EZeroHRank{} for \EZeroHDimension{} coordinates, leaving an \EZeroHNullity{}-dimensional nullspace. Nevertheless, the requested hidden-voltage field is recovered with two orders of magnitude less error than the frozen center. This is empirical evidence for target recovery despite nuisance non-uniqueness, not a proof of exact functional identifiability: the local hidden-voltage residual is nonzero at \EZeroHFunctionalResidual{}. The distinction links the adaptation result to Section~\ref{sec:functional-observability} without claiming that the injection vector itself is recovered.

Point accuracy also does not certify posterior uncertainty. The nullspace-aware Laplace covariance attains 100\% marginal 95\% coverage, yet its median NEES-like statistic increases from \EZeroHNEESHalf{} to \EZeroHNEESMax{} [Fig.~\ref{fig:adequacy-adaptation}(c)]. The independent constrained solver also converges on only part of its six-case subset. The E06-H conclusion is therefore strong for corrected M6 point reconstruction, partial for local target structure and uncertainty, and not established for M1 network mismatch, M7 coupled mismatch, or sequential online adaptation.

\subsection{Does Localization Transfer to Unseen Physical Sources?}

No. With the physical source excluded from source-labeled fitting and the true event family supplied, only \HeldSourceCorrect{} of \HeldSourceDecisions{} decisions across \HeldSourceCases{} held-source cases and three model seeds are exact, a rate of \HeldSourceRate{}. This is not a detector failure or a confusion among event families: it is direct evidence that the learned location heads and candidate descriptors retain source-specific associations. The negative result invalidates any zero-shot-source claim for the legacy system.

Transfer to RAW0001 is also weak. The basic candidate model classifies an average of \RawCandidateEventEpisodes{} of 12 event episodes and localizes \RawCandidatePhysicalEpisodes{} of five physical episodes across three seeds. The simulator bank omits some channels used by the longer-window implementation, so the transfer deficit cannot be assigned to one feature family alone. It nevertheless shows that improving the internal simulation score does not establish cross-record reliability.

The event evidence therefore answers a precise baseline question: supervised, physics-guided feature engineering is effective for detection and known-source diagnosis, but it does not solve source-generalizable physical inference. The result motivates the candidate-conditioned residual objective in \eqref{eq:joint-map-objective} and the profiled separation test in \eqref{eq:pairwise-information}; it is not itself evidence that those proposed components work.
