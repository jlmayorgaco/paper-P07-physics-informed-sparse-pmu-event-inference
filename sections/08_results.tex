\section{Uncertainty, Physical Mismatch, and Adaptation}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Marginal Calibration Does Not Whiten Innovations}

The nominal B2 covariance is conservative and covers essentially all real and imaginary errors at the nominal 95\% level. Two temperatures fitted on 30 independent CAL trajectories bring the TEST marginal coverage closer to its target and improve hidden NLL (Table~\ref{tab:uncertainty-results}). They do not change the lag-one innovation autocorrelation, and the Ljung--Box test still rejects whiteness. Marginal calibration and temporal model adequacy are therefore separate properties [Fig.~\ref{fig:temporal-uncertainty}(b)--(c)].

Four principal components explain 99.2\% of the calibration-residual variance, but low rank alone does not make the residual a useful process model. A rank-four measurement Gauss--Markov correction lowers the lag-one autocorrelation, then worsens HVRE and undercovers both voltage components. Placing one mode in the process model changes neither HVRE nor autocorrelation appreciably. Colored residual structure must therefore be tied to a physical discrepancy mechanism before it improves hidden-state uncertainty.

\subsection{Mismatch Response Depends on Model Family}

The rebuilt-plant campaign reveals a clear family dependence (Fig.~\ref{fig:physical-mismatch} and Table~\ref{tab:mismatch-results}). At the largest tested scale, network mismatch raises the median error by \NetworkMismatchRatio{}$\times$ and an operating-point shift raises it by \OperatingMismatchRatio{}$\times$. Machine, governor, AVR, and load-model perturbations remain near the nominal median because this 3-s excitation does not measurably expose those parameter changes. Their near-$1\times$ ratios are regime-limited null responses, not evidence of robustness. The tested estimator fails visibly when the algebraic network map or equilibrium center changes.

Nominal covariance repair does not transfer automatically. Averaged across all mismatch cases, U0 has \MismatchUzeroCoverage{} 95\% coverage and nominally fitted U2 has only \MismatchUtwoCoverage{}. \subsection{Adequacy Detection and Physical Recentering}

The observed-PMU innovation statistic detects many cases in which hidden HVRE is unacceptable. At the largest tested scale, its macro AUROC/AUPRC is \AdequacyMaxAUROC{}/\AdequacyMaxAUPRC{} [Fig.~\ref{fig:physical-mismatch}(c)]. Family-level AUROC is omitted when every frame has the same frozen adequacy label; AUPRC remains defined. The statistic can flag a stale model, but it neither identifies the mismatch family nor supplies a calibrated alarm probability.

The earlier R2-A offset-only ablation remains scientifically useful because it isolates what does not solve the problem. Across nonnominal identifiable M1, M6, and M7 cases it closes only \OnlineClosurePercent{} of the oracle gap, despite passing its nominal-safety gate. That failure does not imply that adaptation from observed PMUs is impossible; it shows that grouped voltage offsets without AC balance and a rebuilt physical center are inadequate.

The regularized-recentering experiment tests that distinction for M6. Every TEST case satisfies the prespecified convergence gate. Across the three mismatch scales, the physical solve removes \EZeroHClosureHalf{}--\EZeroHClosureMax{} of the error gap to an oracle center [Fig.~\ref{fig:adequacy-adaptation}(a)--(b)]. The median solve takes \EZeroHRuntimeMedian{}~ms. Table~\ref{tab:e06h-results} retains the point errors, paired intervals, and tail runtime; these static timings do not establish end-to-end real-time operation.

The nuisance information matrix has median rank \EZeroHRank{} among \EZeroHDimension{} coordinates. Even with that nullspace, the requested voltage field is recovered with two orders of magnitude less error than the frozen center. This supports target recovery despite nuisance non-uniqueness. It does not prove exact functional identifiability because the local hidden-voltage residual remains nonzero, nor does it show that the injection vector is recovered.

Point accuracy does not certify posterior uncertainty. The pseudoinverse Laplace covariance is formed only on locally informed nuisance directions; because $\bm J_{zd}\bm N(\bm J_{yd})\ne\bm0$ in \eqref{eq:e06h-functional-residual}, it does not represent the image of the PMU-uninformed nuisance nullspace in the hidden-voltage target. This explains how marginal intervals can cover every component while the joint NEES-like statistic grows with mismatch and remains far above its nominal reference [Fig.~\ref{fig:adequacy-adaptation}(c)]. The independent constrained solver also converges on only part of its small diagnostic subset. M6 point reconstruction is supported; joint uncertainty, network mismatch, coupled mismatch, and sequential adaptation are not.

\subsection{Physics-Based Load Detection and Source Resolution}

The centered numerical tangent passes two checks before source inference is scored. Across all 16 load candidates, its largest discrepancy from either one-sided derivative is \LoadTangentMaxRelError{}, and the minimum cosine between the positive and negative perturbation directions is \LoadTangentMinCosine{}. At a separate 10\% stress, the median and maximum relative trajectory errors of the tangent prediction are \LoadTangentMedianStressError{} and \LoadTangentMaxStressError{}. These tests support the numerical physical dictionary over the stated range; they do not complete the pending analytic descriptor-DAE derivative.

Normal-data covariance changes the decision rule materially. The deliberately unscaled W0 identity diagnostic produces a 100\% false-positive rate and 10.9\% source Top-1; it is not an honest competitive covariance baseline. Channel covariance plus temporal AR(1) whitening gives \LoadVOneFPR{} false positives and \LoadVOneTopOne{} Top-1 on \LoadVOneEventCases{} event records. The larger V2 normal bank confirms the statistical mechanism: W2 has NIS per frame \LoadWTwoNIS{}, lag-one autocorrelation \LoadWTwoACFOne{}, and Ljung--Box $p=\LoadWTwoLjungP{}$. Channel covariance alone leaves lag-one autocorrelation at 0.341. A CAL-fitted scaled-identity baseline remains to be run.

The nonlinear remainder follows the expected local order. A log--log fit over candidate buses and development amplitudes gives $p=\LoadTruncationSlope{}$ with bootstrap 95\% interval $[\LoadTruncationCILow{},\LoadTruncationCIHigh{}]$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:load-event-inference}(a)]. The second-order mean model L2 is then selected on DEV with \LoadLTwoDevCoverage{} amplitude coverage. On the independent finite-amplitude TEST block, its 95\% coverage ranges from \LoadFiniteCoverageMin{} to \LoadFiniteCoverageMax{} over signed changes from 1.25 to 9\% [Fig.~\ref{fig:load-event-inference}(f)].

Weak events expose the difference between detection and localization. At $|a|=0.01\%$, sign-pooled AUROC is 0.733 while exact source Top-1 is 13.0\%; at $|a|=0.05\%$, the same quantities are 0.995 and 73.4\%. Source Top-1 reaches 100\% at $|a|=0.4\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:load-weak-results}). The event false-positive rate is 0.25\% on \LoadWeakNoEventCases{} no-event records. Thus a window can contain detectable event evidence while remaining insufficient for a reliable source decision.

Amplitude Fisher information is negatively associated with the empirical amplitude required for 90\% detection, with Spearman $\rho=\LoadEVIRho{}$ over 16 sources [Fig.~\ref{fig:load-event-inference}(e)]. This is exploratory: each 90\% threshold is selected from only six tested magnitudes, so the result has visible discretization and no fitted psychometric interval or permutation $p$-value. The profiled pairwise information has $\rho=\LoadProjectedFisherRho{}$ against a pooled 16-way confusion statistic. That comparison mismatches a pairwise quantity to an amplitude-marginalized multicandidate outcome and is not treated as a validation test. A proper bridge requires predicted versus empirical Top-1 for each source--amplitude pair.

\subsection{Known-Source Detection and Localization}

On the known-source TEST split, the availability-gated hierarchy reaches detection F1 \LegacyEventDetectionFOne{} but only \LegacyPhysicalTopOne{} physical Top-1 (Table~\ref{tab:legacy-event-results}). Expanding the representation adds \LegacyPhysicalGainPP{} percentage points to source ranking over the compact hierarchy. Multiscale dynamics, adaptive residuals, and PMU contrasts therefore help when the source is represented during training; the availability rule contributes mainly to integrity handling.

The gap between detection F1 and physical Top-1 confirms that a recognizable disturbance need not have a uniquely recoverable source. False alarms decrease to \LegacyFalseAlarmsPerMinute{} episodes per normal-labeled minute, but the normal exposure is only about 7.15~min per split. That rate is evidence of alarm burden in a short synthetic stream, not an operational false-alarm guarantee.

\subsection{Source Holdout Breaks Learned Localization}

With the physical source excluded from source-labeled fitting and the true event family supplied, only \HeldSourceCorrect{} of \HeldSourceDecisions{} decisions are exact. Detection and event-family confusion have been removed from this test. The remaining error shows that the learned location heads and candidate descriptors retain source-specific associations, invalidating a zero-shot-source claim for the legacy system.

Transfer to RAW0001 is also weak: the basic candidate model localizes only \RawCandidatePhysicalEpisodes{} of five physical episodes on average. The simulator bank omits some channels used by the longer-window implementation, so no single feature family can be blamed for the deficit. Internal simulation accuracy does not establish cross-record reliability.

Supervised, physics-guided features work for detection and known-source diagnosis but not for source-generalizable inference. The physical load experiment supplies a contrasting result: candidate sources are evaluated through simulated responses rather than learned asset labels. Its 16-source success is restricted to one event family, fixed topology, and matched simulator; it does not repair the broader held-source or cross-record failure.
