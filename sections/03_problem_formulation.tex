\section{System and Sparse-PMU Reconstruction Problem}
\label{sec:problem-formulation}

\subsection{Hybrid DAE Plant}

Let $\bm{x}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_x}$ denote differential states, $\bm{z}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_z}$ algebraic states, $\bm{u}(t)$ exogenous inputs, $\bm{q}(t)$ the physical network configuration, and $\bm{\theta}$ physical parameters. The general plant is
\begin{align}
 \dot{\bm{x}}(t) &= \bm{f}\!\left(\bm{x},\bm{z},\bm{u},\bm{q};\bm{\theta}\right)+\bm{w}_x(t), \label{eq:dae-dynamic}\\
 \bm{0} &= \bm{g}\!\left(\bm{x},\bm{z},\bm{u},\bm{q};\bm{\theta}\right), \label{eq:dae-algebraic}\\
 \bm{y}_{\mathcal P}(t) &= \bm{h}_{\mathcal P}\!\left(\bm{x},\bm{z},\bm{q};\bm{\theta}\right)+\bm{v}(t), \label{eq:dae-measurement}
\end{align}
where $\bm{w}_x$ and $\bm{v}$ denote process and measurement uncertainty in the local probabilistic model. Equations \eqref{eq:dae-dynamic}--\eqref{eq:dae-measurement} define the long-term Hybrid-DAE framework; the experiments in this paper validate its local linear-Gaussian special case, not the complete nonlinear posterior.

The network algebraic core is
\begin{equation}
 \bm{I}=\bm{Y}_{\mathrm{bus}}(\bm q,\bm\theta)\bm V,
 \qquad S_i=P_i+\mathrm{j}Q_i=V_i I_i^{*}. \label{eq:network-core}
\end{equation}
Writing $\bm{Y}_{\mathrm{bus}}=\bm G+\mathrm{j}\bm B$ and $V_i=|V_i|e^{\mathrm{j}\vartheta_i}$ gives
\begin{align}
 P_i &= |V_i|\sum_j |V_j|\left[G_{ij}\cos(\vartheta_i-\vartheta_j)+B_{ij}\sin(\vartheta_i-\vartheta_j)\right], \label{eq:ac-p}\\
 Q_i &= |V_i|\sum_j |V_j|\left[G_{ij}\sin(\vartheta_i-\vartheta_j)-B_{ij}\cos(\vartheta_i-\vartheta_j)\right]. \label{eq:ac-q}
\end{align}
For a PMU measuring one terminal of branch $(i,j)$, the current operator retains the branch primitive orientation,
\begin{equation}
 I_{ij}=Y_{ff,ij}V_i+Y_{ft,ij}V_j,
 \qquad I_{ji}=Y_{tf,ij}V_i+Y_{tt,ij}V_j. \label{eq:terminal-current}
\end{equation}
Replacing \eqref{eq:terminal-current} with a generic bus-injection current would change the measurement contract.

\subsection{Observed and Target Outputs}

The study uses the observed-bus set
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P=\{2,5,6,10,19,22,29,39\},\qquad |\mathcal P|=8, \label{eq:pmu-set}
\end{equation}
and the unobserved set $\mathcal U=\{1,\ldots,39\}\setminus\mathcal P$, with $|\mathcal U|=31$. The synthetic PMU vector stacks rectangular voltage and controlled terminal-current components,
\begin{equation}
 \bm y_{\mathcal P,k}=\operatorname{col}_{p\in\mathcal P}
 \left[\Re V_{p,k},\Im V_{p,k},\Re I_{p\ell,k},\Im I_{p\ell,k}\right]. \label{eq:pmu-vector}
\end{equation}
Frequency and ROCOF remain valid future measurement operators but are not silently treated as independent instantaneous states in the validated experiments.

The primary target is the hidden complex-voltage field
\begin{equation}
 \bm z_{\mathcal U,k}=\operatorname{col}_{i\in\mathcal U}\left[\Re V_{i,k},\Im V_{i,k}\right]\in\mathbb R^{62}. \label{eq:hidden-target}
\end{equation}
The target is therefore narrower than the 114 differential coordinates of the reduced dynamic model. Success on \eqref{eq:hidden-target} does not imply that all machine, exciter, governor, and load states are observable or accurately reconstructed.

\subsection{Reconstruction and Adequacy Questions}

Given $\bm y_{\mathcal P,0:k}$, the causal reconstruction problem is to obtain $\widehat{\bm z}_{\mathcal U,k}$ and a defensible uncertainty model using no sample after time $k$. The paper asks four questions:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Does a structural functional-observability diagnostic predict which hidden outputs are difficult to reconstruct?
 \item How much do snapshot electrical constraints, causal dynamics, and fixed-lag smoothing each contribute under nominal operation?
 \item Can nominal uncertainty and colored innovations be modeled without damaging hidden-state inference?
 \item When the nonlinear plant changes, is excess error caused by lost information or by stale centering and linearization, and can that inadequacy be detected from observed PMUs?
\end{enumerate}

For trajectory $r$ with $T_r$ frames, the primary total vector error is
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{TVE}^{(r)}=\frac{1}{T_r|\mathcal U|}
 \sum_{k=1}^{T_r}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U}
 \frac{|\widehat V_{i,k}-V_{i,k}|}{\max(|V_{i,k}|,\epsilon)}, \label{eq:tve}
\end{equation}
with TVE reported as a percentage and $\epsilon$ used only as a numerical guard. Magnitude RMSE and wrapped angle RMSE are reported separately. Confidence intervals treat a complete nonlinear trajectory or rebuilt physical plant as the independent unit; PMU frames are not counted as independent replicates.

Physical-event support variables, measurement-integrity states, and external-data transfer belong to the broader Hybrid-DAE program, but they are outside the empirical claims of this paper. This boundary keeps the present results tied to reconstructed voltages, nominal calibration, and physical model mismatch.
