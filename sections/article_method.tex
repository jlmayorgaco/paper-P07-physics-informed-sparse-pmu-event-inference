\section{Attribution Against a Recovering State}
\label{sec:narrative-method}

The method is a multiple-hypothesis filter. Its design choice is which
state and switch history to carry when an asset returns to service.
Suppose a line recloses at time $\kappa$. From that instant the intact
network governs the machine dynamics again, but its initial condition is
the state reached during the outage. Compare this trajectory with the one
from the same operating condition in which the line stayed closed. Let
$\delta x(\kappa)$ be their state difference at reclosure. To first order,
the difference between their electrical outputs at time $t>\kappa$ is
\begin{equation}
 \delta y^{\rm el}(t)\simeq C_0\Phi_0(t,\kappa)\,\delta x(\kappa),
 \label{eq:recovery-response}
\end{equation}
where $\Phi_0$ propagates the intact dynamics and $C_0$ maps the state
to the measured phasors. A restored load leaves the same kind of initial
condition. The monitor must infer $\delta x(\kappa)$ from earlier reports.
At the next suspected onset it compares two continuations of that inferred
state. One leaves the network alone. The other applies a candidate load
change or line opening. Their predictions can differ even at PMUs remote
from the candidate asset.

\subsection{From a Network Intervention to a PMU Prediction}

For each topology $q$, hold the prefix-fitted operating point
$(\bar x,\bar u)$ fixed and solve the algebraic constraints
$g_q(\bar x,\bar z_q,\bar u)=0$. Let $H_q$ return bus voltages and
oriented terminal currents. With a fixed angle reference and nonsingular
$g_z$, implicit differentiation gives the state and output sensitivities
by eliminating the algebraic deviations,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{bmatrix} A_q & B_q \\ C_q & D_q \end{bmatrix}
 =
 \begin{bmatrix} f_x & f_u \\ H_x & H_u \end{bmatrix}
 -\begin{bmatrix}f_z\\H_z\end{bmatrix}
    g_z^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}g_x & g_u\end{bmatrix}.
 \label{eq:narrative-reduction}
\end{equation}
All derivatives use the consistent point for $q$. A load change scales
one bus's active and reactive setpoints together. A line opening removes
its branch contribution from the network admittance matrix. None of the
nine candidate lines carries a measured terminal current, so an opening
changes the eight measured currents through the network voltages. The
affine predictor includes both the post-switch drift
$f_q(\bar x,\bar z_q,\bar u)$ and the change of electrical reference
$H_q(\bar x,\bar z_q,\bar u)-H_0(\bar x,\bar z_0,\bar u)$.

The first 300 reports set the operating factors, reference and noise scale.
We fix the resulting offsets during monitoring so a sustained load
change remains visible. Integrating the affine dynamics across a report
interval, including any switch within it, and appending the causal
instrument model gives the one-report equations
\begin{align}
 r_k &= J\xi_k+j+v a+\epsilon_k,\nonumber\\
 \xi_{k+1} &= T\xi_k+g+u a+w_k .
 \label{eq:implemented-predictor}
\end{align}
Here $r_k$ is the centered, scaled and instrument-whitened report.
The augmented state $\xi_k$ contains the physical deviations, phase
and filter memory, and an autoregressive discrepancy term. The
operators $(T,J,g,u,j,v)$ depend on the proposed switch and its
position within the report. The local Gaussian model takes the residual
observation noise $\epsilon_k$ and process noise $w_k$ to be zero-mean,
independent of one another and of the preceding state. The scalar $a$ is
a load's fractional change. A line uses the open or closed branch operator.

\subsection{One Update for Severity, State and History}

For a load history $h$ with severity interval $I$, condition on its
asset, switch times and the reports
preceding $r_k$. For a fixed severity $a$, the local filter represents
the state by a Gaussian with mean $m_h+d_h a$ and covariance $P_\rho$.
Here $\rho$ selects a quiet, load, line or recovery discrepancy regime.
The bank uses a nominal steady-state covariance within each regime.
This approximation permits different histories to share a gain for the
same report operator. With observation noise covariance $R$, the
innovation for severity $a$ is $e-sa$, where
\begin{align}
 e&=r_k-Jm_h-j, & s&=Jd_h+v,\nonumber\\
 S&=JP_\rho J^\top+R, & K&=TP_\rho J^\top S^{-1}.
 \label{eq:innovation}
\end{align}
The factor $T$ in $K$ appears because the target is the next state:
at fixed $a$, its cross-covariance with the present report is
$TP_\rho J^\top$. The vector $s$ is the predicted change in this report per unit severity,
after conditioning on the previous reports
\cite{friedland1969bias,willsky1976glr}.

Write the prior density of $a$ as a Gaussian of center $\mu$ and
variance parameter $v_a$, truncated to $I$. Completing the square in
$\mathcal N(e;sa,S)\mathcal N(a;\mu,v_a)$ gives
\begin{align}
 v_a'&=(v_a^{-1}+s^\top S^{-1}s)^{-1},\nonumber\\
 \mu'&=v_a'(\mu/v_a+s^\top S^{-1}e).
 \label{eq:severity-update}
\end{align}
Let $Z_I(\mu,v_a)$ be the probability that an untruncated draw lies in
$I$. Integrating the product over $I$ yields the evidence assigned to
this history by one report,
\begin{equation}
 L_{h,k}=\mathcal N(e;s\mu,S+v_a ss^\top)
           \frac{Z_I(\mu',v_a')}{Z_I(\mu,v_a)}.
 \label{eq:narrative-evidence}
\end{equation}
The first factor is the ordinary Gaussian predictive density. The ratio
accounts for the admitted load range. The posterior severity law is the
Gaussian with parameters $(\mu',v_a')$ truncated to $I$. Its mean
$\bar a_h$ minimizes conditional squared error in severity.
In particular, $\bar a_h=\mu'+v_a'\,\partial_{\mu'}\log Z_I(\mu',v_a')$:
differentiating the interval probability accounts for the displacement
of the mean near either bound. For a line
the evidence reduces to $\mathcal N(e;0,S)$.

Applying the gain to $e-sa$ and collecting the constant and linear
terms in $a$ gives the next conditional state,
\begin{align}
 m_h^+ &= Tm_h+g+Ke,\nonumber\\
 d_h^+ &= Td_h+u-Ks,\qquad
 \widehat{\xi}_{h,k+1}=m_h^++d_h^+\bar a_h .
 \label{eq:implemented-conditional-correction}
\end{align}
To see which error this estimate minimizes, condition on $h$ and all
reports through $k$ under the local Gaussian model with its assigned
$P_\rho$. Write $E_h$ for that expectation and $X=\xi_{k+1}$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:implemented-conditional-correction} gives
$\widehat{\xi}_{h,k+1}=E_h X$ by averaging first at fixed severity.
For any candidate estimate $z$, the centered error has zero mean, so
\[
 E_h\|X-z\|^2=E_h\|X-E_hX\|^2+\|z-E_hX\|^2 .
\]
The second term is the excess risk of choosing $z$. A load's birth prior has standard
deviation 0.15 before truncation to $[-0.2,0.2]$. The bank partitions
that interval into four histories weighted by their prior masses. Their
evidence sums to the full bounded-prior evidence for a given path while
all four remain in the bank.

\subsection{Which Histories Compete at the Next Report?}

At each report the bank can continue an existing history, restore an
eligible intervention or start a new one from the no-intervention
reference. Let $p(h\mid h')$ be the transition weight from parent $h'$.
Before pruning, the one-step update for admitted paths is
\begin{equation}
 \pi_{h,k}\ \propto\ L_{h,k}
      \sum_{h'}p(h\mid h')\,\pi_{h',k-1}.
 \label{eq:history-bayes-update}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:history-bayes-update} is the usual multiple-hypothesis
weight update \cite{boers2005m3h}. The application-specific choice is the
pair of predictions it compares after a switch has been reversed. A
restored path predicts the PMU report with the intact network driven by
the surviving machine and instrument states. A rival applies a candidate
asset switch during that recovery. The PMU likelihood scores both
continuations, even when the original asset is already back in service.
To propose a recent onset, the bank
screens the last 0--18 report lags, retrieves the stored state preceding
the candidate switch and replays the intervening reports. Its score
replaces the old suffix likelihood. The same reports therefore contribute
once to each path. The bank prunes low-weight histories and renormalizes
the survivors. Averaging $\widehat{\xi}_{h,k+1}$ with these normalized
weights gives the minimum-mean-square-error state estimate for the retained
mixture. Its accuracy as an approximation to the full posterior depends on
the local covariance model and the histories surviving the search.
The source decision uses the history masses below and a calibrated
publication threshold.
The supplement gives the transition hazards, half-report
switch phases, storage limits and pruning rule.

For source $c$, the two relevant sums of history weights are
\begin{align}
 M_{c,k}&=\sum_{h:c(h)=c}\pi_{h,k}\,\mathbf 1\{h\text{ active}\},\nonumber\\
 \Gamma_{c,k}&=\sum_{h:c(h)=c}\pi_{h,k}.
 \label{eq:active-cause-masses}
\end{align}
Thus $\Gamma_{c,k}-M_{c,k}$ is the support assigned to restored paths
of source $c$. A load can have little active support and still account
for ongoing recovery. These sums describe the histories retained at
report $k$.

The primary comparisons use one nominal network. A secondary version
runs this recursion for five fixed resistance/reactance hypotheses
(nominal, global $\pm2\%$, and selected branches $\pm3\%$). Its outer
weights multiply the previous network weight by the one-step predictive
density summed over forward transitions. It averages the source masses
over networks after normalization. Retrospective onset proposals revise
only the within-network weights. Linearization, shared covariances,
screening and pruning make the resulting masses approximate.

\subsection{From Source Evidence to an Alarm}

All methods in the two primary comparisons use the same alarm rule.
The bank scores onset using the log odds of active source mass and
restoration using the log odds of restored source mass. It raises an
alarm and names the asset once the same source leads above threshold
for six consecutive reports. Six qualifying restoration reports for that
source close the episode. We calibrate thresholds on separate sequences
(Section~\ref{sec:narrative-protocol}). When scoring timeliness, we use
the time the monitor names the asset, even if it estimates an earlier onset.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Attributing an intervention as reports arrive}
\label{alg:physical-history}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require PMU reports, network operators and catalog of admissible sources
\State Fit the reference and noise scale on the event-free prefix.
\For{each subsequent report}
 \State Extend paths by continuation or an admissible switch.
 \State Score the report by \eqref{eq:narrative-evidence} or its line likelihood.
 \State Correct each conditional state using \eqref{eq:implemented-conditional-correction}.
 \State Replay selected recent onsets from their stored pre-switch states, replacing their earlier suffix scores.
 \State Normalize the weights, prune and sum the source masses.
 \State Check onset and restoration scores against the six-report rule.
 \If{the monitor declares restoration}
   \State Keep the recovery paths (or merge them in the comparison procedures).
 \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

After declaring restoration, the history bank retains the paths and the
states saved for the onset search. We compare this rule with two ways of merging paths
in Section~\ref{sec:narrative-protocol}. The five-network sensitivity
study uses a different alarm rule with an additional check for load events
and a separate time for reporting the diagnosis (see the supplement).
