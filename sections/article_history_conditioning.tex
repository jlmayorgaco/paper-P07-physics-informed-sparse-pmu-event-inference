\subsection{History Conditioning in an Independent Reduced Plant}
\label{sec:history-conditioning}
A proposed extension conditions the evidence on the state left by a previous intervention. It relinearizes the signature of the new source at that state, adds the bilinear interaction between the two interventions, or integrates the uncertain previous severity out of the likelihood. We tested it where it should matter most, with a plant different from the one used for the bank. It has classical machines, a 5\% droop governor, constant-impedance loads and the network of the competition case. A load $p$ of 8--16\% of its base value is switched on at 2\,s and off at 5\,s; a weak second load of 2--6\% appears at 6.5\,s while the network is still recovering. The estimator knows $p$ and the switching times, fits the previous severity by Gauss--Newton and ranks the new source among the eight loads of the catalog. Observation noise is the empirical covariance of the first differences of the competition record, multiplied by 1, 10 or 100. Some cells shorten the history available to the estimator or detune its inertia and damping by 20\% and 40\%. The protocol, the comparison criterion and the seeds were fixed before the test scenarios were generated, and each cell was run once with \HHScenarios{} scenarios.

Table~\ref{tab:history-conditioning} gives the outcome. In none of the \HHCells{} protocol cells does a predictor conditioned on the history make a significantly better source decision than the recentered nominal signature; the accuracies differ by at most \HHBestGain{} points. The mechanism is visible in the estimates. The previous severity is recovered to within \HHMaxAErr{} points of the base load in every protocol cell, so the history leaves no uncertainty to marginalize. We then looked for the regime where conditioning should finally pay off. Exploratory runs on separate seeds pushed the previous severity to 30--60\%, started the new source 0.2\,s after the previous one ended, detuned inertia and damping by 40\% and 80\%, and cut the history to 0.5--1\,s with noise 100 to 1000 times the measured level. Even there the six predictors tied. We fixed two of those regimes (cells I and J) and ran them once on fresh seeds. The previous severity is then recovered only to \HHExtAErrLo{}--\HHExtAErrHi{} points, yet the decisions still do not change: the accuracies are equal in cell J and within one point in cell I, where integrating out the previous severity is even worse than the plug-in (by \HHWorstGain{} point of accuracy and \HHExtNLL{} nats of log-loss). The reason is structural. The state left by the previous intervention shifts the predicted record of every candidate source by almost the same amount, so it moves all hypotheses together and rarely reorders them; the ranking is set by the shape of the new source's own signature. The nominal signature costs \HHNominalMs{} ms per decision, against \HHPlantMs{} ms for the nonlinear plant and \HHQuadMs{} s for the quadrature reference. A computable bound on the whitened prediction error certified the decisions of the nominal, bilinear and relinearized predictors in \HHCertMin--\HHCertMax\% of the scenarios. Among the certified decisions, \HHCertFlips{} differed from the nonlinear plant. The bound needs the nonlinear prediction it bounds, so it validates the approximation and does not save computation.
