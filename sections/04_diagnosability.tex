\section{Diagnosability and Physical Evidence}
\label{sec:diagnosability}

\subsection{Projected Dynamic Signatures}

Physical hypotheses become distinguishable only through their PMU-projected dynamic consequences. For candidate $H_j$, let $\widehat{\bm{x}}_{j,k}$ and $\widehat{\bm{z}}_{j,k}$ be the state trajectory predicted from the same pre-event context, intervention onset, and candidate parameters. Its signature at the PMUs available at $k$ is
\begin{equation}
 \bm{s}_{j,k}^{\Pset_k}=\operatorname{col}_{p\in\Pset_k}\!\left[\mathcal{M}_{p,\widehat m_{p,k}}\!\left(\bm{h}_p(\widehat{\bm{x}}_{j,k},\widehat{\bm{z}}_{j,k})\right)\right]. \label{eq:projected-signature}
\end{equation}
The prediction may be generated by a nonlinear simulation, local DAE linearization, moving-horizon estimator, or another model whose approximation error is recorded. A useful local event signature is the change from the no-event prediction, $\bm{d}_{j,k}^{\Pset_k}=\bm{s}_{j,k}^{\Pset_k}-\bm{s}_{0,k}^{\Pset_k}$. Unlike a feature vector learned from a fixed sensor order, this signature is indexed by the PMUs actually contributing at $k$.

The observed innovation has the corresponding form
\begin{equation}
 \bm{r}_{j,k}^{\Pset_k}=\bm{y}_{\Pset_k,k}-\bm{s}_{j,k}^{\Pset_k}. \label{eq:physical-innovation}
\end{equation}
Small residuals alone do not prove that a candidate is uniquely identified. Two distinct intervention hypotheses can both fit the data if their projected signatures are close on the currently observed horizon.

\subsection{Dynamic Pairwise Information}

To state this limitation explicitly, consider two physical hypotheses $H_i$ and $H_j$ over an interval $\mathcal{T}$. We use the local approximation
\begin{equation}
 \bm{y}_{\Pset_k,k}\given H_j \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(\bm{s}_{j,k}^{\Pset_k},\bm{R}_k^{\Pset_k}\right), \qquad k\in\mathcal{T}, \label{eq:gaussian-observation}
\end{equation}
where the candidate-dependent nonlinear trajectory is treated as fixed, the observation covariance is common across the pair, and temporal residuals are conditionally independent after the stated history and integrity conditioning. The covariance can include calibrated measurement noise and an explicitly estimated model-discrepancy component. This is a local Gaussian model, not a claim that all PMU disturbances are Gaussian or independent.

Under these assumptions, define the PMU-projected pairwise information accumulated through $T$ as
\begin{equation}
 D_{ij}^{\Pset}(T)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{T}(T)}\!\left(\bm{s}_{i,k}^{\Pset_k}-\bm{s}_{j,k}^{\Pset_k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\!\left(\bm{R}_k^{\Pset_k}\right)^{-1}\!\left(\bm{s}_{i,k}^{\Pset_k}-\bm{s}_{j,k}^{\Pset_k}\right). \label{eq:pairwise-information}
\end{equation}
For the Gaussian product approximation, \eqref{eq:pairwise-information} equals the accumulated Kullback--Leibler divergence between the two observation distributions. Its role here is operational: it reflects the intervention signatures and the PMUs that are available at each time, rather than serving as a generic information-theoretic novelty claim.

\begin{proposition}[Local observational indistinguishability]
\label{prop:indistinguishability}
Under \eqref{eq:gaussian-observation}, suppose that $\bm{R}_k^{\Pset_k}$ is positive definite and $\bm{s}_{i,k}^{\Pset_k}=\bm{s}_{j,k}^{\Pset_k}$ for every $k\in\mathcal{T}(T)$. Then $D_{ij}^{\Pset}(T)=0$, and $H_i$ and $H_j$ induce identical observation distributions over the horizon under this approximation.
\end{proposition}

\IEEEproof
The signature equality makes every quadratic term in \eqref{eq:pairwise-information} zero, so $D_{ij}^{\Pset}(T)=0$. The two Gaussian factors in \eqref{eq:gaussian-observation} then have the same mean and covariance at every $k$. Conditional independence yields identical products over the horizon. Thus no decision rule based only on these modeled observations can distinguish the pair under the stated approximation. \endIEEEproof

The proposition is deliberately local. In practice, imperfect parameters, an unmodeled integrity failure, or a longer horizon can break the equality. These possibilities justify recording model discrepancy and observing how $D_{ij}^{\Pset}(T)$ evolves rather than interpreting a small value as a universal impossibility result.

\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity under conditionally independent PMU addition]
\label{prop:monotonicity}
Assume the conditions of \eqref{eq:gaussian-observation}, and let an added PMU $q$ provide a conditionally independent observation with positive-definite covariance $\bm{R}_{q,k}$. Then adding $q$ cannot decrease the pairwise information measure. Conversely, removing $q$ cannot increase it.
\end{proposition}

\IEEEproof
With conditional independence, the augmented covariance is block diagonal. At each $k$, the augmented quadratic form equals the previous term plus
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{s}_{i,q,k}-\bm{s}_{j,q,k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{R}_{q,k}^{-1}\left(\bm{s}_{i,q,k}-\bm{s}_{j,q,k}\right), \label{eq:added-pmu-information}
\end{equation}
which is nonnegative because $\bm{R}_{q,k}^{-1}$ is positive definite. Summing over the horizon proves the addition result. Removing $q$ subtracts the same nonnegative contribution. \endIEEEproof

Proposition~\ref{prop:monotonicity} does not imply that every PMU is equally useful. A PMU whose signature difference is near zero adds little pairwise information even when its measurements are accurate. Campaign~B in Section~\ref{sec:experimental-protocol} therefore contrasts random loss with removal of information-critical PMUs.

\subsection{Candidate Ambiguity and Diagnosability-Aware Decisions}

For a screened candidate set $\widetilde{\Hset}_k$, define a conservative ambiguity neighborhood for $H_i$ by
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{A}_{i,k}(T,\delta)=\left\{H_j\in\widetilde{\Hset}_k: D_{ij}^{\Pset}(T)\leq\delta\right\}. \label{eq:ambiguity-neighborhood}
\end{equation}
The threshold $\delta$ is an operating quantity to be selected on development data and assessed under shift; it is not a universal source-separation constant. The inference layer should retain a candidate set when high-posterior candidates fall within such an ambiguity neighborhood or when posterior mass is diffuse. Exact localization is appropriate only when posterior support, separation, and integrity diagnostics agree.

This treatment turns a PMU join or loss into a testable change in diagnosability. A new PMU can tighten or leave unchanged the candidate set under the approximation; a departing informative PMU may enlarge it. The appropriate validation target is not merely a Top-1 score but the joint behavior of localization error, candidate-set coverage and size, posterior entropy, and time to a resolved source set.
