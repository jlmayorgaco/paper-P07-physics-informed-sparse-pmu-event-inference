\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Sparse-PMU monitoring contains inverse problems with different information limits. Functional observability determines whether the requested hidden-voltage function is recoverable. Sparse event injectivity and candidate-manifold separation determine whether event cardinality and support remain distinguishable over a finite horizon, including after state and integrity nuisance are fitted. A positive structural margin is not the same as a well-conditioned decision.

The experiments show the distinction. The causal PowerDynamics estimator attains \BTwoTVE{} mean hidden-voltage HVRE, and physical recentering removes \EZeroHClosureHalf{}--\EZeroHClosureMax{} of the operating-point error gap. In the fresh 137-hypothesis load test, exact-support Top-1 rises from \GlobalWeakTopOne{} for weak--weak pairs to \GlobalFiniteTopOne{} for finite pairs; weak cardinality can be resolved before source identity. Retrospective profiled distance explains weak-case recovery, while the T120 diagnostic finds positive but nearly collinear equilibrium signatures. The separate ANDES model still yields only \HeldSourceCorrect{} correct decisions among \HeldSourceDecisions{} after complete physical-source holdout.

Physical candidate responses now have direct evidence for simultaneous interventions in one matched event family. Independent-operating-point horizon validation must precede extension to faults, outages, generation changes, integrity events, changing PMU sets, and simulator shift. The central consequence is practical: state error, event cardinality, exact support, and source diagnosability must be measured separately before a joint decision is trusted.
