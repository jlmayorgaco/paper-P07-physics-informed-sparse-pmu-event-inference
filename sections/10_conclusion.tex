\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper formulated sparse-PMU monitoring as inference of a hidden voltage field, physical event, physical source, and measurement-integrity state. Functional observability characterizes recoverability of the requested state function; affine-manifold separation gives a local diagnosability limit after state and integrity nuisance are profiled.

The consolidated evidence identifies both capability and boundary. On 100 frozen PowerDynamics trajectories, the causal estimator attains \BTwoTVE{} mean hidden-voltage HVRE, and regularized physical recentering closes \EZeroHClosureHalf{}--\EZeroHClosureMax{} of the operating-point error gap on 60 held-out plants despite nuisance rank \EZeroHRank{}/\EZeroHDimension{}. On 690 separate ANDES trajectories, the known-source event baseline reaches detection F1 \LegacyEventDetectionFOne{}, event macro-F1 \LegacyEventMacroFOne{}, and physical Top-1 \LegacyPhysicalTopOne{}. Yet complete source holdout yields only \HeldSourceCorrect{}/\HeldSourceDecisions{} exact decisions, and transfer to RAW0001 is weak.

The central consequence is not a claim of finished joint superiority. Sparse PMUs can support accurate target-state reconstruction and strong known-source detection while still failing to identify an unseen physical source. Source-generalizable diagnosis requires physical candidate responses, an explicit integrity model, and diagnosability-aware abstention. A common-contract end-to-end campaign must establish that proposed capability.
