\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper studied reconstruction of 31 unobserved bus voltages from eight voltage/current PMUs as a functional-output problem. On 100 frozen nonlinear nominal trajectories, physical snapshot conditioning reduced mean TVE from \BZeroTVE{} to \BOneTVE{}, and causal propagation further reduced it to \BTwoTVE{}. A preregistered functional residual predicted bus-level difficulty with Spearman $\rho=\FunctionalSpearman$, while positive fixed lags and low-rank colored-residual variants did not improve the primary target.

Physically rebuilt plants exposed the main boundary: network and operating-point mismatch increased median TVE by \NetworkMismatchRatio{}$\times$ and \OperatingMismatchRatio{}$\times$ at the largest tested scale. Observed innovations detected much of this inadequacy, and oracle physical recentering recovered the lost accuracy, but the tested causal offset proxy closed only \OnlineClosurePercent{} of the oracle gap. The evidence therefore motivates a specific next step---leakage-safe nonlinear physical recentering and Jacobian rebuilding---rather than a generic increase in estimator complexity. Event inference, source localization, and external transfer remain separate, unestablished extensions.
