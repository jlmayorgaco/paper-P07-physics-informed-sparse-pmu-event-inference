\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Sparse-PMU monitoring contains two inverse problems with different information limits. Functional observability determines whether the requested hidden-voltage function is recoverable and gives the covariance of its minimum-variance linear estimate. Candidate-manifold separation determines whether two event sources remain distinguishable after state and integrity nuisance are fitted. A bounded DAE remainder connects that linear margin to a local nonlinear certificate.

The experiments show the distinction. The causal PowerDynamics estimator attains \BTwoTVE{} mean hidden-voltage HVRE, and physical recentering removes \EZeroHClosureHalf{}--\EZeroHClosureMax{} of the operating-point error gap. The separate ANDES model detects known-source events, yet complete physical-source holdout yields only \HeldSourceCorrect{} correct decisions among \HeldSourceDecisions{}. Accurate state reconstruction and event recognition therefore coexist with failed source recovery.

Physical candidate responses, an explicit integrity state, and abstention are necessary components of the next source-generalization test. Their value must be established under one source-disjoint experimental protocol. These results define that test and the conditions under which its source decisions can be trusted.
