\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper formulated sparse-PMU monitoring as joint inference of a hidden voltage field, physical event, physical source, and measurement-integrity state. Functional observability characterizes recoverability of the requested state function; projected pairwise information gives a local limit on source separation after initial-state nuisance is removed.

The consolidated evidence identifies both capability and boundary. On 100 frozen PowerDynamics trajectories, the causal estimator attains \BTwoTVE{} mean hidden-bus TVE, and physical static MAP closes \EZeroHClosureHalf{}--\EZeroHClosureMax{} of the operating-point error gap on 60 held-out plants despite nuisance rank \EZeroHRank{}/\EZeroHDimension{}. On 690 separate ANDES trajectories, the known-source event baseline reaches detection F1 \LegacyEventDetectionFOne{}, event macro-F1 \LegacyEventMacroFOne{}, and physical Top-1 \LegacyPhysicalTopOne{}. Yet complete source holdout yields only \HeldSourceCorrect{}/\HeldSourceDecisions{} exact decisions, and transfer to RAW0001 is weak.

The central consequence is therefore not a claim of finished joint superiority. Sparse PMUs can support accurate target-state reconstruction and strong known-source detection, but source-generalizable diagnosis requires physical candidate responses, an explicit integrity model, and diagnosability-aware abstention. A common-contract end-to-end campaign must establish that result before the title's full joint-inference claim is suitable for submission.
