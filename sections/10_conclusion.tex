\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Sparse-PMU monitoring contains two inverse problems with different information limits. Functional observability determines whether the requested hidden-voltage function is recoverable and gives the covariance of its minimum-variance linear estimate. Candidate-manifold separation determines whether two event sources remain distinguishable after state and integrity nuisance are fitted. A bounded DAE remainder connects that linear margin to a local nonlinear certificate.

The experiments show the distinction. The causal PowerDynamics estimator attains \BTwoTVE{} mean hidden-voltage HVRE, and physical recentering removes \EZeroHClosureHalf{}--\EZeroHClosureMax{} of the operating-point error gap. In the executed 16-source load case, amplitude Fisher information is associated with a discretized 90\%-detection threshold, while source Top-1 falls from 100\% at 0.4\% magnitude to 13.0\% at 0.01\%. The separate ANDES model detects known-source events, yet complete physical-source holdout yields only \HeldSourceCorrect{} correct decisions among \HeldSourceDecisions{}.

Physical candidate responses now have direct evidence for one matched event family. Extending them to faults, outages, generation changes, integrity events, changing PMU sets, and simulator shift remains the next source-disjoint test. The central consequence is narrower: state recoverability, event detectability, and source diagnosability must be measured separately before any joint decision is trusted.
