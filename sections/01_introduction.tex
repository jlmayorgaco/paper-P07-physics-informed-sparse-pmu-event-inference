\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\PARstart{P}{hasor} measurement units (PMUs) reveal electromechanical transients through synchronized voltage and current phasors, yet transmission networks instrument only a fraction of their buses and branches \cite{phadke2008synchronized,zhao2019dse}. A disturbance may begin outside the measured region, alter hidden voltages, and propagate to the available devices. Missing frames and corrupted channels create a second ambiguity because they can resemble physical transients. Diagnosis must therefore separate three questions: what is the network state, which event occurred, and where did each applicable physical or measurement event originate?

Dynamic estimators combine network models with synchrophasors \cite{zhao2019dse,katanic2024recursive}. Sparse-PMU classifiers identify events and faulted assets \cite{pandey2020realtime,yildiz2024few}. Other learning methods reconstruct hidden trajectories before localization or estimate state and attack location from partial observations \cite{rimkus2025gnn,zhai2025partial}. These methods solve different inverse problems. The unresolved case considered here has an under-observed state, concurrent physical and measurement-integrity changes, source assets absent from labeled training, and sensing geometries that may render two sources indistinguishable.

The distinction is operational, not semantic. A generation change can be visible throughout the network while its source remains ambiguous. A dropped PMU identifies an integrity location directly but removes information needed to localize a simultaneous physical event. Likewise, a state estimate can have small point error while the intervention that produced it is not uniquely identifiable. A single multiclass label conceals these differences and produces accuracy values whose denominators do not match the claimed task.

A physical candidate contains an event family, a sparse source support, onset, and signed severity. Measurement availability and corruption remain separate variables. Each candidate generates a counterfactual DAE response, while candidate comparison profiles admissible state and integrity nuisance directions. Specializing descriptor-model distinguishability and matched-subspace ideas \cite{eriksson2013diagnosability,scharf1994matched}, the remaining weighted distance measures local source separation. A zero distance identifies an intersecting pair of response manifolds; a positive but small distance identifies a decision that is structurally distinct yet noise and horizon limited.

Three frozen contracts supply complementary evidence. The PowerDynamics state study reconstructs 31 hidden complex bus voltages from eight voltage/current PMUs and tests operating-point mismatch. The principal event study evaluates \GlobalHypothesisCount{} load-intervention hypotheses---$H_0$, 16 single buses, and 120 simultaneous pairs---using a quadratic physical response manifold and normalized Bayesian evidence, without a fitted source-label head. A retrospective weak-regime audit and a longer-horizon diagnostic probe why support decisions fail. The separate ANDES study preserves the earlier physics-guided hierarchical baseline on eight abnormal labels plus normal operation. The simulators, targets, and splits remain separate; their metrics are never pooled.

The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a hybrid DAE problem formulation that maps eight historical labels to composable physical-intervention and measurement-integrity variables, while representing simultaneous load sources by a sparse support rather than a new class label;
  \item a finite-horizon analysis that separates target-state estimability from sparse event-support injectivity, nuisance-profiled source separation, and the conditioning gained from additional measurements or observation time; and
  \item a fresh \GlobalHypothesisCount{}-hypothesis Bayesian confirmation over all zero-, one-, and two-source load supports, together with frozen state-reconstruction, operating-point recentering, weak-regime geometry, and source-holdout evidence.
\end{enumerate}

The empirical boundary is sharp. The state estimator reaches \BTwoTVE{} mean hidden-voltage HVRE, while physical recentering closes \EZeroHClosureHalf{}--\EZeroHClosureMax{} of the operating-point error gap. In the prospective simultaneous-load test, exact support Top-1 rises from \GlobalWeakTopOne{} for weak--weak events to \GlobalFiniteTopOne{} for finite events; weak cardinality is often resolved before exact support. The retrospective profiled distance predicts weak-case exact recovery far better than a support-only score. A 120-frame diagnostic finds no equilibrium-degenerate source pair in the frozen local model, but near-collinearity and incomplete independent validation prevent a general no-ambiguity claim. Physical candidate inference is therefore supported for matched load interventions with at most two sources, not for the full eight-family joint problem.
