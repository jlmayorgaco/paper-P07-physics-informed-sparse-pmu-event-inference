\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\PARstart{P}{hasor} measurement units (PMUs) provide synchronized voltage and current phasors at rates that expose electromechanical dynamics, but practical deployments instrument only part of a transmission network \cite{phadke2008synchronized,zhao2019dse}. This creates a target-specific inference problem. A control center may not need every machine and controller state to answer an immediate monitoring question; it may instead need the voltage field at uninstrumented buses. Whether that field can be reconstructed depends on how the available phasors constrain the requested output, not only on whether the complete internal state is observable.

Dynamic state-estimation research has developed Kalman, robust, and nonlinear DAE estimators for power systems \cite{kalman1960,zhao2019dse,katanic2024recursive}. These methods describe how to combine a dynamic model with measurements, but their nominal performance does not determine what happens when the estimator carries stale network parameters or an outdated operating point. Sparse sensing and model mismatch interact: a measurement set can retain information about the desired output while a locally linear estimator fails because it is centered around the wrong physical equilibrium. Conversely, adding estimator complexity cannot repair an incorrect measurement operator or an inconsistent benchmark.

Functional observability provides the appropriate structural language for this distinction. It asks whether a specified function of the state can be inferred from an output history even when the full state is not observable \cite{fernando2010functional,zhang2025functional}. For sparse-PMU reconstruction, the target is the complex voltage at unobserved buses. The structural question must then be connected to empirical error, uncertainty calibration, and physical model adequacy. These connections are rarely evaluated together under a provenance-controlled nonlinear benchmark.

This paper studies an IEEE 39-bus dynamic system with PMUs at buses $\{2,5,6,10,19,22,29,39\}$. The remaining 31 complex bus voltages are reconstructed from voltage and controlled terminal-current phasors. The benchmark is implemented in PowerDynamics.jl \cite{plietzsch2022powerdynamics} and is treated as a numerically audited pedagogical plant, not as an experimentally established New England grid model. Static network parity, the PMU operator, the dynamic equilibrium, small-signal behavior, nonlinear trajectories, and first-order linearization accuracy are validated before estimator scoring.

The paper makes four contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item It formulates sparse-PMU voltage recovery as a functional-output problem and connects a preregistered structural residual to bus-level reconstruction error. The distinction prevents partial rank of the 114-state model from being misinterpreted as failure to recover the 62-dimensional hidden-voltage output.
  \item It establishes a validation and anti-inverse-crime chain for the dynamic IEEE 39-bus benchmark, including terminal-current semantics, disjoint calibration/test trajectories, physically rebuilt mismatch plants, and explicit rejection accounting.
  \item It compares an equilibrium baseline, snapshot weighted least squares (WLS), a causal Kalman estimator, and exact fixed-lag Gaussian smoothing. It also evaluates independent covariance calibration and two low-rank discrepancy placements, retaining the negative results on smoothing and residual whitening.
  \item It quantifies network, component, operating-point, and coupled mismatch over 980 main-grid and 175 refinement cases. Oracle and causal recentering ablations separate information loss from stale physical centering, while a nominal-calibration innovation statistic tests whether model inadequacy can be detected from observed PMUs.
\end{enumerate}

The principal result is mechanistic rather than a small estimator ranking. On 100 nominal nonlinear test trajectories, the causal estimator attains \BTwoTVE{} mean hidden-bus TVE, but network and operating-point mismatch increase median TVE by \NetworkMismatchRatio$\times$ and \OperatingMismatchRatio$\times$ at the largest tested scale. Oracle physical recentering removes most of that excess, whereas the tested causal offset-only proxy closes only \OnlineClosurePercent{} of the oracle gap. Thus the current evidence supports functional voltage reconstruction and model-adequacy diagnosis; it does not yet support event localization, external-data transfer, or superiority of a full nonlinear Hybrid-DAE estimator.
