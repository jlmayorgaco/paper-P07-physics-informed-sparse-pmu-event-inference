\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\PARstart{P}{hasor} measurement units (PMUs) reveal electromechanical transients through synchronized voltage and current phasors, yet transmission networks instrument only a fraction of their buses and branches \cite{phadke2008synchronized,zhao2019dse}. A disturbance may begin outside the measured region, alter hidden voltages, and propagate to the available devices. Missing frames and corrupted channels create a second ambiguity because they can resemble physical transients. Diagnosis must therefore separate three questions: what is the network state, which event occurred, and where did each applicable physical or measurement event originate?

Dynamic estimators combine network models with synchrophasors \cite{zhao2019dse,katanic2024recursive}. Sparse-PMU classifiers identify events and faulted assets \cite{pandey2020realtime,yildiz2024few}. Other learning methods reconstruct hidden trajectories before localization or estimate state and attack location from partial observations \cite{rimkus2025gnn,zhai2025partial}. These methods solve different inverse problems. The unresolved case considered here has an under-observed state, concurrent physical and measurement-integrity changes, source assets absent from labeled training, and sensing geometries that may render two sources indistinguishable.

The distinction is operational, not semantic. A generation change can be visible throughout the network while its source remains ambiguous. A dropped PMU identifies an integrity location directly but removes information needed to localize a simultaneous physical event. Likewise, a state estimate can have small point error while the intervention that produced it is not uniquely identifiable. A single multiclass label conceals these differences and produces accuracy values whose denominators do not match the claimed task.

A physical candidate is represented by $h=(c,\ell,\tau,\gamma)$, containing the event family, source, onset, and magnitude. Measurement availability and corruption remain separate variables. Each candidate generates a counterfactual DAE response, while candidate comparison fits admissible initial-state and integrity nuisance directions. Specializing established distinguishability and matched-subspace ideas \cite{eriksson2013diagnosability,scharf1994matched}, the remaining weighted distance measures local source separation. A zero distance identifies a pair for which a uniformly correct source decision is impossible under the declared local model.

Three frozen contracts supply complementary evidence. The PowerDynamics state study reconstructs 31 hidden complex bus voltages from eight voltage/current PMUs and tests operating-point mismatch. A second PowerDynamics contract uses candidate-specific nonlinear load interventions to infer event presence, amplitude, and one of 16 source buses without a fitted source-label head. The ANDES study evaluates a physics-guided hierarchical ExtraTrees baseline on eight abnormal labels plus normal operation. The two simulators have different semantics, state coordinates, and data splits; their metrics are never pooled.

The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a hybrid DAE inference model that maps eight event labels to composable physical-intervention and measurement-integrity variables;
  \item a finite-horizon analysis that separates target-state estimability from event-source distinguishability and characterizes first- versus second-order evidence relative to admissible nuisance; and
  \item frozen tests of hidden-voltage reconstruction, operating-point recentering, and Bayesian load-source inference, accompanied by a complete-source-holdout audit of the earlier supervised hierarchy.
\end{enumerate}

The empirical boundary is sharp. The state estimator reaches \BTwoTVE{} mean hidden-voltage HVRE, while physical recentering closes \EZeroHClosureHalf{}--\EZeroHClosureMax{} of the operating-point error gap. In the load-intervention study, the physical likelihood resolves all sources at 0.4\% magnitude but only 13.0\% at 0.01\%. The supervised event model detects and classifies known-source disturbances, yet complete source holdout leaves \HeldSourceCorrect{} correct decisions among \HeldSourceDecisions{}. Physical candidate inference is therefore supported for one matched event family, not for the full eight-family joint problem.
