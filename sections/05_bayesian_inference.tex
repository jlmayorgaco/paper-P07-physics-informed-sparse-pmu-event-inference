\section{Physics-Based State Estimation and Joint Inference}
\label{sec:estimation}

\subsection{Exact Target and a Generative Candidate Model}

Let $\bm\zeta_k=\bm L(\bm x_k,\bm z_k)$ be the electrical quantity requested by the application. Under squared loss, the exact Bayesian target is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\bm\zeta}^{\rm MMSE}_k=\mathbb E[\bm\zeta_k\mid\bm y_{0:k}],
 \qquad \widehat h_k^{\rm MAP}=\argmax_{h\in\mathcal H}p(h\mid\bm y_{0:k}). \label{eq:exact-bayes-target}
\end{equation}
This statement is an optimality definition, not an implemented-algorithm claim.

For candidate $h=(c,\ell,\tau,\gamma,\bm d)$, assume $\bm x_0\sim\mathcal N(\overline{\bm x}_0,\bm P_0)$, Gaussian process and measurement noise, the hard constraint $\bm g_h(\bm x_t,\bm z_t)=\bm0$, and a Laplace prior on active corruption coefficients. With
\begin{equation}
 \bm r_{h,t}=\bm M_t[\bm y_t-\bm h_{\mathcal P_t}(\bm x_t,\bm z_t)-\bm b_t], \label{eq:hypothesis-residual}
\end{equation}
the negative twice-log joint density conditional on $h$, up to constants and the declared corruption-support prior, is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal J_h^{\circ}={}&\|\bm x_0-\overline{\bm x}_0\|_{\bm P_0^{-1}}^2
 +\sum_{t=0}^{k}\|\bm r_{h,t}\|_{\bm R_t^{-1}}^2\\
 &+\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\|\bm x_{t+1}-\bm F_h(\bm x_t,\bm z_t)\|_{\bm Q^{-1}}^2
 +2\lambda_b\sum_{t=0}^{k}\|\bm b_t\|_1,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:joint-map-objective}
\end{equation}
subject to the DAE constraints. Crucially, $\mathcal J_h^{\circ}$ does not contain the hypothesis prior. The physical family and source enter through $\bm F_h$, $\bm g_h$, and the network/measurement operators rather than as an asset-label head.

The evidence is the constrained integral
\begin{equation}
 p(\bm y_{0:k}\mid h)=\int_{\bm g_h=\bm0}
 \exp[-\mathcal J_h^{\circ}/2]\,d\bm X\,d\bm Z\,d\bm B. \label{eq:hypothesis-evidence}
\end{equation}
A Laplace approximation on locally identifiable directions gives
\begin{equation}
 p(h\mid\bm y_{0:k})\propto p(h)\,\det{}^{+}(\bm H_h)^{-1/2}
 \exp[-\mathcal J_h^{\circ}(\widehat{\bm X}_h,\widehat{\bm Z}_h,\widehat{\bm B}_h)/2], \label{eq:hypothesis-posterior}
\end{equation}
so the prior appears exactly once. The pseudo-determinant requires the active constraint and corruption support to be fixed locally. If a Huber loss, a soft DAE penalty, or an omitted determinant is used without a corresponding normalized density, the result is a penalized evidence score, not a posterior probability.

The learned component is deliberately restricted. The earlier engineered feature system can propose a candidate set or learn a correction $\psi_{\phi}(\bm r_{h,0:k})$ to physical log evidence. It must consume candidate residuals, not source identity alone, and must be tested on held-out physical sources. The required ablation is learned-only, physics-only, physics plus learned discrepancy, and the complete diagnosability-aware rule.

\subsection{Nominal Estimator Hierarchy}

The estimator sequence changes one information source at a time. B0 retains only the equilibrium. B1 adds instantaneous PMU conditioning, B2 adds causal state propagation, and B3 tests retrospective temporal information. When the equilibrium becomes stale, E06-H estimates a physically admissible operating point through regularized nonlinear recentering. The full nonlinear Hybrid-DAE fixed-lag posterior is not evaluated in the experiments.

All nominal estimators use the same equilibrium $\bm y_0$, hidden-voltage center $\bm z_{\mathcal U,0}$, sampled dynamics $\bm A_d$, PMU map $\bm C$, and hidden-output map $\bm L_{\mathcal U}$. This shared model isolates the value of measurement updating and temporal propagation.

The equilibrium baseline B0 returns
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\bm z}^{\mathrm{B0}}_{\mathcal U,k}=\bm z_{\mathcal U,0}. \label{eq:b0}
\end{equation}
It measures how much of the score can be obtained by remaining at the nominal power-flow solution.

The snapshot estimator B1 computes a prior-conditioned linear minimum-mean-square-error (LMMSE) update independently at every frame,
\begin{align}
 \bm K_{\mathrm{LMMSE}}&=\bm P_0\bm C^{\mathsf T}
 \left(\bm C\bm P_0\bm C^{\mathsf T}+\bm R\right)^{-1}, \label{eq:wls-gain}\\
 \widehat{\delta\bm x}^{\mathrm{B1}}_k&=\bm K_{\mathrm{LMMSE}}(\bm y_k-\bm y_0), \qquad
 \widehat{\bm z}^{\mathrm{B1}}_{\mathcal U,k}=\bm z_{\mathcal U,0}+\bm L_{\mathcal U}\widehat{\delta\bm x}^{\mathrm{B1}}_k. \label{eq:wls-update}
\end{align}
B1 uses network-constrained cross-covariance but no state carried from frame $k-1$.

Conditional on one linear-Gaussian candidate with no corruption, B2 is not an ad hoc module: the Kalman recursion computes the exact conditional mean and covariance in \eqref{eq:exact-bayes-target}. Candidate uncertainty, nonlinear switching, unknown integrity support, and non-Gaussian discrepancy are what make the enclosing hybrid problem approximate.

The causal Kalman estimator B2 applies the classical linear-Gaussian recursion \cite{kalman1960}:
\begin{align}
 \widehat{\bm x}_{k|k-1}&=\bm A_d\widehat{\bm x}_{k-1|k-1}, \label{eq:kf-predict-state}\\
 \bm P_{k|k-1}&=\bm A_d\bm P_{k-1|k-1}\bm A_d^{\mathsf T}+\bm Q, \label{eq:kf-predict-cov}\\
 \bm\nu_k&=(\bm y_k-\bm y_0)-\bm C\widehat{\bm x}_{k|k-1}, \label{eq:innovation}\\
 \bm S_k&=\bm C\bm P_{k|k-1}\bm C^{\mathsf T}+\bm R, \label{eq:innovation-cov}\\
 \bm K_k&=\bm P_{k|k-1}\bm C^{\mathsf T}\bm S_k^{-1}, \label{eq:kf-gain}\\
 \widehat{\bm x}_{k|k}&=\widehat{\bm x}_{k|k-1}+\bm K_k\bm\nu_k. \label{eq:kf-update}
\end{align}
The covariance update uses the Joseph form. The hidden estimate is $\bm z_{\mathcal U,0}+\bm L_{\mathcal U}\widehat{\bm x}_{k|k}$. B2 is causal because only $\bm y_{0:k}$ enters the result at $k$.

B3 is an exact fixed-lag Gaussian smoother, following the linear Gaussian backward-recursion principle in \cite{rauch1965smoothing}, evaluated at lags $\ell\in\{0,1,3,5,10,15,30,60\}$ frames. It uses measurements through $k+\ell$ to refine the estimate at $k$ and is therefore retrospective by $\ell/30$ seconds. Dense and cached implementations agree numerically on representative lags. B3 tests whether temporal information beyond the causal update improves the hidden-voltage target; it is not used to support a real-time claim.

\subsection{Marginal Calibration and Colored Discrepancy}

For the hidden two-component voltage error at bus $i$, B2 provides
\begin{equation}
 \bm\Sigma_{i,k}=\bm L_i\bm P_{k|k}\bm L_i^{\mathsf T}. \label{eq:hidden-covariance}
\end{equation}
The original covariance is U0. A two-block temperature model U2 is fitted on an independent calibration split:
\begin{equation}
 \bm\Sigma^{\mathrm{U2}}_{i,k}=\bm T\bm\Sigma_{i,k}\bm T^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 \bm T=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\tau_{\Re}},\sqrt{\tau_{\Im}}). \label{eq:temperature}
\end{equation}
The temperatures minimize Gaussian negative log likelihood (NLL) on calibration data and are frozen before TEST. Coverage, NLL, normalized innovation squared (NIS), and temporal innovation correlation are reported separately; nominal marginal coverage alone is not called complete calibration.

The residual study considers a latent Gauss--Markov process
\begin{equation}
 \bm c_{k+1}=\bm F_c\bm c_k+\bm\eta_k. \label{eq:discrepancy-state}
\end{equation}
In D2-M, $\bm c_k$ enters the PMU measurement residual through a calibration-fitted low-rank basis. In D2-P, it enters a sparse physical process dictionary. Basis, rank, $\bm F_c$, and latent covariance are selected using calibration records only. The experiment asks whether the observed low-rank structure improves reconstruction, hidden uncertainty, or innovation whiteness. It does not assume that a lower innovation autocorrelation is sufficient.

\subsection{Model-Adequacy Statistic and Recentring Ablations}

Let $\bm\mu_{\nu}$ and $\bm\Sigma_{\nu}$ be the mean and shrunk covariance of B2 innovations on the nominal calibration set. The per-frame adequacy statistic is the five-frame trailing mean of
\begin{equation}
 a_k=(\bm\nu_k-\bm\mu_{\nu})^{\mathsf T}\bm\Sigma_{\nu}^{-1}(\bm\nu_k-\bm\mu_{\nu}). \label{eq:adequacy-score}
\end{equation}
Mismatch labels and hidden truth do not enter \eqref{eq:adequacy-score}; hidden truth is used only after inference to label frames whose hidden HVRE exceeds the frozen evaluation threshold. AUROC and AUPRC therefore measure whether observed innovations anticipate unacceptable hidden error, not whether the detector identifies a mismatch family.

Three variants diagnose the failure mechanism without merging their evidence contracts. R1 replaces the nominal equilibrium with the true plant equilibrium and is explicitly oracle. R2-A estimates an eight-dimensional grouped observed-bus voltage offset using a 60-frame trailing window, nominal matrices, ridge regularization, and only PMU measurements. It is a causal negative ablation: its center/state cross-covariance is neglected and it does not enforce the nonlinear AC equations.

The corrected regularized physical estimator addresses M6 operating-point mismatch at one equilibrium snapshot. Let
\begin{equation}
 \bm d=\operatorname{col}(\Delta\bm P_{\mathrm{PQ}},\Delta\bm Q_{\mathrm{PQ}},\Delta\bm P_{\mathrm{PV}})\in\mathbb R^{43} \label{eq:e06h-nuisance}
\end{equation}
collect active and reactive changes at 17 admissible PQ loads and active-power changes at nine PV buses. Voltage is parameterized as $\bm V(\bm\theta,\bm\rho)=\exp(\bm\rho+\mathrm{j}\bm\theta)$. Defining the scaled PMU residual as $\bm e_y(\bm V)=\bm D_y^{-1}[\bm h_{\mathcal P}(\bm V)-\bm y_{\mathcal P}]$, the estimator solves
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 (\widehat{\bm V},\widehat{\bm d})
 &=\argmin_{\bm V,\bm d}\;\mathcal J(\bm V,\bm d),\\
 \mathcal J(\bm V,\bm d)
 &=\|\bm e_y(\bm V)\|_2^2
 +\gamma^2\|\bm g_{\mathrm{AC}}(\bm V,\bm d)\|_2^2
 +\frac{\lambda}{q_d^2}\|\bm d\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:e06h-map}
\end{equation}
where $\bm h_{\mathcal P}$ is the audited voltage/terminal-current operator, $\bm g_{\mathrm{AC}}$ includes PQ balance, explicit PV-magnitude constraints, solved PV reactive power, and bus 31 as Slack, and $\bm D_y$ scales voltage and current components separately. The weights are $\gamma=10^3$, $q_d=1$, and $\lambda=0.01$, selected on a disjoint DEV split; continuation initialization is then frozen for TEST. Neither the true nuisance vector nor the hidden voltages enter the TEST solve.

Because $\bm d$ may be non-unique, evaluation distinguishes nuisance information from target information. If $\bm J_{y d}$ and $\bm J_{z d}$ are the observed-PMU and hidden-voltage Jacobians with respect to $\bm d$ after eliminating voltage through the AC constraints, the local target residual is
\begin{equation}
 r_{d\rightarrow z}=\frac{\|\bm J_{z d}\bm N(\bm J_{y d})\|_F}{\max(\|\bm J_{z d}\|_F,\epsilon)}, \label{eq:e06h-functional-residual}
\end{equation}
where $\bm N(\bm J_{y d})$ spans the PMU-uninformed nuisance nullspace. Exact target identifiability would require a zero residual. The experiment instead tests the weaker empirical question: whether \,\eqref{eq:e06h-map} reconstructs the hidden voltage accurately despite rank-deficient nuisance information, and whether its Laplace covariance is credible.
