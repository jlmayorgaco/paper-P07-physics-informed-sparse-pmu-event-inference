The common detector matched \SecondMatched{} of 36 second events
and issued \SecondExtras{} unmatched alarms in the two-event records.
The original decision at its native publication time was correct for
\SecondNative{}/36. Table~\ref{tab:second-family} compares families
at the common publication report. The same alarm stream gave
\SecondCal{} normal alarms on calibration and \SecondNormal{} over
\SecondQuietHours{} normal test hours (one-sided 95\% upper bound
\SecondUpper{}/h). Thus differences in the table arise downstream of
the trigger. As a separate implementation check, the authors' released
CNN-F weights classified \SecondUpSP{}/\SecondUpN{} examples with
subtraction and \SecondUpNS{}/\SecondUpN{} without it on their public
IEEE 68-bus data at one-second separation.

All \SecondPdfWins{} discordances between the original family decision and
CNN-F favored the bank and concerned true loads classified as lines by
CNN-F. The paired difference was \SecondPdfEffect{} percentage points
(stratified bootstrap 95\% interval $[\SecondPdfLo{},\SecondPdfHi{}]$).
With only \SecondPdfWins{} discordant trajectories, the two-sided exact
paired test gave $p=\SecondPdfP{}$. The fixed-lag estimator and the
original decision identified the same families on every matched second
event. The common trigger missed the remaining \SecondMissed{}.

Carrying the physical mean through restoration gained \SecondStateWins{}
timely family diagnoses and lost \SecondStateLosses{} relative to
resetting it. The net gain was \SecondStateEffect{} points
($[\SecondStateLo{},\SecondStateHi{}]$, exact paired $p=\SecondStateP{}$).
With the same decision times and observations, the reset more often left
no retained history supporting an intervention at the alarm. This
comparison isolates the state treatment within the merged bank. The
bank-to-CNN comparison also changes the features and decision rule.
