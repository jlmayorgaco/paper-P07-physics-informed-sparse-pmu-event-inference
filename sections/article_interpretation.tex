\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What State Continuity Explains}
A restored operator can leave a nonzero physical state, so returning
the predictor to equilibrium introduces a residual that another
candidate can explain. The reset control shows this failure at fixed
thresholds; bounded profiling and marginalization tie in that control.
The nominal signature diagnostics help explain short delays under the
assumed noise model. They do not predict operational error rates.

\subsection{Coverage, Alarm Cost and Scope}
On the held-out comparison, retaining a bank of histories gives higher
timely coverage and fewer extra alarms than the continuous GLR.
ExtraTrees emits fewer extra alarms but misses more interventions.
These are contrasts between complete procedures. A control that
conserves the state and changes only the retention of recovery histories
is needed to attribute a gain to that mechanism at comparable operating
points.

Observations and predictors use the same simulator family. The catalog
is fixed, the gaps between interventions are 3--8\,s, and no intervention
overlaps another. Transfer of the dynamic bank to a distinct plant,
shorter gaps and unseen sources are untested. The terminal map retains
the provisional bus-5 current terminal and excludes the bus-39 current
angle. Under independent per-PMU noise the bank misses most load changes
at 0.05\% TVE. A dropout with held observations prevents recovery in the
auxiliary stress test. The bank did not start on the retrospective
competition record; the integrity checks and separate attributor in the
supplement do not validate the dynamic bank on that record.

Pruning, steady-state covariances, startup fitting and compression
make the attribution weights approximate. Retrospective proposals
change inner history weights without updating the outer model weights.
The nominal bank processed reports faster than they arrived in a
measured replay on one thread; the five-model version did not.
This timing is specific to the reported hardware and records.
