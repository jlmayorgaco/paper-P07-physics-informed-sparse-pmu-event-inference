\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why State Continuity Affects Attribution}

The continuous GLR already propagates a physical state through
restoration. Its lower rate of timely attribution therefore cannot be
explained by a return to equilibrium. It commits to one trajectory and
profiles the size of a candidate load change. The bank can reconsider a
recent onset from a saved state and average over several explanations of
the same reports. The primary comparison measures these choices together.
The fixed-threshold test provides little support for attributing the
difference to severity integration alone. Substituting a profile score
gave identical counts within each state treatment on those records.

Of the two post-closure choices, carrying the estimated physical mean
has the clearest support across plants. Equation~\eqref{eq:recovery-log-loss}
explains how a reset can create an apparent new event. When the discarded
state projects onto a measured phasor, the next report is displaced from
the reset prediction even if the system simply continues to recover.
A new load or line candidate can explain part of that displacement.
The size of the one-report penalty depends on what the PMUs can see.
An alarm also depends on later observations, competing sources and the
six-report decision rule.

A separate test supplied the first restoration time and searched the
eight PMUs for a second switch. At a common alarm, allowing 29 more
reports and averaging over possible onset times raised timely family
diagnoses from \ModalImmediateFamily{}/96 to \ModalConfirmFamily{}/96
with sinusoidal contrasts. With the same reports and onset averaging,
the mean alone gave \ModalConfirmMean{}/96 and a quadratic trend gave
\ModalConfirmQuad{}/96. The timing of the diagnosis mattered more here
than the choice of temporal basis. The supplement gives the source
counts and the recovery-only controls.

The comparison between retained histories and their merged mean has a
different interpretation. Both preserve the physical mean at the point
of fusion for the components being merged. Fusion removes alternative
recovery trajectories, leaves out their dispersion in report space
\eqref{eq:recovery-report-variance}, and restarts the onset search from
the merged state. The experiment changes all three. On the second plant
the coverage interval spans losses and gains, even though retaining
histories reduces unmatched alarms. Determining which part of fusion
matters would require controlling the prediction covariance and the
stored onset-search information separately.

\subsection{Sensitivity to Plant and Instrument Models}

The second plant changes machine dynamics and some passive parameters
while keeping the network layout, measurement terminals and candidate
assets. Transfer here concerns those changes. Both test plants assume a
balanced network, a quiet startup and nonoverlapping interventions from
the 18-source catalog. The results give no measured accuracy for unknown
sources, overlapping switches or unbalanced faults. On the SGSMA competition record, the bank failed its startup check and therefore
produced no diagnosis.

Relative angles across PMUs are unusually quiet under the simulated
common instrument noise. In the auxiliary replay, adding independent
errors reduced load attribution to \RevNoiseTVEaLoad{} even at
\RevNoiseAddedRmsLow{}\% added RMS total vector error. With its original
startup limits the two-plant bank rejected every independent-noise stress
record. A test on field PMUs would therefore have to establish both
admission of the records and source accuracy at their measured noise
correlations. The one-thread timing results likewise establish average
processing throughput only. They leave worst-case delay per report open.

The dynamic bank rejected all M9 startup records. At familiar sites
the fallback located 188/192 sources. On held-out sites the electrical
hybrid classified all 72 physical events and located two. After a fault,
phase balance raised macro-F1 from 0.943 to 0.981 with physical location
fixed at 4/36. In a separate test, registering M9 responses raised exact
location from 23/64 to 56/64 after a fault. All eight errors were weak
composites. With onset and strength supplied and no instrument corruption,
the voltage readout located only 10/78 weak sources (supplement).
