\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The three contracts expose different parts of the inverse problem. Eight voltage/current PMUs recover the requested 31-bus voltage field accurately in the PowerDynamics state tests even though the 114-state local model is not fully observable. A separate physical load-response dictionary supports normalized event and source evidence on the same simulator. The ANDES bank measures what the earlier supervised representation retained when source labels were removed. Low hidden-voltage HVRE still does not guarantee source separation, and high event visibility does not guarantee exact localization.

The local theory explains this split. Proposition~\ref{prop:functional-reconstruction} removes unobservable directions that do not affect the requested voltage function. Corollary~\ref{cor:functional-blue} then attaches a covariance to that estimable function; rank alone cannot provide it. Source diagnosis has a different geometry. Proposition~\ref{prop:source-indistinguishability} tests whether two candidate-nuisance response sets intersect, while Proposition~\ref{prop:nonlinear-separation} requires the affine distance to exceed the combined DAE remainder before local nonlinear separation is certified. Large faults, topology switches, and unmodeled controls may violate the neighborhood in which that bound holds.

Two theory-to-data links are now tested. The preregistered functional residual correlates with bus-level voltage error, although excitation, scaling, estimator covariance, and electrical distance explain several exceptions. In the load study, event-visible information predicts the source-wise detection threshold. The result has the expected negative sign: more whitened response energy requires a smaller amplitude for 90\% detection. The projected pairwise information does not predict confusion convincingly. That negative test prevents a detectability result from being relabeled as a localization theory.

The operating-point study also separates target recovery from parameter recovery. Regularized physical recentering closes \EZeroHClosureOverall{} of the oracle-center gap on M6 although only \EZeroHRank{} of \EZeroHDimension{} nuisance directions are locally informed. The hidden-voltage target can therefore be accurate while the injection coordinates remain non-unique. This result is confined to an equilibrium snapshot. It does not cover line outages, faults, or sequential event-conditioned estimation, and the local curvature covariance remains under-dispersed in the joint NEES-like statistic.

The legacy event model supplies one reusable mechanism and one warning. Multiscale summaries, adaptive residuals, and simultaneous PMU contrasts improve known-source physical ranking by \LegacyPhysicalGainPP{} percentage points. Availability belongs in the integrity state because a missing measurement is observed directly. Source identities and endpoint descriptors, however, allow memorization. Exact localization falls to \HeldSourceRate{} under complete physical-source holdout. The load experiment avoids an asset-label head by evaluating each candidate as a physical intervention. Learned residual features may still help, but their contribution must be measured against this physics-only likelihood.

The principal evidence gap is the absence of one multi-family end-to-end experiment. The state, load-source, and ANDES studies have different targets or simulator semantics. All are simulated; none supplies field or hardware-in-the-loop validation. The physical Bayesian result covers one intervention family, fixed topology, known onset window, and matched candidate simulator. The ANDES records also have fixed onsets and only about 7.15~min of normal-labeled exposure per split, too little for an operational false-alarm rate. PMU loss and addition have not been linked prospectively to the separation margin.

A decisive follow-up requires one audited plant and measurement operator for learned-only, physics-only, learned-residual, and diagnosability-aware methods across all physical families. Event parents and physical sources must be disjoint across fitting, calibration, and test. Operating-point shift, parameter error, topology change, and PMU loss or addition must be imposed before TEST is opened. A long normal stream must report false alarms per hour and event recall; event-source correctness, candidate-set coverage, resolution delay, HVRE, calibration error, runtime, and failed solves complete the evaluation. Until then, multi-family joint superiority remains unproved.
