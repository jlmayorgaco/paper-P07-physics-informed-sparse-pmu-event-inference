\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The experiments support three linked conclusions. First, eight voltage/current PMUs contain enough information for accurate nominal recovery of the requested 31-bus voltage field even though the 114-state local model is far from full rank at the chosen numerical tolerance. This is exactly the distinction functional observability is intended to express. Second, most of the nominal improvement comes from physical conditioning on current PMU measurements; causal dynamics add a smaller benefit and retrospective smoothing adds none. Third, the dominant tested failure is not a universal shortage of sensor information. Network and operating-point changes invalidate the estimator center and local map, as shown by the oracle recovery.

The structural residual is useful because it predicts a measurable error pattern, but $\rho=\FunctionalSpearman$ is not a certificate. Its bus-ranking exceptions show that finite-horizon linear structure, numerical tolerance, excitation, and stochastic estimator design remain entangled. A next observability study should vary PMU membership and operating point prospectively, then test whether changes in the residual predict paired changes in error. The present fixed-placement result cannot support an optimal-placement claim.

The uncertainty experiments expose two distinct pitfalls. Marginal temperature scaling improves nominal interval coverage and NLL without whitening innovations. Conversely, a low-rank Gauss--Markov residual can improve one innovation statistic while severely damaging hidden-state likelihood and coverage. A learned discrepancy model should therefore be admitted only after it beats both the frozen and physically adaptive baselines on hidden-state metrics under a disjoint physical-shift test. Residual predictability alone is insufficient evidence for physics-informed learning.

The adequacy statistic creates a practical gate: observed PMUs can flag that the frozen model is no longer trustworthy before hidden truth is available. Its reported AUROC/AUPRC values are retrospective discrimination metrics under a frozen TVE threshold, not calibrated false-alarm guarantees for a continuous operational stream. Threshold selection, long normal exposure, sensor corruption, topology errors, and missing PMUs remain untested. The statistic also does not identify which physical parameter is wrong.

Four limitations bound external validity. The PowerDynamics IEEE 39-bus data are package-defined and pedagogical; no field, hardware-in-the-loop, cross-simulator, or independent network evidence is included. The nominal trajectories cover three seconds of governor-reference and load perturbations rather than faults, outages, topology switching, or mixed integrity events. The validated estimators are local linear-Gaussian methods, not the full nonlinear Hybrid-DAE Bayesian formulation. Finally, R2-A estimates only grouped observed-voltage offsets and neglects center/state cross-covariance; its failure does not rule out a leakage-safe AC power-flow/KKT update followed by Jacobian rebuilding.

These boundaries also organize the broader research program. Event detection and physical-source localization require a separate intervention space, source-held-out splits, alarm exposure, and explicit comparison against the earlier physics-guided feature system. They should build on an estimator whose physical center can first adapt without oracle state. Until that gate is passed, attaching a source classifier or learned residual would obscure rather than resolve the mechanism established here.
