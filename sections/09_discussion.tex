\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The contracts expose different parts of the inverse problem. Eight voltage/current PMUs recover the requested 31-bus voltage field accurately in the PowerDynamics state tests even though the 114-state local model is not fully observable. On the same simulator, a physical load-response dictionary supports normalized posterior evidence over all zero-, one-, and two-source supports. The ANDES bank measures what the earlier supervised representation retained when source labels were removed. Low hidden-voltage HVRE still does not guarantee source separation, and correct cardinality does not guarantee exact support.

The local theory explains this split. Proposition~\ref{prop:functional-reconstruction} removes unobservable directions that do not affect the requested voltage function, and Corollary~\ref{cor:functional-blue} attaches a covariance to that estimable function. Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse-event-injectivity} instead asks whether any $2K$-sparse event difference lies in the finite-horizon kernel and distinguishes uniqueness from conditioning. Proposition~\ref{prop:source-indistinguishability} then adds state and integrity nuisance. Large faults, topology switches, and unmodeled controls may violate these local descriptions.

Three theory-to-data links now have different evidential status. The functional residual correlates with bus-level voltage error, but its projector fails rank monotonicity and needs an SVD/noise-tied recomputation. In the prospective event test, weak cardinality becomes confident before exact support. Retrospectively, profiled nonlinear distance is strongly associated with exact weak-case recovery, whereas support-only geometry is weak. The tested directions are first-order resolvable, so the curvature-dominated mechanism is not the explanation for this bank. The T120 equilibrium dictionary has positive subset margin, yet near-unit coherence exposes poor conditioning; the correct conclusion is finite-information ambiguity under the validated local model, not absence of structural ambiguity in general.

The operating-point study also separates target recovery from parameter recovery. Regularized physical recentering closes \EZeroHClosureOverall{} of the oracle-center gap on M6 although only \EZeroHRank{} of \EZeroHDimension{} nuisance directions are locally informed. The hidden-voltage target can therefore be accurate while the injection coordinates remain non-unique. This result is confined to an equilibrium snapshot. It does not cover line outages, faults, or sequential event-conditioned estimation, and the local curvature covariance remains under-dispersed in the joint NEES-like statistic.

The legacy event model supplies one reusable mechanism and one warning. Multiscale summaries, adaptive residuals, and simultaneous PMU contrasts improve known-source physical ranking by \LegacyPhysicalGainPP{} percentage points. Availability belongs in the integrity state because a missing measurement is observed directly. Source identities and endpoint descriptors, however, allow memorization. Exact localization falls to \HeldSourceRate{} under complete physical-source holdout. The load experiment avoids an asset-label head by integrating evidence for every physical support. Learned residual features may still help, but their contribution must be measured against this physics-only likelihood on the same event parents.

The principal evidence gap is a multi-family end-to-end experiment. The studies have different targets or simulator semantics and are simulated. The Bayesian result covers one family, fixed topology, known onset, closed candidates, matched operation, and synthetic noise; it does not fit state or integrity nuisance. The T120 physical check contains only 32 nominal-operating-point trajectories, and its absolute separation scale is not reconciled with the earlier profiled metric. The ANDES records have fixed onsets and about 7.15~min of normal exposure per split. Neither field validity nor operational false-alarm control is established.

A decisive follow-up first completes independent-operating-point T120 validation and reconciles the separation metric, then freezes a prospective horizon study at 30--120 frames. The multi-family stage should compare learned-only, physics-only, learned-residual, and diagnosability-aware methods under one audited plant and PMU operator. Event parents and sources must remain disjoint across fitting, calibration, and test. Shift, topology, PMU availability, and long normal exposure must report joint correctness, candidate-set coverage, resolution delay, false alarms per hour, HVRE, calibration, runtime, and failed solves. Until then, multi-family superiority remains unproved.
