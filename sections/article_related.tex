\subsection{Relation to Prior Work}
\label{sec:narrative-related}

Willsky and Jones used the innovations of a linear filter to detect a jump
and estimate its time and magnitude. After a detection, compensating the
state estimate allows the search for another jump to continue
\cite{willsky1976glr}. Sequential detection and isolation extends the
decision to several competing causes \cite{nikiforov1995generalized,lai2000sequential}.
The interacting multiple-model filter mixes estimates conditioned on the
current mode \cite{blom1988imm}. Multiple-hypothesis filters retain
alternative mode sequences \cite{boers2005m3h}. We use these established
filtering operations because two switch histories can leave the network
in the same topology with different rotor states.

Grid outage methods relate observed phasor angles to candidate line
openings \cite{tate2008outage,chen2016quickest}. Subsequent work accounts
for transient dynamics in the observations \cite{rovatsos2017outage} and
uses a time-varying AC model or particle-filtered generator states to detect
an outage \cite{yang2020control,yang2021particle}. For short-circuit
faults, Warner \emph{et al.} compare voltage and current trajectories
predicted for candidate lines and onset times \cite{warner2023highfidelity}.
Noori \emph{et al.} use the admittance matrix and sparse voltage changes
to identify a faulted section or a search region on a radial feeder
\cite{noori2026circuit}. Xing \emph{et al.} test how sensor placement
affects exact fault-bus identification under varying physical conditions
\cite{xing2026placement}. Our question arises after the first asset
returns to service: can its residual motion explain the next transient
without invoking a second switch? Li and Wang already predict and subtract
the first disturbance from PMU measurements. They estimate oscillatory
modes from the residual and classify the second event with a shallow
convolutional network \cite{liwang2019successive}. Their classifier assumes
an event trigger. We use that interface to compare spectral classification
with two ways of inferring the family from network histories, all at the
same alarm times.

For a generator trip, Azizi \emph{et al.} use the bus impedance
matrix to identify the location and size of the lost injection from
sparse phasors \cite{azizi2021log}. Our four-family experiment includes
a finite torque pulse at a machine that stays connected.
Its early motion depends on both the source bus and
the time for which torque has acted. We use this ambiguity to select
a measured current terminal for a joint time-and-source estimate.

Recursive DAE estimators recover differential and algebraic states from
partial measurements \cite{katanic2024recursive}. Trajectory sensitivities
also relate uncertain physical parameters to measured responses
\cite{nagi2022trajectory}. Here the unknowns include which asset switched.
On the Brazilian interconnected system, Lopes \emph{et al.} evaluated
multi-label event categorization over six months of operation
\cite{lopes2025multilabel}. That study addresses overlapping event labels
in field data. Here a diagnosis must also name the individual bus or line
within a specified time.
PMU classifiers can identify disturbances and their locations from
the observed features \cite{pandey2020realtime,li2024knowledge,yildiz2024few}.
Barati uses recurrent PMU features to forecast grid disruptions
\cite{barati2019faster}. PMU hardware also affects the downstream diagnosis. Celeita \emph{et al.} combine real-time co-simulation,
virtual PMUs and OpenPMU for state estimation \cite{celeita2023iec61850}.
Becejac and Dehghanian trace PMU estimation errors through a fault-location
application in hardware-in-the-loop tests \cite{becejac2019pmu}.
Our simulated reporting chain makes the corresponding noise sensitivity
measurable but leaves hardware performance open.

The archived window-based ExtraTrees system detected 32 of 96 load and line
windows from 36 development trajectories, identified the family in 23,
and matched both family and asset in none. Our continuous-stream comparison
uses two adapters trained on 32 independent simulated trajectories from
four earlier campaigns. Both cover normal operation and all 18 catalog
sources. The supplement also reports results for supervised models fitted
on the bank's training and calibration records.
