\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\PARstart{A}{fter} a load is restored or a line is reclosed, physical state
continues to evolve. A second intervention may begin before that response
decays. With eight PMUs, the observed change can combine recovery from the
earlier event with forcing from a new source
(Fig.~\ref{fig:problem-method}(a)).

Physical restoration is not electrical recovery. Resetting state at OFF can
make the remaining response look like another event, while waiting for it to
settle can hide a real intervention. In statistical terms, the post-change
law of a second intervention depends on the history of the first. Classical change
detection and isolation treats a single change of an otherwise stationary
law \cite{nikiforov1995generalized,lai2000sequential,tartakovsky2014sequential},
and the generalized-likelihood approach of Willsky and Jones detects jumps
in a fixed linear system \cite{willsky1976glr}. Power-system outage
detectors compare network hypotheses under quasi-static or linearized
models \cite{tate2008outage,chen2016quickest,rovatsos2017outage}, and
trajectory libraries diagnose faults from sparse PMUs
\cite{warner2023highfidelity}. None of these formulations makes a restored
intervention compete as a physical cause with later interventions in a
switched DAE network.

We address this gap with three contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Recovery-aware attribution as switched-DAE change detection and
isolation.} Candidate histories specify intervention, source, onset,
severity interval and restoration time. Their predictors are reduced from
the network DAE, start from estimated past states and keep the state
through restoration. Severity is integrated in closed form, and separate
active and cause weights separate the present intervention from the
originating cause of the continuing response
(Section~\ref{sec:narrative-method}).
\item \emph{Nominal separation and the cost of resetting state.}
We derive signature energy for the 18 candidate sources and an
innovation bias after a state reset. These diagnostics explain what the
nominal operators predict at a supplied onset and indicate where the
assumed noise model limits that interpretation
(Section~\ref{sec:theory}).
\item \emph{A common-budget evaluation.} We compare the bank with a
continuous physical GLR and refitted ExtraTrees by timely attribution,
extra alarms and parent-level uncertainty
(Section~\ref{sec:recovery-comparison}).
\end{enumerate}

The evaluation covers a known catalog of sequential load and line
interventions. The supplement records auxiliary analyses and
reproducibility details.
