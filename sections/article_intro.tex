\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\PARstart{A}{fter} a load is restored or a line is reclosed, the machines
continue moving. A second intervention may begin before that response has
decayed. With only eight PMUs in a 39-bus network, a change in a measured
signal may therefore indicate a new source, recovery from an earlier
source, or an imperfect physical prediction. The task is to distinguish
these explanations using only the reports available at the decision time.

Physical restoration is not electrical recovery. A detector that returns
to a flat reference after OFF can issue another alarm for the remaining
oscillation, while suppressing alarms during recovery can hide a real
intervention. In statistical terms, the post-change law of a second
intervention depends on the history of the first. Classical change
detection and isolation treats a single change of an otherwise stationary
law \cite{nikiforov1995generalized,lai2000sequential,tartakovsky2014sequential},
and the generalized-likelihood approach of Willsky and Jones detects jumps
in a fixed linear system \cite{willsky1976glr}. Power-system outage
detectors compare network hypotheses under quasi-static or linearized
models \cite{tate2008outage,chen2016quickest,rovatsos2017outage}, and
trajectory libraries diagnose faults from sparse PMUs
\cite{warner2023highfidelity}. None of these formulations makes a restored
intervention compete, as a physical cause, with later interventions in a
switched DAE network.

We address this gap with three contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Recovery-aware attribution as switched-DAE change detection and
isolation.} Candidate histories specify intervention, source, onset,
severity interval and restoration time. Their predictors are reduced from
the network DAE, start from estimated past states and keep the state
through restoration. Severity is integrated in closed form, and separate
active and cause weights separate the present intervention from the
originating cause of the continuing response
(Section~\ref{sec:narrative-method}).
\item \emph{Physics-derived detectability and the cost of forgetting.}
Because each history is linear-Gaussian given its mode path, its
Willsky--Jones signature follows from the network operators. We compute
the detection and isolation information of all 18 candidate sources,
the minimum detectable load change as a function of PMU noise, and the
spurious evidence created by resetting the physical state at restoration.
We extend the analysis to the integrity events of the competition: the
information left when a PMU is missing or corrupted, and two parity
relations, sequence balance and network coupling, that no balanced
physical event can violate. These quantities are obtained before any
campaign and are then checked against measured delays, a controlled
state-reset study, noise and integrity stress tests with unchanged code,
and the labeled competition record (Section~\ref{sec:theory}).
\item \emph{An independent causal evaluation with explicit failure
accounting.} On 18 new physical parents (108 interventions, 648 paired
replays, 11.8\,h of normal records) we separate detection, timely source
attribution, unmatched alarms, restoration and computation time, and we
report the published quickest-change detector of Chen \emph{et al.} and two
learned adapters at their original operating points
(Section~\ref{sec:narrative-results}).
\end{enumerate}

The bank attributes \COneMixTimely{}/\COneMixTotal{} interventions before
physical restoration and raises no alarm on normal records. The analysis
explains why: under the noise model calibrated on the reference data, a
10\% load step or a line opening carries between about 300 and
$5\times10^{7}$ times the evidence required for an alarm within one
second. The errors that remain arise from network-model mismatch and from
the publication rules, not from missing information. The
same analysis identifies the conditions under which this ceases to hold:
independent per-PMU noise that the filter does not model, and a missing
PMU whose values are imputed instead of deleted. On the labeled competition
record the parity relations diagnose every missing and corrupted PMU
episode at its first report and reveal corruptions that the labels miss.
The supplement documents the implemented filter, the complete outcomes and
the reproducibility contract.
