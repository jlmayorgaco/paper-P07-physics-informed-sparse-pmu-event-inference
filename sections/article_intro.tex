\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\PARstart{S}{uppose} a load at bus~16 returns to its original setting.
While the generators are still swinging, a phasor measurement unit (PMU)
on a neighboring branch records another change in current. The change may
follow from the load recovery alone. It may also reflect the opening of
line 16--17 during that recovery. Can a monitor with eight PMUs distinguish
these cases and, if the line opened, identify it before it recloses?

We treat recovery as a competing explanation for each new report.
A bank of switched differential-algebraic equation (DAE) models predicts
how voltages and currents would evolve if recovery continued and how they
would change if a particular load or line switched. The bank assigns each
history a weight based on its prediction errors. Two sums of these weights
answer different questions. A load already restored contributes no weight
to the hypothesis that it is still active, but its history can still
explain which load produced the measured transient
(Fig.~\ref{fig:problem-method}).

We test this distinction on successive, nonoverlapping interventions from
a fixed catalog of nine load buses and nine lines. We first compare the bank
with a continuous generalized likelihood ratio (GLR) observer and an
ExtraTrees classifier at a common calibration target for alarms in normal
operation. We then ask what information a monitor needs to keep after it
reports restoration. On paired sequences from two simulated plants, we
compare retaining recovery histories, merging them into a single state
mean, and resetting the physical part of that mean. We count an intervention
as correctly attributed when the monitor names its family and asset before
the intervention ends. We also count alarms that cannot be matched to an
intervention.
