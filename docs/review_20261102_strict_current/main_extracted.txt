ABSTRACT Sparse PMU measurements can reveal a disturbance without uniquely identifying its cause. We
study how network equations and conditional Bayesian evidence complement feature-based classification
for this task. A bank of switched differential-algebraic predictors compares source, onset, severity and
restoration histories, retaining electrical and instrumental memory between interventions. Local severity
integration and fixed-network model averaging produce separate active-event and originating-cause masses.
Component experiments examine hidden-voltage reconstruction, engineered-feature localization and mixed-
source response sensitivities; these are distinguished from the final causal estimator. In a frozen IEEE 39-
bus comparison with eight PMUs, 18 six-event schedules are replayed under three network conditions and
two noise levels. The physical bank makes 644/648 timely family/source decisions with 72 extra alarms,
compared with 330/648 and 29 for a causal ExtraTrees reference. Its strongest gain is load-source attribution;
line attribution is similar to the short-window learned comparator. Most extra physical alarms occur under
network changes outside the model bank. A separate historical transfer experiment retains the original
hackathon submission and its confusion matrix. The results identify a useful coverage–alarm trade-off within
the simulated catalog. Transfer to a different network model, calibration of event probabilities and the
separate contribution of each inference component require further evidence.
INDEX TERMS event detection, model uncertainty, phasor measurement units, physics-informed inference,
sequential diagnosis, source localization.
I. INTRODUCTION
E
IGHT PMUs observe only a small part of a 39-bus
network, yet an operator needs to know whether a
disturbance occurred, which physical mechanism caused it,
and where it began. An unmeasured bus can change several
remote PMU signals, while different sources can produce
similar measured responses. The problem is therefore both
electrical and statistical: reconstruct the response allowed by
the network, then compare its possible causes under uncertain
state, severity and noise.
Our starting point is a feature-based hackathon system.
Voltage, current, frequency and spatial/temporal descriptors
supported an ExtraTrees detector, classifier and localizer. We
retain that route and its results. The complementary route
asks an explicit counterfactual question: if this source had
changed at a candidate past time, what would all eight PMUs
have measured up to now? Physical equations provide the
candidate path; conditional Bayesian evidence accounts for
unknown severity and competing histories. After restoration,
those histories must still explain the remaining machine mo-
tion.
Physical restoration does not erase machine motion. A flat
reference can mislabel its remaining oscillation as a second
event; suppressing all alarms during recovery can hide an
actual second event. A corrected voltage offset also need not
correct the response to a later input.
DAE estimators recover unmeasured electrical states [1];
outage detectors compare physical hypotheses [2], [3]; and
simulated trajectories support sparse-PMU fault diagno-
sis [4]. These ingredients are established. Our question is
whether a continuing recovery should explain a new PMU
change or yield to a later intervention hypothesis.
We formulate a causal attribution procedure that keeps the
estimated physical response after an intervention ends and
tests subsequent changes against that continuing response.

Warner et al. compare simulated voltage/current paths

Learned PMU classifiers and physical uncertainty models

physical route instead computes the observation likelihood
of candidate interventions; its graph is the admittance net-
work and terminal map, not a learned message-passing layer.
The two frozen ExtraTrees implementations test supervised
references, not all learned diagnosis. The new contribution
claimed here is the specified sparse-PMU procedure and its
evidence: new interventions and retained recovery histories
compete for the same causal reports, with component exper-
iments explaining the role and limits of physical represen-
tation. We do not claim that combining physics, Bayes and
a tensor is itself new. Table 1 distinguishes the neighboring
tasks and the comparators actually executed here.
III. PROBLEM AND MEASUREMENT MODEL
At report k, inference uses measurements through tk = k/30
to decide whether a physical intervention occurred. Detection
concerns its presence; classification selects a physical oper-
ator; localization selects a bus or branch. These outputs can
become reliable at different times. We record the estimated
physical onset bτ separately from the alarm and diagnosis
emission times.
Figure 2 introduces the broader measurement phenomena
using the known RAW0001 reference record. Faults, line
changes, generation changes and load changes alter the phys-
ical network; missing or corrupted reports alter observation
integrity. The present causal test addresses load changes and
line openings with restoration. The other panels provide con-
text, not additional validated event classes.

IV. RECOVERY-AWARE PHYSICAL ATTRIBUTION
Figure 1 gives the report-level logic. A history is a proposed
cause and trajectory, not just a bus label. The inference bank
predicts the next report under no new event, a new load or
line action, or retained recovery. Its switched predictors come
from the network DAE and oriented PMU terminal map.
Statistical filtering handles uncertain states and residual error.
The nonlinear simulator generates the observations; inference
uses local predictors, not a new full simulation for every
hypothesis and report.

F. EMIT SEPARATE DETECTION, DIAGNOSIS AND
RESTORATION MESSAGES
The internal observer proposes an alarm after six reports
above the 0.99 active-mass threshold. Public alarm publica-
tion additionally requires an internal pending episode and six
reports of qualified active support: the frozen source screen
qualifies load cells, while admitted line cells qualify through
their switched operator. Public OFF requires six reports above
0.99 for the diagnosed cell’s joint cause-and-restoration mass.
The observer also has a separate, more conservative 15-report
internal OFF clock. Diagnosis selects the family with greatest
summed cause mass and then the highest-mass source within
that family. It is emitted after six reports of stable cause
support or at the 15-report post-alarm deadline. The supple-
ment defines the source screen, early line rule and episode
replacement, including how internal messages are propagated
publicly. Every message records emission time and the last
report used. A diagnosis can be correct eventually but too late
to precede physical OFF.
The nominal comparator uses the same nominal condi-
tional model with its own fixed publication path. Compar-
ing nominal and mixture evaluates two complete configured
procedures; it does not isolate the outer network bank from
publication decisions. The admission-rule ablation tests a dif-
ferent publication decision; it is not a pure recovery-memory
ablation.

G. PER-REPORT ALGORITHM AND COMPUTATIONAL
BOUNDARY
Algorithm 1: one causal update. Input: nominal model,
fixed method configuration and PMU reports. Initialize con-
text, reference, noise and conditional states from the 300
startup reports. Event truth is never an input.
1) Transform report k; predict it from each retained phys-
ical or recovery history. Save each network model’s
predictive density before screening or correcting with
this report.
2) Screen onsets in the latest 18 reports. Start each pro-
posed history from its stored pre-onset state and replay
its suffix, replacing the old suffix likelihood once.
3) Score histories, integrating load severity or using the
switched line operator. Then correct state and severity,
update network weights, and prune by the fixed rules.
Retain state and cause through restoration.
4) Aggregate active mass M, cause mass Γ and ∆M.
Apply the frozen alarm, source and OFF rules; record
emission time, estimated onset and last report used.
The nonlinear simulator constructs observations; this algo-
rithm propagates local switched predictors. The input grid is
30 reports/s. Causality concerns report availability; acquisi-
tion throughput, computation time and the electrical history
needed for a decision are separate quantities. Wall-clock real-
time operation has not been established.

VI. EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN
The test asks whether one method can attribute six successive
load or line interventions in a continuous trajectory, including
an intervention that begins while the preceding response is
still recovering. The plant is a fixed Julia/PowerDynamics
IEEE 39-bus research candidate. Each parent is integrated
once across all ON and OFF actions; differential states are not
reset. PMU reports are exported at 30 frames/s, independently
of solver steps. A switch-aligned report uses the post-switch
algebraic value. RAW0001 informed the simulator retrospec-
tively and is not an independent test record.
The sealed evaluation has 18 six-event continuous parents,
27 persistent line-opening parents and 12 normal parents.
Every parent is replayed under matched R/X, an R/X change
represented in the model bank and an R/X change outside it.
Two instrumental-noise scales give 171 physical trajectories
and 342 observed records. The 648 continuous event realiza-
tions repeat 108 interventions; the physical parent, rather than
the PMU frame or replay, is the uncertainty unit. The earlier
development examples in Fig. 8 are not part of this sealed
comparison.
A. WHAT THE FEATURE BASELINE ACTUALLY DOES
The hackathon submission was a hierarchical detector, event
classifier and typed localizer based on ExtraTrees. It summa-
rized each 30-s PMU window with voltage/current magnitude
and angle statistics, frequency, ROCOF, missingness, phase
spread, approximate P/Q, rolling changes, RLS/Kalman level
innovations and graph-temporal differences. Its selected bun-
dle reports 45,162 input features and internal simulated-
validation macro-F1 0.960 with exact localization 0.852.
These numbers describe that archive’s broader nine-label,
window-level task; they are not scores on the present event-
level cohort. The original submission is preserved.
To compare at 30 reports/s, an earlier development cam-
paign built two new, frozen causal ExtraTrees adapters from
47 PMU coordinates. Each coordinate is centered and ro-
bustly scaled using the quiet prefix. For trailing windows of 1,
5 and 10 s, the reference adapter computes median, standard
deviation, 10th/90th percentiles, a recent-quarter change and
RMS first difference. This gives 47 × 3 × 6 = 846 features
per report. The short-window adapter adds the current value
and mean, standard deviation and two lag changes at 3, 8 and
15 reports, giving 1,457 features in total. Both use 400 trees,
balanced class weights, 19 classes (quiet plus 18 assets), and
six-report confirmation; tree votes are scores, not calibrated
event probabilities. Their training parents precede and are
disjoint from the sealed cohort. Neither model was retrained
or threshold-adjusted on these outcomes. The original split
contains 32 local physical IDs for fitting (64 records, 78,784
training rows) and eight for validation (16 records). A later
dependency audit maps these to 26 fitting and eight validation
groups, with two normal-control groups shared across those
sets. This limits independence of the internal validation; it
is not evidence of leakage into the separate confirmatory
cohort. The reference and short-window classifiers already
obtain only 0/72 and 4/72 timely load attributions in that
development validation. Their frozen performance therefore
does not represent a successfully optimized load classifier.
A later grouped retraining study uses 31 fit, eight valida-
tion and 17 reserved physical groups: approximately 70/30
development/reserved, with an inner validation partition. That
model is a separate artifact, not either comparator executed
in the confirmatory study. Randomly splitting PMU reports
70/30 would allow correlated trajectories to cross partitions.
RAW0001 is a known retrospective transfer check, not the re-
served 30% or an external test. The supplement distinguishes
these three evaluation contracts.
The sealed methods are the five-model physical bank, its
nominal-network version and these two causal ExtraTrees
adapters. All receive the same 30-frame/s PMU stream and
retain their own frozen decision rules. This tests complete
procedures at their declared operating points, not a matched-
false-alarm or unlimited-supervision comparison. The physi-
cal banks also have different publication rules, so their con-
trast does not isolate network-model averaging.
Chen et al.’s published quickest-change detector [2] is
eligible only for persistent line openings and normal records.
The declared adapter uses nonoverlapping 15-Hz angle in-
crements, a nominal single-line catalog and prefix-estimated
drift and scale. It stops at its first alarm. Its DC and white-
injection assumptions do not fully describe the simulator’s
colored PMU noise; no published average-run-length guar-
antee is transferred to these records.

before inspecting its source. A timely decision requires the

stop is scored as correct, premature, wrong-source or cen-

VII. DEVELOPMENT COMPARISONS AND WORKED
SEQUENCE
A. DIRECT TRANSFER OF THE SUBMITTED HACKATHON
SYSTEM
Before the confirmatory continuous study, the unchanged
submitted ZIP and an earlier physical estimator were exe-
cuted on the same 124 windows: 96 event realizations and
28 normal windows. Figure 6 preserves their actual family
confusions. The submitted ExtraTrees pipeline detected 32 of
the 96 events, assigned the right family to 23, and the right
family and exact source to 0. The earlier physical estimator
obtained 96/96 for all three quantities. Neither alarmed in the
28 normal windows. The source result was checked against
the submission’s output CSVs, not inferred from the family
matrix.
This comparison exposes a transfer failure of the delivered
system on the later simulator’s load/line catalog. It does not
show an official hackathon-score improvement. The submit-
ted model retains its broader training domain and 30-s win-
dow rule, whereas the physical estimator knows the simulator
and its 12 candidate origins. These were development cases,
and the comparison permits both methods the same window
endpoint. The next study therefore uses separately trained
causal ExtraTrees comparators, successive events and a new
frozen cohort. Its results must be read as a second experiment,
not as more independent repetitions of the perfect develop-
ment score.
B. FOLLOWING SUCCESSIVE EVENTS THROUGH
RECOVERY
The example in Fig. 8 follows six physical interventions with-
out restarting the network. With matched R/X, all six sources
are identified before their OFF times. Under a network change
outside the bank, an extra bus-20 diagnosis precedes the real
bus-31 load; bus 31 is then diagnosed only after its physical
restoration. The lower panel shows the mechanism visible to
the method: active bus-31 mass rises late in the mismatched
record. This is one development trajectory, not a performance
estimate.
The development controls also show that deleting alarms
is easy if true events may be lost (Fig. 7). The stricter onset
witness has no extras but loses a real bus-16 intervention. The
frozen five-model procedure was therefore evaluated with its
original publication rule; no rule was revised from the sealed
outcomes.
C. COMPARISON WITH A SIMPLER PHYSICAL
PROCEDURE
An earlier development control uses the same nominal physi-
cal and PMU operators, one retained state and a short general-
ized likelihood-ratio search. With a common six-report publi-
cation clock, it obtains 37/54 timely decisions and 37 extras;
the history mixture obtains 51/54 with 3. This is an executed
physical comparator, separate from the later confirmatory co-
hort. Different search, severity treatment and score semantics
prevent it from being a pure Bayes or memory ablation. Its
equations and full table are included in the supplement.

Small distance makes location noise-sensitive; it is neither a

why correct location and inaccurate response amplitude can

B. WHAT RECOVERY MEMORY ESTABLISHES
A separate full-DAE control supplies the earlier history and
later onset. Source separation stays essentially unchanged
when that history is known and falls when prior severity is
uncertain; binary source accuracy has no mean gain. Retain-
ing recovery is therefore justified as a consistent prediction of
the continuing response, not as a universal increase in location
information. The stream comparison tests the complete frozen
procedure. Its admission-rule ablation does not isolate every
operation that retains recovery.

C. WHAT THE COMPARISON DOES NOT ESTABLISH
Trajectory libraries, sequential likelihoods and multiple-
model filters precede this work. The claim here is the speci-
fied competition of new interventions with retained recovery,
and its measured behavior, rather than a general filtering
optimum. Warner’s trajectory procedure was not run on this
cohort. The feature baseline’s trailing summaries describe
how signals changed, but do not encode the counterfactual
response of each candidate source through the network and
PMU terminal map. That is a concrete difference in infor-
mation used by the algorithms and is consistent with the
observed load gap; these experiments do not prove that a
better feature set or larger training set could not close it.
The original hackathon submission addressed nine labels with
30-s decisions. It is a useful earlier result and a source of
feature design, but its internal validation score cannot be com-
pared numerically with event-level timely attribution here.
The two causal ExtraTrees adapters were trained separately
for this task; their load misses do not establish a new official
hackathon score or rule out better supervised models. Chen’s
first-stop adapter is tested only on persistent lines under DC
and white-injection assumptions.
D. BROADER HACKATHON SCOPE AND RETROSPECTIVE
TRANSFER
The earlier all-family pipeline and integrity interface are
retained as separate results. An archived observation-only
composition reproduces all 180 physical/integrity windows
and reconstructs availability over 161379 reports; this verifies
interface consistency, not additional detection accuracy. On
retrospectively annotated RAW0001 windows, a source-only
learned hybrid changes joint family/source correctness from
1/24 to 2/24, with one gain and no losses. It is not the phys-
ical bank and does not establish broad transfer. The frozen
physical bank rejects that record because the prefix ROCOF
noise scale is outside its admitted support; no later quiet prefix
was substituted to force admission. The supplement preserves
these outcomes alongside the simulation results.
E. SIMULATION AND DEPLOYMENT BOUNDARY
The causal catalog has nine load buses and nine lines, with
a quiet startup. Missing PMUs, unknown families, unre-
stricted simultaneous sources, unseen-source transfer and
field records remain untested. The generating plant and in-
ference predictors share the F01 equation family and PMU
map. The three R/X conditions perturb parameters within that
family; they do not test a different producer, control law or
instrument installation. Thus the high within-simulator hit
rate is not evidence of the same rate on RAW0001 or a field
network. The simulator keeps a provisional BUS5 current
terminal and omits BUS39 current angle. Its noise includes
temporal and cross-PMU dependence within signal families
but omits the archived cross-family factor; frequency/ROCOF
noise is added after phase-based estimation as separate signal
families. It is not obtained by differentiating the exported
noisy angle, and the archived cross-family noise factor is not
applied. Hence agreement with this simulator does not verify
the source producer’s CT placement or a complete physical
PMU installation.
Out-of-bank R/X preserves most timely source decisions
but sharply raises unmatched alarms. The parent-cluster in-
tervals describe these simulated parents, not all grids or
PMU layouts. Context fitting, history pruning and shared
covariance approximations also prevent a calibrated-posterior
claim. Report-causal operation has not been timed as a real-
time service; the implementation is not validated for protec-
tion or control.
XI. CONCLUSION
The proposed procedure attributes load and line interven-
tions by comparing their predicted PMU responses with the
observed stream. Each candidate starts from an estimated
past state. Restoration changes its input or topology while
its physical state continues to evolve. The method integrates
local load severity and reports event activity separately from
the cause of a continuing response.
The sealed continuous evaluation gives 644/648 timely
family/source decisions with 72 extra alarms for the mixture,
and 643/648 with 92 for nominal. The frozen ExtraTrees
reference gives 330/648 with 29 extras. Most mixture extras
arise under out-of-bank R/X, so the gain in timely attribution
does not imply uniformly better alarm control.
The component experiments preserve useful earlier find-
ings: network conditioning recovers hidden voltage informa-
tion, graph-temporal features improve a historical learned
localizer, and mixed-source terms reduce local response error.
They do not establish an isolated causal gain from Bayes or
tensor storage in the final estimator.
The earlier hackathon pipeline remains a broader nine-
label, feature-based system. Its 30-s-window validation is a
separate result; the causal ExtraTrees successor in this paper
provides the direct same-stream comparison. The physical
bank’s strongest supported gain is timely load-source attri-
bution, while learned features already diagnose most lines in
this catalog with fewer unmatched alarms.
The evidence concerns a specified load/line catalog in one
simulator. Unmodeled R/X increases the alarm burden. The
controlled component study addresses amplitude scoring and
state carry at inferred closure; it does not remove all Bayesian
estimation or all temporal memory. All-family diagnosis,
calibrated event probabilities, field transfer and real-time de-
ployment remain outside the demonstrated scope.
A source-excluded comparison would test whether the pro-
cedure generalizes beyond its supported catalog. A learned
correction of physical residuals could address model mis-
match, provided it is evaluated independently of source-label
fitting. Joint state/context uncertainty and event-probability
calibration also require further validation.
