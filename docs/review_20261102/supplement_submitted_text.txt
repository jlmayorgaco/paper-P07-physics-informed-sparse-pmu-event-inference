ABSTRACT This supplement gives the executable statistical contract of the causal physical-history method:
local DAE reduction, candidate transitions, truncated-severity evidence, state updates, restoration, worked
examples, and scenario-level reproduction. It also defines the measurement-integrity interface and separates
causal alarm, diagnosis and restoration metrics from the historical hackathon window evaluation. Earlier
reconstruction, batch-source and proposed joint-estimator studies are preserved in the separately compiled
Historical Technical Notes. Their results do not enter the causal method’s denominators.

APPENDIX A HOW TO READ AND REPRODUCE THE
CURRENT METHOD
This companion begins with the recovery-aware causal
method used in the current article. Sections A–C give its
execution order, local derivations, worked examples and sim-
ulation contracts. The separately compiled Historical Tech-
nical Notes preserve full diagnosability proofs, earlier state-
reconstruction and batch-estimator studies, and secondary
evidence. The earlier studies support particular components;
they are not additional tests of the complete causal algorithm.1
The article retains the physical model, the defining evi-
dence integral, the recovery mechanism, the decision outputs
and decisive results. This supplement supplies the interme-
diate calculations. The Historical Technical Notes develop
functional observability, nuisance-profiled information and
source ambiguity, and distinguish the implemented batch esti-
mator EB from the proposed joint estimator EJ. Neither should
be silently substituted for the causal history bank, archived as
R11. The conditional Gaussian updates use standard filtering
identities [2]; the derivation below specifies their role and the
approximations made by the implemented bank.

A. INPUTS, OUTPUTS AND THE INFORMATION
BOUNDARY
The inputs are successive reports from eight PMUs, the fixed
physical and instrumental operators, the admitted load/line
catalog and the frozen method configuration. The out-
put is a timestamped sequence of detection, family/source
and restoration messages, together with retained attribution
masses. True ON/OFF times, true sources and generating
severities belong to the evaluator. They are not inputs to the
causal algorithm.
Three times must remain distinct: the latest observed re-
port, the estimated physical onset, and the public emission
time. Looking back to propose an onset is compatible with
causality when the message is emitted only after the required
reports arrive. It does not permit backdating the public alarm.

D. FROZEN NUMERICAL CHOICES
The input grid is 30 reports/s. The five network hypotheses
are nominal, global ±2% and selected-branch ±3% paired
R/X perturbations. Each hypothesis is fixed in time; the
weights change. They do not identify a continuous impedance
posterior.
The per-report birth and restoration hazards are 1 −
exp[−(1/60)/30] and 1−exp[−(1/30)/30]. Birth mass is di-
vided equally between load and line families, then uniformly
among the admitted sources within each family. Load sever-
ity has a zero-mean Gaussian prior with standard deviation
0.15, truncated to [−0.2, 0.2] and integrated over intervals
bounded by −0.2, −0.1, 0, 0.1, 0.2. After three reports, eli-
gible active histories can create OFF children at either half-
report position, inheriting state and severity. After likelihood
normalization, the beam retains the union of the 96 highest-
weight recent histories, 64 older histories and at least one per
occupied source. The union is renormalized.
The internal observer confirms active-mass evidence at
0.99 for six reports and has a separate 15-report internal
OFF clock. The public R11 publisher requires an internal
pending episode and six reports of source-qualified active
support above 0.99; its public OFF requires six reports of
the diagnosed cell’s joint cause-and-restoration mass above
0.99. Source/line confirmation and episode replacement fol-
low the archived publication configuration. These thresholds
are implementation settings, not guarantees of probability
calibration or false-alarm control. Nominal and mixture use
their frozen publication paths, so their comparison does not
isolate network averaging alone. The admission-rule ablation
changes publication, not the recovery dynamics.

APPENDIX D MEASUREMENT INTEGRITY AND THE
HISTORICAL SUBMISSION INTERFACE
An electrical intervention and a measurement failure are
separate objects. The physical output identifies a family and
asset; the integrity output identifies missing or corrupt PMU
observations. Missingness alone does not establish malicious
intent. A concurrent physical event and missing PMU can
therefore have two different locations.4
The successor interface reuses the fitted detector, classi-
fier and localizer from the actual submitted archive, rather
than a similarly named exploratory pipeline. Archive mem-
ber hashes are checked before inference. The input schema
admits timestamps, three-phase voltage/current magnitudes
and angles, frequency, ROCOF and the observed availability
flag. Event labels, locations and annotated ON/OFF times are
excluded. The fitted feature lists are also checked for label
fields. This distinction matters because a historical diagnostic
utility estimated a baseline from labeled normal samples over
the complete record; that utility is not part of this inference
interface.
The submitted classifier uses a physical-family head, a
bad-data head and a missing/composition head with fixed
rules. Its E5 and E7 outputs refer to missing and bad-data
PMUs, respectively. E6 denotes missing data together with a
physical prediction; its legacy location is the physical source,
not the missing PMU. In the archived configuration, both
composition and physical-confidence thresholds are zero, so
E6 requires a nonzero physical prediction in a window with
missing data; it is not a calibrated test of concurrent causation.
E8 is the legacy mixed-physical proxy class, not an open-set
rejection guarantee.
The availability interface evaluates each received report
without looking ahead. It retains the affected PMU and chan-
nel mask rather than replacing missing measurements with
future samples. The historical bad-data classifier uses a com-
plete window and reports its result at the end. Within-window
interpolation and centered features are permitted only under
that delayed decision contract. Broadcasting the result to
earlier timestamps does not make those earlier labels causal.

APPENDIX E ALARM, DIAGNOSIS AND RESTORATION
ARE DIFFERENT DECISIONS
Let τ and ω be true ON and OFF, and let tA, tD and tR
denote the emission times of an alarm, source diagnosis and
restoration. The detector can signal before it has a source. An
eventual correct source at tD > tA is not a correct location
already available at tA. Timely attribution requires a correct
family and source at tD < ω. Estimated onset times never
replace emission times in a delay metric.5
An event is associated with the first eligible unmatched
alarm using time, before inspecting the predicted label. In-
correct labels remain associated errors rather than being re-
assigned to another event to improve the score. An alarm
without a diagnosis, a wrong diagnosis, and no alarm are
distinct outcomes. Restricted delay includes failures up to
the evaluation horizon; conditional delay among successes is
reported separately.
A restoration of an unmatched episode and a premature
restoration of a matched event are also different errors. With
a tolerance δ fixed before scoring, premature restoration is
tR < ω −δ; its lost active duration is [ω −tR]+. Signed
restoration delay is tR −ω. Report its distribution, missing
closures and episodes still open at the horizon; a median alone
can hide early closures.

APPENDIX F RETROSPECTIVE RAW COMPARISON AND
INDEPENDENT UNITS
The archived RAW0001 table contains 21 contexts, includ-
ing nine labeled-normal and twelve abnormal contexts. Its
recovered loader places a 30-s window three seconds before
a supplied abnormal onset, and selects normal contexts from
supplied normal intervals. Even a successful reproduction
would measure classification and localization within event-
guided contexts, not continuous detection. However, the re-
covered calendar is incompatible with the event annotations
in the actual RAW files. File-history checks show that those
RAW files have not changed, and the timing differences
cannot be corrected by a common offset. The archived score is
therefore not a reproduced comparison endpoint. The unsuc-
cessful reconstruction and its provenance audit are retained;
predictions from mismatched windows are not counted as
accuracy against the archived labels.6
A separate complete-record replay uses fixed windows
without event-guided placement and retains all normal, phys-
ical, missing and corrupt periods. Both interfaces preserve
availability failures and abstentions. Physical and integrity
locations are scored separately where their meanings differ;
the legacy single-label export is retained for comparison.
Physical-source scoring additionally requires verified source
annotations; an event-code sequence alone cannot identify
every intervention boundary or source. The separately sealed
successor analysis uses the recovered original source work-
book for physical identity, retaining current event-code sup-
port for window membership. Its coarse timing does not re-
solve the individual actuation or restoration times. This replay
is not an official competition score. RAW0001 previously in-
formed F01 calibration, so even a frozen new replay remains
retrospective.
Simulation partitions use the complete physical parent and
all of its derived windows, conditions and noise realizations.
Validation belongs inside the development fraction. Repar-
titioning previously inspected campaigns does not restore
independent-test status. Uncertainty calculations resample
physical parents, retaining their paired conditions together.
Different PMU channels and adjacent 30-Hz reports are not
independent trials.
