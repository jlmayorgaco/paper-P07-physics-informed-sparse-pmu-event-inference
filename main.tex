\begin{abstract}
Sparse PMU measurements can reveal a disturbance without uniquely
identifying its cause. We study how network equations and conditional
Bayesian evidence complement feature-based classification for this task.
A bank of switched differential-algebraic predictors compares source,
onset, severity and restoration histories, retaining electrical and
instrumental memory between interventions. Local severity integration and
fixed-network model averaging produce separate active-event and
originating-cause masses. Component experiments examine hidden-voltage
reconstruction, engineered-feature localization and mixed-source response
sensitivities; these are distinguished from the final causal estimator.
In a frozen IEEE 39-bus comparison with eight PMUs, 18 six-event schedules
are replayed under three network conditions and two noise levels. The
physical bank makes \COneMixTimely{}/\COneMixTotal{} timely family/source
decisions with \COneMixExtras{} extra alarms, compared with
\COneETTimely{}/\COneETTotal{} and \COneETExtras{} for a causal ExtraTrees
reference. Its strongest gain is load-source attribution; line attribution
is similar to the short-window learned comparator. Most extra physical
alarms occur under network changes outside the model bank. A separate
historical transfer experiment retains the original hackathon submission
and its confusion matrix. The results identify a useful coverage--alarm
trade-off within the simulated catalog. Transfer to a different network
model, calibration of event probabilities and the separate contribution
of each inference component require further evidence.
\end{abstract}
