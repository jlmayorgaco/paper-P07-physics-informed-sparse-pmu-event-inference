  pdfsubject={Causal physical event attribution, recovery, and source resolution from sparse PMUs},

\begin{abstract}
After a load is restored or a line is reclosed, the machines keep moving,
and the residual response can resemble a new intervention. We formulate
load/line attribution from eight PMUs in the IEEE 39-bus system as change
detection and isolation with switched differential-algebraic (DAE)
post-change models. A bank of linearized DAE predictors compares
intervention and recovery histories that start from estimated past
states, integrates load severity in closed form, and keeps a restored
intervention available as the cause of the continuing response. The same
operators give Willsky--Jones signatures, from which we compute, before
any experiment, each source's detection and isolation information, its
minimum detectable change, the cost of forgetting the physical state at
restoration, and the information left when a PMU is
missing or corrupted. In an independent simulation (18
physical parents, 108 interventions, 648 paired replays) the bank
attributes \COneMixTimely{}/\COneMixTotal{} interventions before
restoration with no alarm in 11.8\,h of normal records; resetting the
state lowers timely attribution from 108/108 to 75/108. With unchanged
code it tolerates twentyfold noise, but not independent per-PMU noise of
0.05\% TVE.
Sequence-parity and network-coupling relations, which no balanced
physical event can violate, diagnose every labeled missing and corrupted
PMU episode of the competition record at its first report, identify the
corrupted phase and reveal six unlabeled integrity incidents. Without
training, a network-physics attributor then detects, classifies and
locates all 12 labeled events of every type on that record; in a stored
simulated test it misses most short load pulses, which the bank attributes.
\end{abstract}
