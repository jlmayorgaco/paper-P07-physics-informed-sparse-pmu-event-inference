\begin{abstract}
Sparse PMUs can recover a requested voltage field while leaving the physical interventions that produced it ambiguous. We formulate these tasks with a hybrid differential-algebraic model that separates physical interventions from measurement integrity, and we distinguish functional state observability from finite-horizon event-source diagnosability. The evaluated load-intervention likelihood assigns posterior mass to event cardinality, support, and signed amplitude over a closed bank of \GlobalHypothesisCount{} hypotheses: no event, 16 single sources, and all 120 two-source supports. Its quadratic response manifold includes source self-curvature and pairwise interaction. On \GlobalPhysicalTrajectories{} fresh nonlinear trajectories and \GlobalNoiseRecords{} frozen noise realizations, exact support Top-1 is \GlobalWeakTopOne{} for weak--weak pairs, \GlobalWeakStrongTopOne{} for weak--strong pairs, \GlobalModerateTopOne{} for moderate pairs, and \GlobalFiniteTopOne{} for finite pairs. Cardinality accuracy in the weak--weak regime is \GlobalWeakCardinality{}, showing that detecting two active sources can precede identifying them. A separate causal estimator reconstructs 31 hidden bus voltages from eight voltage/current PMUs with \BTwoTVE{} mean error, while physical recentering removes \EZeroHClosureHalf{}--\EZeroHClosureMax{} of an operating-point error gap. Retrospective geometry links profiled distance to weak-case support recovery, but the 120-frame diagnostic remains limited to one operating point. The evidence supports matched-simulator state reconstruction and load-intervention inference, not a validated multi-family joint estimator, changing PMU sets, unknown onset, or field transfer.
\end{abstract}
