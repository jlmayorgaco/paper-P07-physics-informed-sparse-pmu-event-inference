\begin{abstract}
Sparse phasor measurement unit (PMU) networks can observe the consequences of a power-system disturbance without directly observing its physical source. This paper develops a research framework for physics-informed Bayesian event inference and localization under sparse, time-varying PMU observation. Rather than treating an event as a fixed source label, the framework represents it as a physical intervention in a differential-algebraic network model and separates that intervention from measurement-integrity states. It combines causal innovation evidence, physical-hypothesis screening, nonlinear refinement, a source-agnostic learned discrepancy likelihood, and posterior candidate sets that permit an exact source decision or an explicit unresolved result. A dynamic diagnosability measure relates the presently available PMUs to pairwise hypothesis separation and source ambiguity. The manuscript also specifies a conditional concept for transferring operator-confirmed physical-event knowledge across PMU nodes through the network model rather than copying local waveforms. The planned evaluation covers held-out-source generalization, physics-only versus hybrid inference, PMU join and loss, cross-simulator and operating-condition shifts, continuous causal scanning, and controlled knowledge transfer. Quantitative claims are intentionally deferred until these experiments are completed and frozen.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
Bayesian inference, diagnosability, event localization, phasor measurement units, power-system dynamics, sparse observability.
\end{keywords}
