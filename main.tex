\begin{abstract}
Sparse phasor measurement unit (PMU) deployments leave much of the network voltage field unmeasured, and accurate reconstruction can fail when the estimator's physical model becomes stale. This paper formulates recovery of unobserved complex bus voltages as a functional-observability problem and evaluates physics-based local Gaussian estimators on an audited PowerDynamics IEEE 39-bus benchmark. \ObservedPMUCount{} PMUs observe voltage and terminal-current phasors while the target comprises the remaining \HiddenBusCount{} bus voltages. On \NominalTestCount{} frozen nonlinear test trajectories, a causal Kalman estimator reduces mean hidden-bus total vector error (TVE) from \BZeroTVE{} for the nominal-equilibrium baseline to \BTwoTVE{}; a snapshot weighted least-squares estimator reaches \BOneTVE{}. A preregistered functional-observability residual correlates with per-bus Kalman TVE (Spearman $\rho=\FunctionalSpearman$), whereas fixed-lag smoothing provides no improvement over zero lag in the tested regime. A separate campaign rebuilds \MismatchMainCount{} nonlinear plants over seven physical mismatch families and adds \MismatchRefinementCount{} refinement cases. At the largest tested scale, network and operating-point mismatch increase median TVE by factors of \NetworkMismatchRatio{} and \OperatingMismatchRatio{}, respectively. Oracle recentering and relinearization recover most of this excess error, but a causal offset-only recentering proxy closes a median \OnlineClosurePercent{} of the oracle gap. The evidence therefore identifies stale physical centering and linearization, rather than PMU count alone, as the dominant limitation under the tested shifts and motivates stronger leakage-safe physical adaptation before residual learning or event inference.
\end{abstract}
