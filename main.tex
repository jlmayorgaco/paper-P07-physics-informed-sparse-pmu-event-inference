\begin{abstract}
A load can return to its original setpoint, or a line can reclose, while
the generators are still responding to the first switch. We estimate
whether the next phasor measurements follow that recovery or identify a
new intervention. A bank of switched differential-algebraic predictors
carries the state through restoration and integrates over the unknown
size of each candidate load change. We derive the minimum mean-square
state estimate under each local model and the one-report prediction cost
of resetting its physical coordinates. On the IEEE 39-bus system with
eight phasor measurement units, the bank named the correct family and
asset before restoration for \RecBankTimely{} simulated interventions.
A continuous generalized likelihood ratio observer attributed
\RecGlrTimely{} and ExtraTrees attributed \RecTreesTimely{}. With thresholds
calibrated to the same normal-alarm budget, the three procedures issued
\RecBankExtras{}, \RecGlrExtras{} and \RecTreesExtras{} unmatched alarms
during event records. A second study tested what to retain at closure.
After short gaps, retaining recovery histories, carrying their mean and
resetting the physical state yielded \CThreeFOneHistoryShortTimely{},
\CThreeFOneCarryShortTimely{} and \CThreeFOneResetShortTimely{} timely
attributions out of 144 on the original plant. On a plant with different
dynamics the counts were \CThreePTwoHistoryShortTimely{},
\CThreePTwoCarryShortTimely{} and \CThreePTwoResetShortTimely{}. Keeping
the physical state improved attribution on both plants. The additional
coverage from retaining histories was \CThreePTwoHistoryCarryDifferencePP{}
percentage points on the second plant (paired 95\% interval
$[\CThreePTwoHistoryCarryLowerPP,\CThreePTwoHistoryCarryUpperPP]$).
\end{abstract}
