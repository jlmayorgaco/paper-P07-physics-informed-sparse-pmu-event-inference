\begin{abstract}
When a load is restored or a line is reclosed, the electrical
response can persist and resemble a new intervention. We formulate
source attribution from eight phasor measurement units (PMUs) in the
IEEE 39-bus system using switched differential-algebraic predictors.
The bank carries the estimated state through restoration and retains
the preceding intervention as a candidate explanation of the remaining
response. Nominal signatures describe separation under the assumed
plant and noise model. In held-out simulation at a common calibration
budget, the bank correctly attributed the family and source of
\RecBankTimely{} interventions before physical restoration,
compared with \RecGlrTimely{} for a continuous
generalized likelihood ratio observer and \RecTreesTimely{} for
ExtraTrees. The bank gained \RecGlrDiff{} percentage points over the
continuous observer (95\% interval \RecGlrCI{} across \RecParents{}
physical parents) and emitted \RecBankExtras{} unmatched alarms,
compared with \RecGlrExtras{} and \RecTreesExtras{}, respectively.
No method emitted a normal alarm in \RecNormalHours{} monitored hours.
At fixed thresholds in a separate control, resetting the estimated
physical state reduced timely attribution from 108/108 to 75/108.
Independent per-PMU noise at 0.05\% total vector error degraded load
attribution. These results describe sequential, nonoverlapping
interventions in a known catalog on one simulator family.
\end{abstract}
