  pdfsubject={Causal physical event attribution, recovery, and source resolution from sparse PMUs},

\begin{abstract}
After a load is restored or a line is reclosed, the remaining transient
can resemble a new intervention. We use eight phasor measurement units
(PMUs) and switched differential-algebraic predictors of the IEEE 39-bus
system to compare a new event against the predicted recovery. A correct
attribution names the event family and source before restoration.
At a calibration target of one normal alarm per hour, the bank correctly
attributed \RecBankTimely{} interventions, compared with \RecGlrTimely{}
for a continuous generalized likelihood ratio observer and
\RecTreesTimely{} for ExtraTrees. The paired gain over the continuous
observer was \RecGlrDiff{} percentage points (95\% interval \RecGlrCI{}
across \RecParents{} physical parents). The three procedures emitted
\RecBankExtras{}, \RecGlrExtras{} and \RecTreesExtras{} unmatched alarms
during event records, respectively. None emitted a normal alarm in
\RecNormalHours{} monitored hours per method. In a separate fixed-threshold
control, resetting the estimated physical state reduced timely attribution
from 108/108 to 75/108. These results quantify the coverage and alarm
trade-off for sequential, nonoverlapping interventions from a known catalog
in one simulator family. Independent per-PMU noise reduced load coverage.
\end{abstract}
