\begin{abstract}
Sparse phasor measurement unit (PMU) coverage couples three questions that are often evaluated separately: what is happening in the unobserved network, which event occurred, and where it originated. We formulate these questions as joint state, physical-intervention, and measurement-integrity inference in a hybrid differential-algebraic model. The formulation separates physical source hypotheses from missing or corrupted measurements and introduces two target-specific limits: functional observability of hidden voltages and projected pairwise information for event-source diagnosability. Evidence comes from two frozen IEEE 39-bus campaigns using the same eight PMU buses but different simulators and contracts. On 100 PowerDynamics nonlinear test trajectories, a causal Kalman estimator reduces mean hidden-bus total vector error from \BZeroTVE{} to \BTwoTVE{}; a preregistered functional residual correlates with bus-level error ($\rho=\FunctionalSpearman$). A measurement-only static physical MAP closes \EZeroHClosureHalf{}--\EZeroHClosureMax{} of the nominal-center error gap on 60 operating-point-shifted test plants despite nuisance-information rank \EZeroHRank{}/\EZeroHDimension{}. On a separate 690-trajectory ANDES event bank, a physics-guided hierarchical baseline attains detection F1 \LegacyEventDetectionFOne{}, event macro-F1 \LegacyEventMacroFOne{}, and physical-source Top-1 \LegacyPhysicalTopOne{} for sources represented during training. Under complete source holdout, however, only \HeldSourceCorrect{} of \HeldSourceDecisions{} decisions are correct. These results establish the state-reconstruction component and expose source memorization as the binding failure; they do not yet validate the proposed end-to-end joint estimator.
\end{abstract}
