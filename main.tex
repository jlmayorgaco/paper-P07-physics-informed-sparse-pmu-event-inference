\begin{abstract}
Recovering unmeasured bus voltages and identifying a disturbance source are different inverse problems: sparse PMUs may determine a voltage function while leaving two sources indistinguishable. We separate them with a hybrid differential-algebraic model and specialize functional observability and descriptor-model distinguishability to the requested decisions. Three frozen IEEE 39-bus contracts test distinct parts of the formulation. A causal PowerDynamics estimator reaches \BTwoTVE{} mean hidden-voltage error, and measurement-only physical recentering removes \EZeroHClosureHalf{}--\EZeroHClosureMax{} of the operating-point error gap. A matched, known-onset, fixed-topology experiment ranks a closed bank of 16 load sources without source-labeled training. Source Top-1 falls from 100\% for a 0.4\% load change to 13.0\% at 0.01\%, although the latter remains detectable; source-wise response information is associated with the discretized 90\%-detection threshold ($\rho=\LoadEVIRho{}$, $n=16$). In a separate ANDES bank, a supervised hierarchy localizes only \HeldSourceCorrect{} of \HeldSourceDecisions{} decisions after complete source holdout. The results separate state recoverability, event detectability, source resolution, and learned transfer. They do not validate multi-family nuisance-projected localization, unknown onset, open-bank events, changing PMU sets, ambient load variation, or field transfer.
\end{abstract}
