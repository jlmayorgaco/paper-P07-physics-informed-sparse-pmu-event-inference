\begin{abstract}
A line can reclose while generators are still responding to its opening.
If a phasor measurement unit (PMU) then records another change in
current, continued recovery and a new intervention are competing
explanations. We predict the next PMU report under both by carrying
the estimated machine and instrument states through restoration. A
multiple-hypothesis filter integrates over load severity and sums the
weights of switch histories by asset. Its posterior mean minimizes
squared state error within the retained local model. We calibrate the
alarm threshold on separate records. On the simulated IEEE 39-bus system
with eight PMUs, the filter identified the family and asset before
restoration in \RecBankTimely{} cases, compared with \RecGlrTimely{} for a
continuous generalized likelihood ratio observer and \RecTreesTimely{}
for a streaming ExtraTrees adapter. They issued \RecBankExtras{},
\RecGlrExtras{} and \RecTreesExtras{} unmatched alarms over 0.54
monitored event-hours. Carrying the estimated state improved timely
attribution over resetting it on two plants. Retaining separate
histories gave a further gain on the reference plant, with little
observed change on the plant with different machine dynamics. In a
separate four-family test on a reduced plant, the recovery bank
identified \RepBankJoint{}/420 family--asset pairs within 1.5~s and
issued \RepBankExtras{} unmatched alarms. Independent PMU noise
prevented startup of the original two-family filter on both plants.
\end{abstract}
