\begin{abstract}
A line can reclose while generators are still responding to its opening.
A monitor that observes another change in current must distinguish a new
intervention from the continuing response to the first. We address this
problem with a bank of switched network models. Each model predicts the
next phasor measurement unit (PMU) report from the state reached before
the switch. For a load change, the model averages over its unknown size.
We derive the conditional minimum mean-square state estimate for each
history and quantify the one-report increase in expected log loss caused by a
physical-state reset. On the simulated IEEE 39-bus system with eight
PMUs, the bank identified the correct event family and asset before
restoration in \RecBankTimely{} cases. A continuous generalized
likelihood ratio observer achieved \RecGlrTimely{} and an ExtraTrees
classifier achieved \RecTreesTimely{}. Over 0.54 monitored event-hours
the bank, observer and classifier issued \RecBankExtras{}, \RecGlrExtras{} and \RecTreesExtras{}
unmatched alarms, respectively. We also tested successive interventions
separated by less than three seconds. Carrying the mean physical state
through restoration improved attribution over a reset on both test plants.
Retaining separate histories improved on the mean by
\CThreeFOneHistoryCarryDifferencePP{} percentage points on the plant
used to construct the predictor. With different generator dynamics,
the gain was \CThreePTwoHistoryCarryDifferencePP{} points (paired 95\%
interval $[\CThreePTwoHistoryCarryLowerPP,\CThreePTwoHistoryCarryUpperPP]$).
\end{abstract}
