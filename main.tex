\begin{abstract}
After a load is restored or a line is reclosed, the machines keep moving,
and the residual response can resemble a new intervention. We formulate
load/line attribution from eight PMUs in the IEEE 39-bus system as change
detection and isolation with switched differential-algebraic (DAE)
post-change models. A bank of linearized DAE predictors compares
intervention and recovery histories that start from estimated past
states, integrates load severity in closed form, and keeps a restored
intervention available as the cause of the continuing response. The same
operators give Willsky--Jones signatures, from which we compute, before
any experiment, each source's detection and isolation information, its
minimum detectable change versus noise, and the spurious evidence created
by forgetting the physical state at restoration. The analysis predicts
detection at the first post-onset report and reset evidence at least two
orders of magnitude above the alarm threshold. In an independent
simulation evaluation (18 physical parents, 108 interventions, 648 paired
replays) the bank attributes \COneMixTimely{}/\COneMixTotal{} interventions
before restoration with no alarm in 11.8\,h of normal records; resetting
the state lowers timely attribution from 108/108 to 75/108. \RevAbstractNoise{}
The single-model bank processes reports faster than the 30-report/s input.
\end{abstract}

change detection and isolation, differential-algebraic equations, event attribution, phasor measurement units, physics-informed inference, power system dynamics.
