\begin{abstract}
Recovering unmeasured bus voltages and identifying a disturbance source are different inverse problems. Sparse phasor measurement units (PMUs) may determine a requested voltage function even when two physical sources remain indistinguishable. We study that distinction with a hybrid differential-algebraic model in which physical interventions and measurement-integrity faults are separate variables. Functional observability determines whether the hidden-voltage target is estimable and gives its minimum-variance linear covariance. Event-source diagnosability is measured by the weighted distance between candidate-specific response manifolds after state and sparse-corruption nuisance directions are fitted. A nonlinear remainder bound states when the local separation survives DAE curvature. We test these quantities in two independent IEEE 39-bus studies. In PowerDynamics, the causal estimator reaches \BTwoTVE{} mean hidden-voltage error, and measurement-only physical recentering removes at least \EZeroHClosureHalf{} of the operating-point error gap. In a separate ANDES event bank, the learned hierarchy detects known-source events but localizes only \HeldSourceCorrect{} of \HeldSourceDecisions{} decisions after complete source holdout. Accurate state reconstruction therefore does not establish source recovery. The end-to-end candidate-conditioned estimator remains an experimentally testable model, not a validated result.
\end{abstract}
