\begin{abstract}
Recovering unmeasured bus voltages and identifying a disturbance source are different inverse problems. Sparse phasor measurement units (PMUs) may determine a requested voltage function even when two sources remain indistinguishable. We formulate both tasks with a hybrid differential-algebraic model that separates physical interventions from measurement-integrity faults. Functional observability gives the minimum-variance linear covariance of an estimable voltage target. Source diagnosability is the weighted distance between candidate-response manifolds after state and corruption nuisance directions are fitted, with a local curvature bound for nonlinear separation. Three frozen contracts test the resulting claims on IEEE 39-bus models. A causal PowerDynamics estimator reaches \BTwoTVE{} mean hidden-voltage error, and measurement-only physical recentering removes at least \EZeroHClosureHalf{} of the operating-point error gap. A 16-source load-intervention study obtains second-order local residual scaling and finds that event-visible information predicts the empirical 90\%-detection amplitude with Spearman $\rho=\LoadEVIRho{}$. Exact source Top-1 falls from 100\% for a 0.4\% load change to 13.0\% at 0.01\%, although the latter remains statistically detectable. In a separate ANDES bank, a supervised hierarchy localizes only \HeldSourceCorrect{} of \HeldSourceDecisions{} decisions after complete source holdout. The evidence links physical information geometry to state and source difficulty while exposing distinct limits for event visibility, source resolution, and learned transfer. A full multi-family joint estimator remains unvalidated.
\end{abstract}
