\begin{abstract}
Sparse phasor measurement units leave two different questions unresolved: whether the unmeasured voltage field can be reconstructed and whether the physical sources of an event can be distinguished. We separate those questions with a hybrid differential-algebraic model. Functional observability governs the requested voltage target; finite-horizon separation between candidate response manifolds governs event cardinality and source support. We test this distinction on the IEEE 39-bus system with eight voltage/current PMUs. A causal model-based estimator reconstructs the 31 unmeasured bus voltages with \BTwoTVE{} mean relative error under matched operation. A separate physical likelihood evaluates \GlobalHypothesisCount{} load-intervention hypotheses, comprising no event, every single source, and every two-source support. On \GlobalPhysicalTrajectories{} fresh nonlinear trajectories, exact-support Top-1 ranges from \GlobalWeakTopOne{} for weak pairs to \GlobalFiniteTopOne{} for finite pairs. The weak cases reveal the central limitation: posterior evidence can identify that two sources are active before it can identify which two. Profiled response distance explains this ambiguity better than support-only geometry, while longer observation increases separation without removing severe signature collinearity. The results support matched-model state reconstruction and simultaneous load-source inference. They do not establish a multi-family joint estimator, changing-PMU operation, unknown-onset inference, or field transfer.
\end{abstract}
