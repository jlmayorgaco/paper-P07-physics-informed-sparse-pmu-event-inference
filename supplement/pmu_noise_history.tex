\subsection{Startup Support and Covariance in the History Bank}
\label{app:pmu-history-noise}

To test the history bank itself, we selected two event and two quiet
parents by hash among the 24 archived stress parents. The same parents
appear on the reference plant (F01) and the second plant (P2).
We replayed their original and noisy recordings
at the primary bank's frozen alarm threshold. Each event record
contains six interventions. We monitored the first 120~s of each
record, including 12~s for startup. The wider-range rule raised the
upper admissible noise scale from 10 to 1000 and retained the six
estimated family scales. Each physical response bank was linearized
at the operating point inferred from the prefix it actually received.

The second rule retained the same physical response and onset
proposals but separated independent terminal error from the nominal
correlated background. Let $\Delta\phi_k$ be the first difference of
the eight voltage angles. Independent angular error with variance
vector $v$ contributes $-\operatorname{diag}(v)$ to its lag-one
covariance. With $P=I-\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}/8$, subtraction of
the nominal lag covariance and projection by $P$ remove the modeled
background and the common phase direction. We estimated $v\geq0$ from
300 startup reports by fitting the upper triangle of
$P\operatorname{diag}(v)P$ to the symmetrized projected difference
(nominal minus observed). A fixed ridge of 0.1 shrinks the eight
variances toward the nonnegative part of the mean diagonal target. We used these
voltage-based estimates for both measured phasors at each terminal,
as the stress generator gives voltage and current equal relative
error scales. This assumption would require calibration for actual
transducers.

After restoring nominal background scales, we propagated the added
polar errors through the instrument model. Twenty lagged angular
errors per PMU give the 21-report derivative its time correlation.
The state gain uses $T P_\rho J^\top+
\operatorname{Cov}(w_k,\epsilon_k)$ as its cross-covariance.
The updated gain and innovation covariance enter each history's
likelihood. The electrical response operators and alarm threshold
are shared by the two admitted rules.

Under added noise, all three extra correct diagnoses on the reference
plant were loads. On the second plant, modeling the added noise cost
three previously correct line diagnoses in aggregate and yielded
eight more unmatched event-record alarms. Even on its nominal
recordings the second plant lost timely diagnoses when the covariance
rule changed. This rules out startup rejection as the sole explanation
for the poor transfer. The paired design uses the same two event
schedules and two quiet recordings per plant. With zero quiet alarms
in 0.06~h the one-sided 95\% Poisson upper bound is 49.9 alarms/h.
