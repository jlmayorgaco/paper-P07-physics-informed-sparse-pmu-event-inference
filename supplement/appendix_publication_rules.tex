\subsection{Source Qualification and Public Episode Rules}
\label{app:publication-rules}

The source screen and the onset search are distinct. Source qualification
uses a 30-report (one-second) conditional innovation vector $v$ with 47
coordinates per report. Fixed normal-mode gains propagate the stored
pre-window state and whiten the suffix. For each prepared response $s_j$
and lag from 0 to 25 reports, let
\begin{align}
 g_j&=s_j^\top s_j,\qquad u_j=s_j^\top v,\qquad R=v^\top v,\nonumber\\
 \widehat a_j&=\operatorname{clip}(u_j/\max(g_j,10^{-30}),-0.2,0.2),\nonumber\\
 G_j&=2\widehat a_j u_j-\widehat a_j^2g_j,\nonumber\\
 F_j&=\frac{\max(G_j,0)}{\max\{(R-G_j)/1409,1\}}.
 \label{eq:supp-source-screen}
\end{align}
Line columns use fixed amplitude one. The source score is the maximum
over its columns; the threshold is the $1-0.001/936$ quantile of an
$F_{1,1409}$ distribution, approximately 24.0115. This threshold and the
variance floor are a fixed proposal/publication rule, not a calibrated
global false-alarm probability. The statistic is never multiplied into
the Bayesian history likelihood. This one-second qualification screen
does not enlarge the separate birth search beyond its 0--18 report lags.

Define $b_c=1$ for a line, and $b_c=\mathbf1\{F_c>24.0115\}$ for a
load. At each monitored report, the publisher updates consecutive-report
counters for $M_cb_c\ge0.99$, $\Gamma_c\ge0.99$, and
$\Gamma_cP(\mathrm{OFF}\mid c)\ge0.99$. A false condition clears its
counter. An internal alarm opens a pending episode. A public alarm then
requires either six pending reports with $\sum_c M_cb_c\ge0.99$ or an
already accumulated six-report line-active counter. The latter permits
line evidence collected before the internal pending message to count.

A public episode receives one diagnosis when its highest cause-mass cell
has six consecutive reports above 0.99, or at the 15-report deadline after
the public alarm. The output family maximizes summed cause mass; sources
are ranked within that family. The diagnosed source becomes the episode
owner. Six reports of that owner's joint cause-and-OFF mass above 0.99
emit a public restoration. These publication operations do not rescale
$M$ or reset the physical state.

The internal mixture lifecycle separately replaces an episode after six
reports with mass at least 0.99 on active histories whose onset is later
than its alarm (using the implementation's quarter-report timing guard).
It withdraws an unconfirmed episode after the diagnosis delay when null
mass has exceeded 0.99 for 15 reports. Its internal restoration requires
cause mass at least 0.99 and conditional retired mass at least 0.99 for
15 reports. These terminal messages are propagated to the public episode
if already emitted; an unconfirmed pending episode is recorded as
rejected. Finalization marks any remaining public episode right-censored.
The nominal comparator retains its own fixed internal lifecycle, so the
nominal/mixture comparison is not an isolated model-averaging ablation.
