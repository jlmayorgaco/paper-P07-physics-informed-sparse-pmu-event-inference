\subsection{Nominal-Operator Mechanism Diagnostics}
Two independent diagnostics use the fixed nominal IEEE-39 load operators
with nine load sources and 47 observation coordinates. They do not use
observations from the mechanism/transfer campaign. The code, noise seeds,
threshold grid and endpoints were fixed before generating the cohorts.
The deterministic null-innovation recursion reproduces explicit physical
state and null-filter propagation through ON and OFF to maximum absolute
error $2.8\times10^{-10}$. Noise is generated in innovation coordinates;
neither a nonlinear second plant nor independent per-PMU TVE is simulated.

\paragraph{Inherited uncertainty at a fixed recovery mean.}
The first diagnostic holds the onset, current-event amplitude and recovery
mean known. Over 15 reports, let $\mu_c$ be the nominal signature of source
$c$ and let $u$ be the preceding load's recovery signature scaled by its
severity standard deviation, 0.002. In whitened coordinates the fixture is
\begin{equation}
 y=\mu_c+u\zeta+\epsilon,\qquad
 \zeta\sim\mathcal N(0,1),\quad\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I),
 \label{eq:cov-fixture}
\end{equation}
with independent $\zeta,\epsilon$ and dimension $15\times47=705$.
The preceding load is restored 0.5\,s before the observation window.
The two source selectors use the same means and respectively covariance
$I$ or $I+uu^\top$. The latter evaluates
\begin{align}
 d_c&=\|y-\mu_c\|^2-
 \frac{[u^\top(y-\mu_c)]^2}{1+u^\top u},\nonumber\\
 \log\det(I+uu^\top)&=\log(1+u^\top u).
\end{align}
The covariance is common across candidate sources for each draw, so its
log determinant cancels in the source ranking, but remains in the density.
Across 360 independent noise/severity draws, correct source identification
increased from \CovMeanHits{}/360 to \CovCarryHits{}/360:
$+\CovDelta{}$ percentage points, paired 95\% bootstrap interval
$[\CovLow{},\CovHigh{}]$ from 10,000 resamples of the draws.
Coverage of the nominal 95\% true-source innovation ellipsoid increased
from \CovNisMean{}\% to \CovNisCarry{}\%.
This demonstrates the cost of discarding a known covariance component under
the declared Gaussian model. It neither compares explicit histories with
an equivalent fused distribution nor establishes detector performance with
unknown onset and amplitude.

\paragraph{Causal noise learning with an unchanged observation center.}
The second diagnostic adds covariance $\alpha_k\operatorname{diag}(S_0)$
to nominal innovation covariance $S_0$. Normal records contain stationary,
step-changing or ramping noise. Each event record contains two nonoverlapping
loads, active at 30--70\,s and 80--90\,s, with magnitudes 0.0005 or 0.002
and both signs. A per-report source/age mixture compares nine sources and
eight fixed ages with a Gaussian severity prior of standard deviation 0.01.
It is a local likelihood scan, not a temporal history posterior, and uses
an untruncated severity prior. No event-type or restoration estimator is run.

The arms hold $\alpha=0$, learn continuously after startup, or learn only
after 15 quiet reports and a 60-report cooldown. Quiet means nominal
posterior mass at least 0.99 under normal prior mass 0.99. The noise moment
estimate uses a learning rate $1/900$ and projects $\alpha$ onto $[0,8]$.
Each report is scored before learning; the current report can affect the
guard only for later reports. All arms share the same likelihood scan,
six-report publication confirmation and five-second refractory interval.
The lowest feasible threshold on the fixed grid is selected using only
36 normal calibration records, each 600\,s with 12\,s startup. Thresholds
are fixed before generating 36 new normal and 36 event TEST records.
Publication does not modify inference in this diagnostic.

The deterministic prefix check passes for all inference arrays. An
independent recount verifies the saved prediction streams, calibration
selection, event matches, normal exposure and saved-file integrity. These
diagnostics support preserving inherited uncertainty and revisiting the
adaptation guard on new development data. They do not support adding an
adaptive-performance claim to the article abstract.
