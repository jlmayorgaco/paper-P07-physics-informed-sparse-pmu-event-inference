# Prospective clock and angular-noise stress test

Compare the two estimators frozen before the preceding relative-phase trial. Keep weights, 63.456685 angular threshold, priors, clearance timestamp, delay and rejection rule fixed. Each trace contains the same eight voltage and current PMU pairs at 30 reports/s in a 60 Hz IEEE-39 system. All conditions retain the original measurement noise. The two arms use identical observations and can alarm only once per recovery episode. The angular arm consumes the corrupted phasors directly.

## Independent trials and blinding

Use 480 new catalog event/control pairs (120 per ordered combination of first and second line/load family), and a separate panel of 120 new bus-31 load/control pairs, balanced by first family. Generate with the existing nonlinear P2 network, asset catalog, event-size and dwell distributions and independent preregistered seeds in stress_plan.py. The targeted panel excludes bus 31 as first event. Each twin has the identical operating point, first event, underlying PMU noise and new device-error realization as its positive. Reuse the same realization and physical records at every stress level. The inference functions see one PMU stream and the first clearance only. Archive every condition's anonymous calls before opening labels. Do not pool the enriched panel with the catalog for overall rates.

## Error processes

At 60 Hz add 0.0216 degrees per microsecond to both V and I on each affected device. This is an output phasor reference error model. It preserves report arrival order and envelope sampling times and represents the carrier-phase effect on the measurements. Packet loss, frame slips and re-digitization of instantaneous switches are outside this comparison. All additions start at the raw phase channels. Fixed per-unit offsets can cancel in the initial phase reference, while a varying timing error shared by the local V/I pair can survive in voltage differences across PMUs.

* Independent device/channel angle noise has a bounded uniform marginal with the specified standard deviation (degrees) and a Gaussian copula with 0.10 s correlation time before the marginal transform. Clock jitter has independent zero-mean Gaussian processes across devices, 0.50 s correlation time, with the specified per-device RMS (microseconds). A pairwise timing difference has √2 times that RMS. Joint settings combine both mechanisms. Each listed RMS is additional to native noise.
* Persistent offsets are drawn independently within ±1 or ±26.5 μs per device. Holdover starts at first clearance on one uniformly selected PMU with a random sign, runs for eight seconds at the listed μs/s, then holds the final offset. A separate step experiment changes one random device by the listed signed amplitude at a uniformly selected report 0.5–6 s after clearance, independently of the second event. Levels, device and signs are drawn without using observations or labels.
* The grid includes the 0.005–0.009° spread measured by Salls et al. in two devices under calibrated laboratory stimuli. The distribution and temporal correlation used here are experimental assumptions. NIST measured tens of nanoseconds in one synchronized testbed. NASPI gives an example of a crystal accumulating 1 ms in four hours (mean rate about 0.069 μs/s). These observations anchor scales; their quoted quantities do not estimate the jitter distribution of this simulated fleet. ## Endpoints and operating limits

Detection requires the first alarm at or after onset and strictly before clearance. Timely correct family and exact asset additionally require the decision 29 reports later before clearance. Count every scheduled second event. Report recovery-only controls with any alarm and positive records pre-empted by early alarms separately. Primary joint pass requires **more than 95%** in each catalog efficacy metric (≥457/480), **more than 95%** timely detection on the independent bus-31 panel (≥115/120), less than 5% catalog controls alarmed and no more catalog controls alarmed than the frozen magnitude comparator. Also report targeted-control alarms, the angular arm's nominal false alarm count, and the number of newly alarmed versus relieved controls. Evaluate 36 archived long quiet P2 records with fresh stress draws for an additional 5.861 eligible hours per condition. Report the subset of primary passing levels with no excess long-quiet alarms separately. These records have been used previously under native noise.

For each ordered stress axis report the contiguous passing levels from nominal and the first failing tested level. Report nonmonotone outcomes explicitly. If nominal already fails, the sweep establishes no contiguous passing range under that criterion. These are observed discrete operating points for this case mix. Repeated levels on the same parents do not increase the independent sample size. Report marginal binomial bounds and a paired description of newly introduced false alarms; point equality alone cannot establish population non-inferiority. No threshold adaptation, selection of a new random cohort or replacement of missing trials is permitted in this comparison.
