## Development decision

The physical bank compares a continuing recovery with a second intervention using voltage and branch-current magnitudes, same-instrument V–I phase and inter-PMU voltage phase. Synthetic network responses to each candidate intervention determine the mean signal. Quiet and first-event-only data determine the covariance. For a whitened innovation x and an onset, amplitude and asset template d, the Gaussian log likelihood ratio is dᵀx − ||d||²/2. Log-sum-exp marginalizes over the discretized nuisance parameters. The estimator publishes a family and site 29 reports after its first threshold crossing.

Development compares four physical background/channel choices, a measurement-quality selector, and an optional BUS31 likelihood correction. The selector fits two Gaussian densities to log median absolute inter-PMU angular increments in the first quiet second. It uses the nominal full-angle model when the nominal density is higher. Under degraded measurements it uses magnitudes and local V–I angles, which cancel a common rotation at each instrument. The weak-source correction trains on previously disclosed event/control pairs. Its inputs are causal BUS31 log likelihood ratios, local voltage-phase changes and measured recovery size. Its binary gradient-boosted classifier estimates a discriminative score for this alternative. Its control threshold is estimated separately. The earliest accepted alternative opens the alarm. The native model marginalizes amplitude and age on its finite dictionary. The complete estimator includes empirical calibration and supplies no global optimality guarantee.

Physical noise and covariance fitting uses 256 older recoveries. Thresholds use 320 independent null recoveries, each exposed to every development condition. The labeled selection cohort contains 240 earlier event/control pairs. Among candidates giving at most one alarm per 240 controls in every development condition, maximize worst-condition exact locations, then worst-condition BUS31 detections, then total control specificity and aggregate exact hits. Where no candidate meets the cap, minimize the excess before maximizing efficacy. Every candidate score is disclosed. MiniRocket uses its own independently calibrated detector and source classifier. ## Prospective test

Generate 240 general-catalog positive/control twins with 60 in each ordered combination of first and second family. Independently generate 60 more BUS31 twins. The two members share first event, first clearance, operating point, measurement errors and reported noise. Both are identical before the randomly timed second switch. Use seeds 64971191 and 82036117. Each trace supplies six original simulator outputs. The primary scan ends nine seconds after first clearance. Restrict decisions to the first alarm. A timely alarm falls within the second event, and both family and exact-site success also require publication before its clearance. Every positive enters each efficacy denominator. Report control alarm probability and premature alarms separately. For macro-F1 score E0, E2 and E4. A missed or untimely positive is a missed event. An alarm on a control always counts against specificity. The target is at least 95 percent on all three event endpoints with no increase in control alarms. The trial also reports the 0.96 point targets separately.

The four repeated-measure conditions are native report noise, native plus 0.005° angular RMS and 0.25 µs jitter, native plus 0.008° and 0.5 µs, and 0.005° with 0.1 µs jitter plus 0.07 µs/s drift and a 1 µs step on one PMU. Voltage and current on a device share the clock perturbation. Each condition uses exactly the same underlying trajectories. The perturbations act on reported phasor angles. Frequency and ROCOF retain their simulator values. No claim is made about waveform-level timing faults or regions between these four settings.
