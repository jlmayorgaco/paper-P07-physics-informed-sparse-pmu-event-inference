# Second switches during recovery in IEEE-39

The primary comparison is the frozen fixed-lag modal method and a replacement that estimates the electrical response to the first restoration from its first 30 post-restoration PMU reports. Both receive the same first restoration report and the same eight voltage magnitudes and eight metered terminal-current magnitudes at 30 reports/s. Each outputs one alarm and, 29 reports later, one family and one source. The first switch's identity, duration, and twin are unavailable to either estimator. The simulation guarantees an initially quiet second and a minimum 1.25 s gap between restoration and the second onset. Both methods require 60 post-restoration samples for the first alarm.

## Adjustment

The intact-network hypothesis predicts a continuing recovery. The recovery library is indexed by the first asset and dwell time. For each library curve, five within-prefix contrasts of successive five-report means provide a Gaussian likelihood. The prior gives equal mass to load recovery, line recovery, and quiet. For loads, the signed amplitude has a zero-mean normal prior. Its posterior mean and the posterior model weights predict the continuing recovery. Subtract this prediction before testing for a second switch. We compared two references for the remaining voltage and current levels, the post-clearance residual mean and the mean of the first 30 quiet PMU reports.

For each candidate second asset, onset age and load amplitude, let d be its simulated shift in the vector of PMU contrasts and mean levels. The detector evaluates the shifted Gaussian log likelihood against the continuing recovery, dᵀΣ⁻¹r − dᵀΣ⁻¹d/2, for centered residual features r. It sums the likelihoods with uniform family, asset and onset-age priors, integrating positive and negative load sizes by quadrature. The covariance and centering come from 256 single-event recoveries. Trigger at the first score strictly above threshold. At the common publication delay a longer window repeats the likelihood calculation, marginalizing over candidate times and sizes, selecting the family by posterior mass and then the most probable asset within it. The Gaussian residual and isolated incremental signature are model assumptions.

The retained alarm window contains 60 reports in four-report bins with onset ages 5, 12, 20, 32 and 42 reports. The diagnostic window contains 84 reports with onset ages 29 through 71 inclusive. Candidates were explored on two previously disclosed cohorts (192 event episodes and their 192 controls); their scores are development data. Model selection compares the two references on these cohorts, maximizing timely exact location, then family, then timely detection, with fewer control alarms and the 0.99 calibration level breaking ties. Only candidates below 5% development control alarms qualify. Candidate thresholds are the higher of the maximum over 36 archived long quiet F01 calibration traces and the 0.975, 0.99 or 0.995 order statistic of maxima in 320 independent single-event calibration records, rank ceil(q × 321). The upstream method retains its original weights and threshold.

## Prospective test and endpoints

After the freeze, generate 240 new independent parent simulations at the established P2 operating point, seed 9316879. Each of the four ordered combinations of first and second family (load or line) has 60 parents. Each parent supplies a case with a second event and a control with the identical first event and noise but no second event. The two assets within a parent differ. This tests new draws from the same nine-load, nine-line catalog. Test asset identities and the operating point were represented in the physical library.

Publish every prediction and its checksum before opening the target file. Use integer 60 Hz ticks and score one attempt per record. Detection is a first alarm in the half-open second-event interval. Family and exact asset both require timely detection and publication before clearance, on the denominator of all 240 second events. An early first alarm forfeits the case. A false positive is any alarm on a recovery-only control, divided by 240. Also report early alarms on positive records separately. The prespecified target requires each efficacy count to be at least 228 and at most 11 control alarms. Report one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson bounds as a separate binomial precision summary, conditional on this benchmark case mix; paired exact discordance tests are descriptive. A third arm retains the original readout behind the adjusted gate.

For continuity, replay the same 36 archived ten-minute P2 quiet records used in the previous study, counting upward-crossing episodes and eligible hours for both methods. These quiet data were previously published. Novel blinded outcomes concern the 240 matched pairs.

## Reproduction

Use the upstream simulator and archive recorded in `../results/freeze.json`. In a clean scratch directory of the same checkout run the following scripts in order from this directory. Output creation is exclusive to avoid replacing frozen trials. The development step requires the two previous public test cohorts in the upstream scratch directories.

1. Run `python cohort.py fit` and `python cohort.py cal`, followed by `python templates.py` and `python recovery.py`.
2. Run `python condition.py`, `python profile_gate.py`, `python quiet.py short` and `python reference.py`. The optional `develop.py` runs an exploratory unconditioned comparison.
3. Freeze with `python run.py freeze`. Generate the new pairs with `python cohort.py test`, then run `python run.py predict`. Archive the predictions before `python run.py score`.
4. With the committed outputs, `python run.py verify` checks the freeze and `python run.py score` independently recounts the published calls and labels.
