# Second-event family, prospective paired experiment

Compare the frozen single-network history monitor of the manuscript (C03 history, threshold log(999)) and an independent adaptation of Li and Wang's prediction-subtraction CNN-F. The published CNN requires a detection time. Use the identical causal history-bank ON alarms for all diagnostic arms. Freeze all methods before generating the new two-event schedules and seal all predictions before matching. The study measures family discrimination under a shared detector, not independent detection or localization by CNN-F.

* Use 36 new paired-intervention trajectories on the manuscript's classical-machine second plant. Exactly nine each of load→load, load→line, line→load and line→line. Draw different consecutive assets, magnitudes 3–14% with both signs for load, pulse durations 2–5 s, gap 0.5–3 s after first clearance, first onset 15–19 s, 40 s per record. Draw on the half-report grid. Use the 36 archived quiet TEST startup prefixes for this plant, one per record, to reuse its frozen electrical initialization. Preserve the instrument noise state at 12 s and draw fresh subsequent innovations. Both methods get the same signals and no true switch times. Use the original normal CAL and TEST streams as fixed alarm controls, distinct from training. Validate the startup fingerprint and the archived control prediction seals. Reusing the startup and normal controls does not create new quiet exposure.
* Train CNN-F on separate isolated events from the second plant (20 per catalog asset, six more per asset for validation), including a random 0–12 report detection lag. Fit normalization on training only. RMSprop and validation early stopping, three prespecified initializations. Both family classifiers use the same 30 reports beginning at each shared alarm. The original published family is retained as a separate arm. Compare its early decision at its native clock as a sensitivity. For CNN-F use eight voltage magnitudes, eight frequencies and seven active-power proxies from the same available terminal angles. Prediction uses DMD on the PMUs from the previous alarm through two reports before the current one; no true first onset, clearance or family. Center the signals using the quiet startup. Use the paper's rank ratio 0.999, reconstruction check 0.5 and slow-mode cutoff 1 Hz. Score the same trained CNN with subtraction switched off. Ages, PMU instrumentation and two output classes adapt the original IEEE-68 experiment.
* The proposed fixed-lag estimator reads the retained DAE histories at the last report in that same window. Sum the weights of histories whose onset follows the preceding alarm, starts no later than the alarm in question and whose intervention is active at that alarm. Sum by family, choose the higher mass and abstain on zero support. Under equal family error costs this minimizes conditional family error under the retained local model. A common-gate control merges recovered histories at each restoration announced by the original clock. Its paired twin also resets only the physical part of the merged mean. Both controls use the same fixed-lag readout and receive exactly the same restoration and classification times.
* Primary endpoint is the fraction of all 36 second events whose family is correctly published strictly before restoration. Match each true onset chronologically to the first unused ON alarm before the next onset (30 s cap), using neither predicted family nor site. All misses, abstentions and late labels are failures. Report family-specific denominators, differences with 95% bootstrap intervals paired by record and stratified by the four ordered pairs (10,000 draws), and two-sided exact paired sign tests for discordances. The calibration budget is the original one-sided 95% Poisson upper bound ≤1 normal alarm per hour. Count every unmatched gate alarm, even if a classifier abstains. Electrical model, source catalogue, clock, preprocessing and trained weights are fixed for testing.
